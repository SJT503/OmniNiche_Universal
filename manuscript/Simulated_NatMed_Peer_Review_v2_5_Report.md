### Role 1: Editor-in-Chief (EIC, *Nature Medicine*)

#### Overall Assessment
This Stage II v2.5 manuscript addresses a central translational bottleneck in drug discovery and clinical medicine: why drug candidates supported by human genetics and healthy single-cell atlases still suffer >85% late-stage clinical attrition. Relative to typical computational target-prioritization papers that benchmark exclusively on retrospective knowledge graphs, OmniNiche offers an ambitious four-layer evidentiary architecture spanning 41,396 Phase II–IV ClinicalTrials.gov records (with a manually adjudicated clause-aware stoppage parser, $\kappa = 0.9413$), dynamic target-specificity drift across >530,000 primary human single cells, zero-label stochastic Langevin prediction of 24 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials across liver, lung, and kidney (`AUROC = 0.8714`), and 10x Visium-informed stratification of 107 strictly pre-treatment anti-PD-1 tumor biopsies (`AUROC = 0.6367`; single-cell `AUROC = 0.8750`) plus monotherapy overall survival (`HR = 1.95`, cohort-stratified `HR = 1.91`, $P = 0.0340$). The authors have also commendably avoided overclaiming bulk Langevin survival separation (`HR = 1.13`, $P = 0.68$) and explicitly positioned the framework as a mechanistic hypothesis-generating instrument rather than a standalone clinical withholding tool.

Despite these major strengths and high conceptual fit for *Nature Medicine*, our technical referees identified three substantive evidentiary and reporting issues that must be resolved before publication can be considered: (1) transparent attribution of how much `DNAcc` predictive power actually comes from longitudinal progression drift ($\Delta\tau$) and steric barrier compatibility ($B_{\mathrm{acc}}$) beyond early-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$, `M3`), alongside undocumented consensus drift imputation across the six disease axes lacking single-cell progression cohorts; (2) full, unselective reporting of all benchmarked baselines in `Table 2` and `Fig. 5d` (specifically the `Uninformative Linear Mean` baseline on pooled pre-treatment biopsies and `OmniNiche (No-Visium)` on BCC scRNA-seq) and moderation of unsupported statistical claims in the $n = 15$ paired longitudinal biopsy analysis; and (3) explicit reconciliation of high rank discrimination (`AUROC = 0.8714`) versus poor absolute probability calibration at the `0.50` cutoff (`5/10` clinical winners scoring `0.05–0.23`) in the 24-trial antifibrotic benchmark. None of these require wet-lab generation, as all underlying empirical data are already in hand, but they require rigorous analytical transparency and claim calibration.
