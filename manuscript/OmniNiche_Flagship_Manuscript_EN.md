## Abstract

Human genetics and healthy cell atlases guide target selection, yet over 85% of clinical drugs fail because diseased tissues remodel target expression and erect physical barriers against macromolecules. To model this missing tissue dimension, we developed OmniNiche, uniting outcome deconvolution of 41,396 Phase II-IV trials across 12 organ axes with single-cell and spatial biophysics across 20 cohorts. Stripping operational closures (51.4% of stoppages) via clause-aware deconvolution ($\kappa = 0.941$) uncovers 1.57-fold higher efficacy futility for ~150-kDa antibodies than small molecules. Capturing lineage drift and matrix exclusion within Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) outperforms healthy specificity (AUROC 0.671 versus 0.539), generalizes to unseen targets, and complements human genetics (0.702 versus 0.636). Extending this principle to zero-label Langevin dynamics compiled solely from primary biopsies ranks 24 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials across organs (AUROC 0.871), mirrors fibroblast drug reversal, stratifies 107 pre-treatment anti-PD-1 biopsies, and isolates a 3.60-fold immunotherapy-specific melanoma survival hazard. Dynamic tissue accessibility thus provides a biophysical counterpart to human genetics for matching modalities and barrier-breaking combinations.

## Introduction

Nearly 90% of investigational drugs entering clinical testing fail to achieve regulatory approval, with Phase IIb and Phase III efficacy failures driving the majority of late-stage patient exposure and capital loss [1, 2]. Human genetic evidence has become the foundational computational filter for target discovery, approximately doubling clinical approval probabilities when causal variant-to-gene support links a target to its intended indication [3, 4]. Knowledge-graph platforms such as Open Targets and biomedical foundation models such as TxGNN have scaled genetic and repurposing prioritization across thousands of diseases [5, 6]. Drug-discovery teams simultaneously consult healthy human single-cell atlases, including the Human Protein Atlas, to prioritize genes with high baseline cell-type specificity ($\tau_0$) and minimize vital-organ toxicity [7, 8]. Yet pivotal efficacy failures persist even when targets possess strong genetic plausibility and healthy-organ selectivity, spanning chemokine receptor antagonists in steatohepatitis, matrix-crosslinking antibodies in organ fibrosis, and checkpoint antibodies in immune-excluded solid carcinomas.

This translational gap arises because germline knowledge graphs and healthy single-cell atlases encode stationary, modality-agnostic gene-disease associations rather than stage-resolved lesion architecture, leaving them blind to whether a genetically valid target remains physically reachable inside remodeled human tissue. We tested the hypothesis that clinical realization of genetic target validity is gated by two dynamic tissue barriers during human pathology. First, chronic parenchymal injury, mucosal ulceration, and malignant growth reorganize cellular niches through profibrotic macrophage and myofibroblast expansion [9, 10], causing healthy-enriched targets to erode within intended compartments ($\Delta\tau < 0$) or switch ectopically into bystander stroma. Second, densely crosslinked extracellular matrix (ECM) scars and peritumoral cancer-associated fibroblast (`myCAF`) capsules erect physical steric barriers that impede ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies while permitting diffusion of small molecules and compact peptides [11, 12].

Testing whether dynamic lineage drift and macromolecular steric exclusion govern clinical attrition across human organs first requires deconfounding public clinical trial registries. In ClinicalTrials.gov, studies halted for corporate portfolio reprioritization, sponsor mergers, pandemic disruption, or slow accrual share the same terminated or withdrawn flags as studies halted for interim efficacy futility or organ toxicity [13, 14]. Multi-clause narratives routinely embed negated safety disclaimers (for example, closing for strategic reasons with no safety concerns), causing naive keyword matchers to misclassify operational closures as biological failures and mask genuine modality-by-matrix attrition laws.

To bridge germline target validity and diseased-tissue biophysics, we developed OmniNiche, a multi-scale computational framework that models dynamic tissue accessibility from clinical registries down to spatial cellular niches. At the macro-registry scale, OmniNiche strips operational trial closures to formulate `DNAcc`, a parsimonious white-box law coupling single-cell lineage drift with macromolecular steric compatibility across unseen drug targets; at the meso-tissue scale, it compiles primary human single-cell and spatial biopsies directly into zero-label stochastic Itô-Langevin dynamics without training on clinical response labels. We evaluated OmniNiche across 41,396 Phase II-IV trials spanning 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes alongside 237 primary clinical specimens (>530,000 single cells, 99 strictly pre-treatment bulk tumor biopsies, and 20,880 quality-filtered 10x Visium spots of 21,796 profiled), 73 human fibroblast perturbation RNA-seq samples, and 264 non-immunotherapy melanoma survival controls. Across these scales, OmniNiche delivers predictive power orthogonal to Open Targets human genetics, ranks 24 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials across liver, lung, and kidney, stratifies 107 pre-treatment anti-PD-1 tumor biopsies, and isolates immunotherapy-specific stromal survival hazard across 338 melanoma patients.

## Results

### Clause-aware deconvolution of 41,396 clinical trials exposes administrative noise and macromolecular futility

Testing whether tissue barriers shape clinical drug attrition first requires separating biological failures from commercial or operational trial closures across peripheral organs governed by interstitial matrix remodeling. We therefore queried interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV trials from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes [14] (Fig. 1a–d, Extended Data Table 1, and Supplementary Table 1). Deduplicating 43,878 raw axis hits to 41,983 unique NCT studies and excluding 587 primary central nervous system or brain-metastasis trials yielded 41,396 unique clinical trials (23,536 Phase II, 11,537 Phase III, and 6,323 Phase IV studies). Cohort volume was led by solid oncology ($n = 15,717$), metabolic and endocrine disease ($n = 4,915$), hematologic oncology ($n = 3,695$), pulmonary fibrosis and acute lung injury ($n = 3,525$), cardiovascular disease ($n = 3,381$), and autoimmune or mucosal inflammation ($n = 2,529$) (Fig. 2a,b and Extended Data Fig. 1a). Mapping drug interventions against ChEMBL v33 mechanisms and Open Targets v24.09 drug-target annotations [5, 15] resolved 29,791 trials (72.0%) to 1,155 unique human target genes (586 unique primary targets) (Extended Data Fig. 1b). These target-resolved studies spanned small-molecule regimens ($n = 18,128$, comprising 17,963 typed small molecules and 165 untyped chemical entries), IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 7,308$), and non-antibody biologics ($n = 4,355$, comprising 4,021 protein or peptide regimens and 334 cell, gene, or oligonucleotide therapies).

To resolve registry label confounding without circular heuristics [13], we built a unified clause-aware stoppage parser that segments multi-clause `whyStopped` narratives across punctuation and contrastive conjunctions and strips negated safety or efficacy disclaimers before classification (Extended Data Fig. 1c and Supplementary Table 2). We benchmarked this parser against a Naive Keyword Baseline on an independent 240-trial clinical adjudication reference (`Manual_Clinical_Adjudication_240`: 64 futility, 54 safety, 96 administrative, and 26 unreported stops) annotated directly from verbatim registry narratives. The clause-aware auditor achieved 95.83% accuracy (95% Wilson confidence interval (CI) 92.50%–97.72%) and Cohen's $\kappa = 0.9413$ (F1 = 0.9600 for efficacy futility, 0.9815 for safety, 0.9634 for administrative halts, and 0.8929 for unreported stops), substantially outperforming the Naive Keyword Baseline (70.00% accuracy, 95% CI 63.92%–75.45%, $\kappa = 0.6105$, administrative recall 43.75%) (Fig. 2f).

Applying this validated parser uniformly across all 9,406 stopped trials (31,990 completed) showed that administrative, commercial, and accrual halts represent 51.4% ($n = 4,838$) of all trial stoppages, outnumbering explicit biological failures ($n = 793$, comprising 476 efficacy futility and 317 safety/toxicity stops; $n = 3,775$ unreported or non-specific stops) by 6.1-fold (Fig. 2a). From 2005 to 2025, administrative halts rose from 44.1% of stopped trials in 2005 to 64.0% ($1,296 / 2,024$) pooled across 2020–2025 (peaking at 66.3% in 2021), while monoclonal antibody share among target-resolved trials doubled from 19.5% to 40.1% in 2021 (Fig. 2c). Enrollment distributions corroborated biological separation: administrative halts closed early at a median of 16 patients (0.29 million cumulative patients) and safety halts at 24 patients (48,497 patients), whereas efficacy futility trials reached interim or primary readouts at a median of 78 patients (0.12 million patients) alongside completed trials at 84 patients (9.77 million patients) (Fig. 2e).

Cross-tabulating therapeutic modality against deconvoluted stoppage cause revealed a selective macromolecular efficacy penalty predicted by interstitial transport biophysics [12] (Fig. 2d). Compared with small molecules ($n = 17,963$), ~150-kDa IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 7,308$) exhibited 1.57-fold higher odds of explicit biological efficacy futility (odds ratio (OR) = 1.57, exact two-sided Fisher's 95% CI 1.24–1.99, $P = 2.26 \times 10^{-4}$) without significant excess safety termination (OR = 1.12, 95% CI 0.84–1.50, $P = 0.45$). Compact protein and peptide therapeutics (~2–15 kDa; $n = 4,021$) incurred significantly lower efficacy futility odds than small molecules (OR = 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.90, $P = 0.0139$). Filtering administrative ($n = 3,412$) and unreported ($n = 2,618$) halts from the 29,791 target-resolved studies defined a 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 565 explicit biological failures [328 futility and 237 safety] across 374 primary targets) and a 23,761-trial Definitive Phase II-to-III/IV Maturation Cohort across 561 primary targets.

### Human single-cell disease progression destabilizes healthy target lineage specificity

While the 1.57-fold antibody futility penalty implicates interstitial transport barriers, small molecules also fail when pathology erodes their intended cellular targets, a dynamic invisible to healthy reference atlases. To measure how disease progression reshapes baseline target selectivity ($\tau_0$), we harmonized primary human transcriptomic cohorts across 237 clinical specimens (Extended Data Fig. 2a, Extended Data Table 1, and Supplementary Table 3). These cohorts encompassed >530,000 single cells across 126 single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-sequencing donor profiles, 99 strictly pre-treatment bulk RNA-seq tumor biopsies, and 12 primary carcinoma 10x Visium spatial sections (20,880 quality-filtered spots of 21,796 total profiled). Six core disease axes were directly resolved from primary single-cell progression biopsies: hepatic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and cirrhosis (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF, `GSE135893`), chronic kidney disease (CKD, `GSE271188`), solid carcinomas (`GSE123813` and `GSE207422`), mucosal ulcerative colitis (`GSE250487`), and bloodstream sepsis (`GSE167363`) [16-21]. Across 1,181 unique human target and pathway genes, we quantified healthy $\tau_0$ across 154 Human Protein Atlas cell types alongside early-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), advanced-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$), dynamic drift ($\Delta\tau = \tau_{\mathrm{late}} - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and ectopic dominant-lineage switching. In solid oncology, progression drift was computed strictly from response-label-free early primary tumors versus advanced resected carcinomas without accessing clinical immunotherapy response labels. Empirical interstitial ECM barrier density $D_{\mathrm{ECM}}(\mathrm{axis}) \in [0.070, 0.350]$ was derived objectively on a harmonized $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ 10-gene collagen-myofibroblast scale across all 12 axes, peaking in solid oncology (0.3500) and pulmonary fibrosis (0.3265) and reaching a minimum in bloodstream sepsis (0.0700; Supplementary Table 3).

Disease progression destabilized baseline target-lineage assignments across all six single-cell organ axes (Fig. 3a). Across all profiled targets, 17.8% in solid oncology (21.0% among expressed targets), 21.5% in pulmonary fibrosis (22.5%), 28.2% in ulcerative colitis (32.1%), 30.6% in hepatic MASH (31.3%), 31.1% in renal CKD (31.6%), and 37.1% in systemic sepsis (41.3%) switched their primary expressing cellular compartment between early and advanced human pathology (Extended Data Fig. 2b,c). Mapping clinical targets into the $(\tau_0, \Delta\tau)$ remodeling phase space separated late-stage clinical winners from failures along the vertical dynamic-drift dimension rather than the horizontal healthy-specificity axis (Fig. 3h). Targets that preserved or sharpened lineage selectivity in accessible parenchymal or vascular-mesenchymal niches ($\Delta\tau \ge 0$), including `THRB`, `PCSK9`, `ERBB2`, and `PDGFRB`, achieved high Phase III/IV maturation rates, whereas high-$\tau_0$ targets undergoing late-stage lineage erosion or ectopic stromal capture, such as `LOXL2` and `TGFB1`, repeatedly failed in pivotal trials.

### Dynamic Niche Accessibility complements human genetic evidence and generalizes across unseen targets

Because neither healthy specificity ($\tau_0$) nor molecular size alone captures both cellular remodeling and matrix transport, we unified these two tissue constraints into a six-step nested white-box logistic ablation (`M1` through `M6`) culminating in `DNAcc`, which integrates healthy $\tau_0$, vital-organ bystander hazard, early-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), remodeling drift ($\Delta\tau$), ectopic compartment switching, harmonized modality-by-ECM steric compatibility ($B_{\mathrm{acc}}$), and the biophysical $\tau_{\mathrm{early}} \times B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ interaction (Table 1 and Fig. 3b). On the 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort, static healthy $\tau_0$ (`M1`) performed near chance (stratified 5-fold AUROC = 0.5387, 95% CI 0.5152-0.5610). Sequentially adding vital-organ hazard (`M2`, 0.5618), early diseased-niche specificity (`M3`, 0.6614), progression drift and switching (`M4`-`M5`, 0.6589 and 0.6650), and steric barrier compatibility (`M6`) elevated discrimination to AUROC = 0.6710 (95% CI 0.6480-0.6932; $\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1323$ versus `M1`, two-sided paired DeLong $P = 1.11 \times 10^{-21}$) [22]. Pairwise step attribution showed that replacing healthy $\tau_0$ with diseased-lesion specificity (`M3`) captured 92.7% of the global scientific gain ($+0.1227$, $P = 7.38 \times 10^{-18}$), while progression drift and steric exclusion (`M6` versus `M3`) added finer incremental refinement ($\Delta = +0.0096$, $P = 0.205$ overall; $+0.0151$, $P = 0.0563$ under unseen-target `Group5Fold`; $+0.0394$, $P = 0.0353$ within single-target monotherapies).

Across the higher-powered 23,761-trial Definitive Maturation Cohort, out-of-fold discrimination rose monotonically at every ablation step (`0.5412 -> 0.5777 -> 0.6607 -> 0.6623 -> 0.6716 -> 0.6744`; $\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1332$ versus `M1`, DeLong $P = 4.12 \times 10^{-180}$; Brier error reduced from 0.2477 to 0.2273). Full `DNAcc` (`M6`) significantly exceeded `M3` under both stratified cross-validation ($\Delta = +0.0137$, $P = 8.93 \times 10^{-13}$) and unseen-target `Group5Fold` ($\Delta = +0.0076$, $P = 1.70 \times 10^{-4}$; single-target maturation $+0.0295$, $P = 1.19 \times 10^{-12}$). Within the six directly single-cell-resolved axes ($n = 5,662$ scientific trials across 293 targets, 386 biological failures), `DNAcc` achieved stratified AUROC = 0.6792 versus 0.5447 for static $\tau_0$ ($P = 2.11 \times 10^{-15}$; `M6` versus `M3` [0.6766] $\Delta = +0.0025$, $P = 0.696$) and unseen-target `Group5Fold` AUROC = 0.6415 versus 0.5049 ($P = 4.97 \times 10^{-15}$). Under Core-6 `Group5Fold`, the three-feature `M3` model (0.6575) exceeded nine-feature `M6` ($\Delta = -0.0160$, $P = 0.0188$), reflecting higher parameter-estimation variance when fitting six progression-barrier terms across 386 failure events without cross-axis consensus regularization (Extended Data Fig. 1e and Supplementary Table 3).

To test directly whether dynamic tissue accessibility adds orthogonal clinical value beyond human genetics [4], we benchmarked `DNAcc` head-to-head against Open Targets v24.09 target-disease human genetic association and somatic mutation evidence across all 11,637 scientific and 23,761 maturation trials (Table 1 and Fig. 3b). On the Deconfounded Scientific Cohort, human genetics alone achieved AUROC = 0.6362 (95% CI 0.6085-0.6625; unseen-target `Group5Fold` AUROC = 0.6175), whereas adding static healthy $\tau_0$ to genetics yielded zero gain (AUROC = 0.6344, $\Delta = -0.0018$, DeLong $P = 0.45$). `DNAcc` alone outperformed human genetics alone (AUROC = 0.6710 versus 0.6362, $\Delta = +0.0348$, DeLong $P = 0.0224$; `Group5Fold` 0.6541 versus 0.6175, $P = 0.0215$). Combining `Human Genetics + DNAcc` achieved strong additive synergy, reaching AUROC = 0.7023 (95% CI 0.6791-0.7244; $\Delta = +0.0661$ over genetics alone, DeLong $P = 1.58 \times 10^{-10}$) and unseen-target `Group5Fold` AUROC = 0.6844 versus 0.6175 ($P = 1.34 \times 10^{-9}$). Within already genetically supported targets (Open Targets genetic score $\ge 0.10$, $n = 7,174$ trials), `DNAcc` reached AUROC = 0.6916 versus 0.5267 for static $\tau_0$ ($P = 3.59 \times 10^{-25}$). Across the 23,761-trial Definitive Maturation Cohort, `Human Genetics + DNAcc` reached AUROC = 0.7210 (95% CI 0.7145-0.7273; DeLong $P = 1.72 \times 10^{-111}$ versus genetics alone [0.6631]; unseen-target `Group5Fold` 0.6998 versus 0.6413, $P = 3.86 \times 10^{-115}$).

Five orthogonal deconfounding audits ruled out target-identity shortcut learning [6] and confirmed that `DNAcc` is not a proxy for drug modality or polypharmacology. First, under target-disjoint `Group5Fold` cross-validation holding out entire target-gene families during training, `DNAcc` generalized across unseen targets in all 12 axes (AUROC = 0.6541 versus 0.5030 for $\tau_0$, $P = 3.91 \times 10^{-25}$) and in the six core single-cell axes (0.6415 versus 0.5049, $P = 4.97 \times 10^{-15}$) (Fig. 3b and Extended Data Fig. 1e). Second, `DNAcc` retained significant unseen-target `Group5Fold` discrimination within strictly single-target monotherapies on the scientific cohort ($n = 6,115$ trials across 235 targets; stratified AUROC = 0.6404, $P = 1.18 \times 10^{-5}$; unseen-target `Group5Fold` 0.5989 versus 0.4697, $P = 7.63 \times 10^{-5}$). Consistent generalization held across single-target maturation trials ($n = 10,507$ trials across 374 targets; `Group5Fold` 0.6451 versus 0.4310, $P = 3.81 \times 10^{-130}$) and multi-target regimens (stratified 0.6835, $P = 4.59 \times 10^{-13}$; `Group5Fold` 0.6645, $P = 2.63 \times 10^{-20}$) (Extended Data Fig. 1d). Third, `DNAcc` significantly outperformed static $\tau_0$ strictly within every major drug modality: small molecules ($n = 7,286$, 235 targets; AUROC = 0.6663 versus 0.5451, $P = 1.58 \times 10^{-14}$), IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 2,264$, 137 targets; 0.6690 versus 0.5959, $P = 0.0093$), and proteins or peptides ($n = 1,904$, 99 targets; 0.6190 versus 0.4870, $P = 0.0030$) (Fig. 3d).

Fourth, we fit multivariable fixed-effects logistic regressions controlling simultaneously for disease axis, drug modality, calendar era, sponsor class, and polypharmacology with Liang-Zeger cluster-robust sandwich inference across $G = 374$ to $561$ target clusters [23]. On the scientific cohort, `DNAcc` remained independently predictive after five-family adjustment (OR per 1-s.d. = 1.42, Wald 95% CI 1.28-1.59, $P = 1.69 \times 10^{-10}$; target-clustered 95% CI 1.21-1.68, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 2.81 \times 10^{-5}$). By contrast, Open Targets human genetic support attenuated from unadjusted OR = 1.70 ($P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 4.47 \times 10^{-17}$) to adjusted OR = 1.12 (target-clustered 95% CI 1.00-1.25, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.0477$). In the simultaneous three-pillar model adjusting for human genetics, static $\tau_0$, and all five confounder families, `DNAcc` retained robust independent effect (`DNAcc` OR = 1.42, target-clustered 95% CI 1.19-1.70, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1.04 \times 10^{-4}$). Conversely, static $\tau_0$ collapsed to unity (OR = 0.97, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.719$) and human genetics attenuated to OR = 1.06 (target-clustered 95% CI 0.95-1.18, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.328$), because the 11 disease-axis indicators absorb between-axis genetic and somatic score prevalence while `DNAcc` operates within individual axes and modalities (Fig. 3c and Extended Data Fig. 3b). Fifth, per-axis Definitive Maturation stratification confirmed significant gains across all five solid and mucosal axes (`OR = 1.25` to `2.12`), whereas intravascular PBMC sepsis ($n = 141$) showed expected null discrimination (AUROC = 0.3822 versus 0.3616, $P = 0.689$), establishing an explicit anatomical boundary condition (Table 1 and Extended Data Fig. 2d).

Across quartiles of the 23,761-trial maturation cohort, empirical Phase II-to-III/IV progression climbed 2.45-fold from 27.10% in Q1 to 66.28% in Q4 under `DNAcc`, versus 1.20-fold (44.96% to 53.87%) under static $\tau_0$ (Fig. 3e). Out-of-fold probabilities were well calibrated across deciles on both cohorts (Fig. 3f and Extended Data Fig. 3e) and yielded higher Decision Curve Analysis net benefit across Phase III gating thresholds ($p_t = 0.28$ to $0.65$) [24] (Fig. 3g). Seven sensitivity regimes (`R1`-`R7`) confirmed invariance across unfiltered naive-keyword outcomes (`R2`, $n = 11,843$, AUROC = 0.6751), worst-case assignment of all 2,618 target-resolved unreported stops as biological failures (`R3`, $n = 14,255$, AUROC = 0.6533, $P = 3.89 \times 10^{-70}$), inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting (`R4`, 0.6713), and posted-results-only trials (`R5`, $n = 5,609$, 0.6742). Equal stability held against regulatory-approved drug-indication pairs (`R6`, $n = 9,697$, AUROC = 0.6767 versus 0.5177, $P = 6.27 \times 10^{-28}$) and the strict nine-axis solid-parenchymal interstitial boundary (`R7`, $n = 10,701$, AUROC = 0.6884 versus 0.5304, $P = 1.21 \times 10^{-25}$) (Extended Data Fig. 3c). Gating out the bottom 25% of Phase II candidates by `DNAcc` lifted Phase III/IV maturation from 46.60% to 53.10%, preventing 4,326 failed trials (~1.19 million patient exposures at median 276 patients per Phase III trial) while retaining 85.51% of winners (Extended Data Fig. 3d).

### Zero-shot cross-organ human fibrosis transfer predicts 24 Phase IIb/III clinical trial outcomes

Registry-wide `DNAcc` scoring flags targets vulnerable to lineage drift and matrix exclusion across thousands of trials, yet predicting whether a specific regimen can reverse established organ scarring requires modeling nonlinear multicellular feedback across histological stages without relying on historical clinical outcome labels. Harmonizing 64 primary human single-cell and single-nucleus biopsies across liver (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`, $n = 17$), lung (`GSE135893`, $n = 30$), and kidney (`GSE271188`, $n = 17$) using closure-free cell-level module activity scores confirmed a conserved three-compartment fibrotic triad (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Table 4). Across all 64 donors, injured epithelial or hepatocyte parenchyma coupled tightly with profibrotic $TREM2^+SPP1^+$ SAMacs and $COL1A1^+POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts ($r = 0.45$, two-sided Pearson $P = 2.07 \times 10^{-4}$; human liver $r = 0.86$, $P = 1.20 \times 10^{-5}$, parenchyma-SAMac $r = 0.61$, $P = 0.0091$, and SAMac-myofibroblast $r = 0.62$, $P = 0.0084$). Across 19 human myeloid donor profiles spanning blood monocytes, healthy liver macrophages, F2/F3 MASH, and F4 cirrhosis, circulating monocyte `CCR2` expression (mean log-expression 6.19) plunged by 76% to 1.47 in F2/F3 intrahepatic macrophages. Concurrently, tissue-resident $SPP1^+$ (0.00 in blood and 5.46 in healthy liver rising to 8.31 in F4 cirrhosis) and $TREM2^+$ (0.39 to 5.77) SAMacs expanded, explaining why the high-$\tau_0$ `CCR2/CCR5` antagonist cenicriviroc ($\tau_0 = 0.9531$) failed in Phase III `AURORA` (`NCT03028740`) [25] (Fig. 4b).

We compiled the three-compartment stochastic Itô-Langevin grammar exclusively from the 17 human liver donors without accessing any clinical trial outcome labels. Bilateral 25-point profile-likelihood scans with nuisance rate-scale re-optimization verified structural identifiability ($\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$) across all four directed intercellular edges [26, 27] (Extended Data Fig. 3a). To eliminate hand-coded drug-compartment heuristics, target compartment weights were derived objectively from organ-specific single-cell pseudobulk expression ($\ge 58\%$ of peak compartmental expression) coupled with $\tau_0$-weighted receptor-sink dilution and unsigned ridge-GRN causal leverage. Extrahepatic incretin peptides lacking intrahepatic receptor expression (`GLP1R` and `GIPR`) and renal proximal-tubule `SLC5A2` ($\tau_0 = 0.8959$, expressed in tubular epithelium rather than `Myofibroblast` or `SAMac` scar compartments in `GSE271188` and acting via tubuloglomerular hemodynamic unloading) were routed objectively to upstream `Parenchyma_Injury` unloading. Freezing network topology and Hill kinetics, we evaluated 24 live ClinicalTrials.gov SHA-256-locked Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials, comprising 10 regulatory or Phase IIb/III histological winners versus 14 explicit scientific efficacy failures across liver MASH ($n = 13$), lung IPF ($n = 8$), and kidney CKD ($n = 3$), including the pre-specified Core-14 landmark subset [28-40] (Fig. 4c).

Across all 24 Phase IIb/III trials, zero-label Langevin transfer separated clinical winners from failures with AUROC = 0.8714 (95% CI 0.7071–0.9929), AUPRC = 0.7769, Brier error = 0.2072, and 10,000-permutation exact $P = 0.0010$ ($P = 0.0103$ versus static $\tau_0$ [AUROC = 0.4643]). This biophysical score outperformed single-cell GRN retention (0.8357), enrolled-stage prior alone (`B0`, 0.7714), modality prior (0.7500), target count (0.6750), and static ligand-receptor degree (0.4714) (Table 2 and Fig. 4d). On the Core-14 landmark subset, OmniNiche achieved AUROC = 0.9583 (95% CI 0.8333–1.0000, AUPRC = 0.9484, Brier = 0.1191, exact $P = 0.0031$). Even when initial histological stage was held identical across all trials (`OmniNiche_StageBlind_FixedS0`), target-by-barrier dynamics retained significant discrimination across all 24 trials (AUROC = 0.7643, exact $P = 0.0311$) and reached AUROC = 0.9375 ($P = 0.0058$) on Core-14, surpassing enrolled-stage prior alone (0.8125). Excluding the three extrahepatic or hemodynamic-routed regimens (`semaglutide`, `tirzepatide`, and `dapagliflozin`) likewise preserved AUROC = 0.8673 across all 21 strictly intra-organ direct-receptor trials (versus 0.3367 for static $\tau_0$; Core-13 AUROC = 0.9500).

Individual trial predictions resolved distinct antifibrotic mechanisms while highlighting the difference between continuous rank discrimination and raw probability thresholding under zero-label cross-organ transfer (Fig. 4c). Small-molecule `THRB` agonist resmetirom (`MAESTRO-NASH`, simulated probability 0.9648), pan-PPAR agonist lanifibranor (`NATIVE`, 0.9649), multi-kinase inhibitor nintedanib (`INPULSIS-1`, 0.9704), and metabolic peptides semaglutide (`ESSENCE`, 0.7531) and tirzepatide (`SYNERGY-NASH`, 0.8545) exceeded the physical 0.50 attractor-basin threshold, matching human MASH histology and proteomics showing absent intrahepatic `GLP1R` alongside systemic suppression of circulating `TREM2` and fibrotic remodeling [28, 30, 37]. Under stage-blind `OmniNiche_StageBlind_FixedS0` ($s_0 = [0.66, 0.61, 0.60]$), semaglutide (0.0592) and tirzepatide (0.0623) dropped sharply whereas failed `FGFR1/KLB` biologic pegbelfermin (`FALCON 1`) rose from 0.1589 to 0.9648, reflecting the biological stage boundary whereby upstream metabolic unloading resolves pre-saddle F2/F3 steatohepatitis but cannot reverse post-saddle cirrhosis.

Conversely, five clinical winners acting through pleiotropic, cAMP, endocrine, or tubuloglomerular hemodynamic pathways compressed into an intermediate 0.0535-0.2330 probability band under liver-compiled Hill parameters: pirfenidone (`ASCEND`, 0.2330), efruxifermin (`HARMONY`, 0.2299), dapagliflozin (`DAPA-CKD`, 0.0749), nerandomilast (`FIBRONEER-IPF`, 0.0682), and finerenone (`FIDELIO-DKD`, 0.0535). Consequently, the raw physical 0.50 basin cutoff yielded 50.0% sensitivity (5/10 winners: 4/5 hepatic, 1/3 pulmonary, 0/2 renal) and 92.9% specificity (13/14 failures, with permeable autotaxin inhibitor ziritaxestat [`ISABELA 1`, 0.9667] as the sole high-scoring off-niche toxicity failure). By contrast, the empirical Youden-optimal rank threshold ($p \ge 0.0535$, $J = 0.7143$) captured 10 of 10 winners (100.0% sensitivity) at 71.4% specificity (10/14 failures), because sterically excluded antibodies (`Simtuzumab`, `Pamrevlumab`, `Lebrikizumab`, 0.0366-0.0442) and lineage-eroded `Cenicriviroc` or `Selonsertib` (0.0409-0.0423) clustered below 0.048. Bidirectional $3 \times 3$ single-organ compilation and leave-one-organ-out (LOOO) validation confirmed symmetric rank transfer: lung-only compilation ($n = 30$) achieved All-24 AUROC = 0.8500 (held-out 0.8571; Core-14 0.9583), kidney-only compilation ($n = 17$) achieved 0.8393 (held-out 0.8365; Core-14 0.9583), and LOOO calibrations achieved 0.8357-0.8821 across All-24 and 0.8667-1.0000 on held-out organs (Table 2 and Fig. 4e).

### Bistable Waddington hysteresis defines a late-stage monotherapy cliff and orthogonal rescue law

The sharp loss of simulated and clinical incretin efficacy between pre-cirrhotic F2/F3 steatohepatitis and F4 cirrhosis pointed to a dynamical phase transition rather than a gradual linear decline. Reconstructing the two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential surface $U(S_{\mathrm{Myofib}}, S_{\mathrm{Paren}})$ and scanning 25 anatomical progression states from early F1/F2 steatohepatitis to F4 cirrhosis explained why single-compartment drugs lose activity in advanced scars [9, 30, 32] (Fig. 4f,g). Early F1/F2 disease resides in a monostable reversible valley where unloading parenchymal injury (`THRB` or `GLP1R`) permits spontaneous relaxation of downstream SAMac and myofibroblast activity. Once progression crosses a saddle-node bifurcation ($s_{\mathrm{prog}} \approx 0.42\text{–}0.54$, corresponding to monotherapy window cliff $t_{50} = 8.3\text{ h}$), cooperative Hill feedback ($n = 3.6$) between $SPP1^+TREM2^+$ SAMacs and $POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts forms a deep second attractor (`Locked F4`, $s_0 = [0.854, 0.880, 0.901]$) that traps every single-agent monotherapy at 0.0% simulated remission (Fig. 4f,g).

Evaluating a symmetric $6 \times 6$ clinical-target two-dimensional Bliss synergy matrix alongside a continuous $13 \times 13$ dose-response synergy surface in locked F4 cirrhosis identified a strict orthogonal in silico rescue rule (Fig. 4h and Supplementary Table 4). Pairing two parenchymal agents (`THRB + GLP1R`, 0.0% remission, Bliss excess $0.00$) or combining an active parenchymal agent with sterically blocked anti-`LOXL2` IgG or target-lost `CCR2` blockade (Bliss excess $0.00$) failed to breach the stromal saddle. Conversely, all four orthogonal combinations pairing upstream parenchymal unloading (`THRB` or `GLP1R`) with direct small-molecule myofibroblast `TGFBR1/PDGFRB` inhibition or `TREM2/SPP1` SAMac modulation collapsed the hysteresis barrier and restored 99.1% to 100.0% simulated remission (theoretical bistable attractor-escape Bliss excess $+0.99$ to $+1.00$, nominating prospective experimental combination testing). Across the continuous $13 \times 13$ perturbation grid ($\eta_{\mathrm{Paren}} \in [0, 0.90]$, $\eta_{\mathrm{Myofib}} \in [0, 0.30]$; 169 grid points), monotherapy axes remained trapped at 0.0% remission whereas 76 of 169 dual-perturbation points crossed the 50% remission isobologram and 86 of 169 exhibited positive Bliss synergy $> 0.02$ (`THRB + TGFBR1i` 99.5% remission, Bliss excess $+1.00$) (Fig. 4g,h).

## Discussion

Computational target discovery has long treated clinical efficacy as a static gene property inferred from germline genetics and healthy cell atlases [3-5]. By uniting validated clause-aware outcome deconvolution across 41,396 Phase II-IV clinical trials ($\kappa = 0.941$) with primary human single-cell, pre-treatment bulk, and 10x Visium transcriptomics across 237 clinical specimens, our study reframes drug translation across peripheral solid and mucosal organs as a dynamic tissue-accessibility problem, gated initially by diseased-niche target specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$) and refined across clinical maturation by stage progression drift and macromolecular steric exclusion. Under this framework, human genetics and dynamic niche accessibility (`DNAcc`) resolve two orthogonal questions: germline genetics asks whether perturbing a gene causally modifies disease biology (AUROC = 0.6362), whereas `DNAcc` asks whether a given therapeutic modality can still find and reach that target inside remodeled human tissue. Empirically, `DNAcc` reaches AUROC = 0.6710 alone (anchored by $\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$ at 0.6614 with maturation refinement to 0.6744, $P = 8.93 \times 10^{-13}$) and combines additively with human genetics to reach 0.7023 scientific and 0.7210 maturation AUROC. Conversely, once operational halts (51.4% of all trial stoppages) are stripped from registry labels, static healthy specificity ($\tau_0$) adds zero predictive value to human genetics ($P = 0.45$) and collapses to unity ($\mathrm{OR} = 0.97$, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.72$) alongside `DNAcc`.

Three mechanistic principles reconcile our macro-registry, 24-trial antifibrotic, and 107-biopsy oncology findings with recent clinical observations. First, 17.8% to 37.1% of profiled targets switch dominant cellular lineage during human pathology, explaining why targets selected from healthy atlases or circulating blood monocytes (`CCR2` in MASH) lose engagement inside advanced lesions [25]. Second, macromolecular hydrodynamic radius interacts directly with interstitial ECM remodeling: ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies incur a 1.57-fold excess in biological efficacy futility across the registry and fail uniformly in densely crosslinked scars (`LOXL2`, `CCN2`, and `IL13` antibodies) [29, 32], whereas small molecules (`THRB`, pan-`PPAR`, and `PDGFR/FGFR/VEGFR` agents) and compact acylated peptides (`GLP1R` and `GLP1R/GIPR` agonists) achieve late-stage histological resolution [28, 30, 37]. Our indirect parenchymal-unloading route reconciles a prominent paradox in human MASH: Jara et al. [28] confirmed absent `GLP1R` protein across all parenchymal and non-parenchymal cells in 26 human livers yet observed broad reversal of circulating fibrotic and `TREM2`-linked soma-proteomic signatures (`ADAMTSL2`, `TREM2`) under semaglutide, because extrahepatic metabolic unloading relaxes the upstream hepatocyte-to-SAMac injury drive prior to the bistable cirrhosis saddle.

Third, advanced organ fibrosis and immune-excluded solid tumors behave as bistable Waddington attractors locked by reciprocal myeloid-mesenchymal feedback (`SPP1+TREM2+` SAMac/TAM coupled to `POSTN+` myofibroblast/`myCAF`) [9, 10, 42, 57, 58], creating an abrupt monotherapy cliff that requires orthogonal cross-compartment combinations. Direct human fibroblast perturbation datasets (`GSE226249`, `GSE154646`, and `GSE210020`) [52-54] independently corroborate that small-molecule `TGFBR1`/ALK5 blockade, pirfenidone, and nintedanib collapse this locked `POSTN+/ACTA2+` myofibroblast barrier in human cells. This spatial barrier model also reconciles recent clinical immunotherapy cohorts because peritumoral stromal geometry gates effector entry independently of bulk T-cell transcription: Li et al. [59] showed that `FAP+/collagen IV+` stromal and `CD163+/MMP9+` myeloid co-localization stratifies checkpoint outcomes where `PD-L1` fails. In parallel, Shen et al. [60] identified `TGFB`-driven fibroblast and endothelial exclusion in inflamed non-responders, and Yoo et al. [61] documented frequent divergence between overall survival and surrogate radiographic shrinkage, matching our melanoma survival separation (`HR = 1.95` unadjusted; `HR = 3.02` multivariable versus `HR = 0.84` in non-ICB melanoma). Because systemic first-generation pan-`TGFBR1` (ALK5) inhibitors caused dose-limiting cardiac valvulopathy via homeostatic endocardial `TGFB` signaling, clinical execution of orthogonal rescue should prioritize spatially or lineage-restricted modulators, including inhaled ALK5 inhibitors [62], selective $LRRC15^+$ myofibroblast or latent-`TGFB1` blockade [63], and myeloid `TREM2/SPP1` or `CXCL9:SPP1` reprogramming (74.6% versus 78.1% simulated rescue) [42].

Methodologically, OmniNiche complements supervised clinical artificial-intelligence models trained on routine blood panels, bulk transcriptomes, or histopathology-derived virtual spatial proteomics, including SCORPIO, COMPASS, and HEX-MICA [59-61], as well as foundation repurposing and virtual-cell platforms [6, 64, 65]. Whereas supervised predictors achieve high accuracy when regimen-matched clinical training cohorts exist, OmniNiche requires zero clinical response labels during biophysical compilation, providing a white-box mechanistic prior for emerging targets and untested combinations lacking historical outcome data. Beyond small molecules, peptides, and conventional IgG antibodies, the modular `DNAcc` and spatial-Langevin grammar can be reused directly across emerging therapeutic modalities, including antibody-drug conjugates (~150-kDa carrier penetration coupled to bystander payload diffusion), bispecific T-cell engagers, and targeted oligonucleotide conjugates, whenever paired stage-resolved single-cell and spatial references are available.

Several limitations bound the interpretation of our findings and define their expected direction of statistical bias. First, 8.8% ($n = 2,618 / 29,791$) of target-resolved ClinicalTrials.gov records (9.1%, $n = 3,775 / 41,396$ registry-wide) halt without narrative reasons, and six of the 12 registry axes rely on consensus drift imputation alongside harmonized $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ stromal ECM scaling. Both constraints introduce non-differential label and feature noise that conservatively attenuates effect sizes toward the null, as bounded by worst-case unreported-failure assignment (`R3` AUROC = 0.6533), regulatory-approval outcomes (`R6` AUROC = 0.6767), and zero-shot `LODO` organ transfer (`AUROC = 0.6306`, $P = 5.19 \times 10^{-16}$). While three-feature `M3` ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$) captures >90% of `DNAcc`'s baseline-to-lesion scientific lift over healthy $\tau_0$ and slightly exceeds unpenalized nine-feature `M6` under Core-6 `Group5Fold` (0.6575 versus 0.6415) due to finite-sample variance across 386 failure events, ongoing progression drift and macromolecular steric exclusion govern Phase II-to-III/IV maturation ($P = 8.93 \times 10^{-13}$), single-target monotherapies ($P = 0.0353$), the 1.57-fold antibody futility excess, 24-trial antifibrotic transfer (`Spearman` $\rho = 0.7861$ with macro `DNAcc`), and melanoma survival.

Second, in zero-label cross-organ antifibrotic transfer, high continuous rank discrimination (`AUROC = 0.8714`; Youden cutoff $p \ge 0.0535$: 10/10 sensitivity, 10/14 specificity) coexists with compressed absolute probabilities (0.0535-0.2330, 50% sensitivity at the raw 0.50 basin threshold) for pulmonary and renal drugs acting through hemodynamic, endocrine, or cAMP axes weakly parameterized by three-compartment hepatic Hill kinetics (`pirfenidone`, `efruxifermin`, `dapagliflozin`, `nerandomilast`, and `finerenone`). In parallel, permeable `ziritaxestat` represents an off-niche systemic toxicity false positive, and incretin unloading exhibits strict pre-cirrhotic stage dependence consistent with negative Phase IIb semaglutide histology in compensated F4 cirrhosis [66]. Third, in solid-tumor checkpoint blockade, bulk tissue lysis collapses concentric `myCAF` geometry to linear-combination parity (`OmniNiche` 0.6367 versus `Linear Mean` 0.6442 on $n = 107$), while pan-visceral Visium priors slightly attenuate cutaneous BCC discrimination relative to `No-Visium` (0.8750 versus 1.0000). Finally, paired longitudinal T-cell trajectories ($n = 15$, Mann-Whitney $P = 0.78$) and deterministic operator rescue shifts (78.1% and 74.6%) remain hypothesis-generating pending combination-trial biopsies, while 55-µm Visium capture spots conservatively dilute sharp sub-spot T-cell exclusion boundaries toward zero (retaining 48.3% admixture-residualized `SEI`, toroidal Stouffer $Z = +7.82$). Comparing anti-PD-1 monotherapy melanoma (multivariable HR = 3.02, E-value = 3.68) against 264 non-ICB metastatic melanoma patients (multivariable HR = 0.84, $P = 0.389$; interaction HR = 3.60, $P = 0.0059$) establishes immunotherapy-specific survival hazard, although prospective randomized combination trials remain essential to confirm interventional barrier reversal.

Prospective silent-mode shadow evaluations embedded in ongoing Phase II trials, alongside biomarker-stratified combination trials pairing pre-treatment spatial transcriptomic or multiplex proteomic biopsies with lineage-selective stromal modulators, are warranted to calibrate patient-level decision thresholds. In line with responsible clinical AI standards [60], OmniNiche outputs are intended as mechanistic hypothesis-generating instruments for target prioritization, modality matching, and biomarker-enriched trial design and must never be used in isolation to withhold standard-of-care immunotherapy or antifibrotic therapy. Anchoring computational target discovery and virtual-tissue trials in dynamic single-cell remodeling and spatial barrier biophysics establishes a quantitative path from static gene prioritization to tissue-aware therapeutic design.

## Methods

### Ethics and research oversight

This study analyzed exclusively publicly available, deidentified clinical trial registry records and previously published, deidentified human single-cell, bulk, and spatial transcriptomic datasets approved by the institutional review boards of the original data-generating studies, with written informed consent obtained from all participants in those primary cohorts. No new human participants were recruited and no identifiable private health information was accessed; secondary computational analysis of public deidentified data did not require additional institutional review board approval.

### ClinicalTrials.gov registry ingestion, scope definition, and target-gene resolution

Interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV clinical trial records were retrieved from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (`https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies`) [14] across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes: Solid Oncology, Metabolic and Endocrine, Hematologic Oncology, Pulmonary (IPF and ARDS), Cardiovascular, Autoimmune (IBD and Rheumatology), Renal (CKD and AKI), Dermatologic Immune, Musculoskeletal, Ophthalmic and Retinal, Hepatic (MASH and Cirrhosis), and Infectious and Sepsis. To maintain uniform interstitial transport biophysics, the cohort focuses on peripheral systemic and solid-organ pathologies governed by interstitial extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling rather than specialized blood-brain barrier neurovascular transport (43,878 raw axis-level registry hits deduplicated to 41,983 unique NCT studies prior to excluding 587 primary central nervous system or brain-metastasis records; 41,396 unique NCT studies retained).

Intervention strings, generic names, and synonyms were normalized and mapped against ChEMBL v33 curated drug mechanisms (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data`) [15] and Open Targets Platform v24.09 drug-target and indication evidence (`https://api.platform.opentargets.org/api/v4/graphql`) [5], resolving 29,791 unique trials (72.0%) to 1,155 HGNC human target gene symbols (586 unique primary target genes; 1,181 total target and pathway genes profiled in the six-axis single-cell drift atlas) and standardized therapeutic modalities: 18,128 small-molecule regimens (17,963 typed small molecules and 165 untyped chemical entries), 7,308 IgG monoclonal antibody regimens, and 4,355 non-antibody biologic regimens (4,021 protein or peptide regimens, classifying compact acylated peptides such as 4.11-kDa semaglutide and 4.81-kDa tirzepatide as `peptide`, and 334 cell, gene, or oligonucleotide therapies).

### Unified clause-aware stoppage deconvolution and 240-trial clinical adjudication

Stopped trials (`TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `SUSPENDED`; $n = 9,406$ across the 41,396-trial registry, alongside $n = 31,990$ `COMPLETED` studies) were classified by a unified deterministic clause-aware natural-language parser operating identically across registry ingestion and validation on verbatim `whyStopped` narratives [13]. Narratives were segmented across punctuation and contrastive clause boundaries (`;`, `.`, `but`, `however`, `although`, `whereas`, `while`, `unrelated to`, `independent of`, `with no`, `without any`, and `not due to`) prior to lexical matching. Clauses containing negated safety or efficacy disclaimers (such as `no safety concerns`, `not due to safety or efficacy`, `absence of toxicity`, or `no unexpected adverse events`) were stripped of false-positive biological triggers before classification. Remaining non-negated futility or lack-of-efficacy clauses were assigned to `Scientific_Efficacy_Futility` ($n = 476$), explicit non-negated toxicity, adverse-event, or clinical-hold clauses to `Scientific_Safety_Toxicity` ($n = 317$), operational, commercial, accrual, sponsor-reprioritization, or pandemic clauses to `Administrative_Commercial_Enrollment` ($n = 4,838$; 51.4% of stopped trials), and empty or non-specific narratives to `Other_Unreported_Stop` ($n = 3,775$).

Parser accuracy, per-class precision, recall, F1, and Cohen's $\kappa$ were benchmarked head-to-head against a Naive Keyword Baseline (which matches raw safety, futility, and operational substrings without clause splitting or negation stripping) on an independent 240-trial clinical adjudication reference (`Manual_Clinical_Adjudication_240`: 64 efficacy futility, 54 safety/toxicity, 96 administrative/operational, and 26 unreported/other stops; Supplementary Table 2). Excluding administrative ($n = 3,412$) and unreported ($n = 2,618$) halts among the 29,791 target-resolved studies defined the 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 565 explicit biological failures [328 futility and 237 safety] across 374 primary targets) and the 23,761-trial Definitive Phase II-to-III/IV Maturation Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 12,689 Phase II non-maturing or biologically failed trials across 561 primary targets).

### Primary human single-cell, pre-treatment bulk, and 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics processing

All single-cell, single-nucleus, pre-treatment bulk, and spatial transcriptomic datasets were processed from primary human clinical matrices retrieved from NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) [21] and the Human Protein Atlas [7] without synthetic imputation, maintaining peak Python memory below 2.3 GB via exact single-UMI streaming (`umi_stride = 1`), compressed sparse matrices, and donor-level pseudobulk aggregation across 237 human clinical specimens:

1. **Healthy baseline reference**: Human Protein Atlas v23 single-cell transcriptomics consensus normalized expression across 154 human cell types [7].
2. **Hepatic MASH and cirrhosis**: `GSE136103` primary human healthy and cirrhotic liver plus paired circulating blood monocyte scRNA-seq ($n = 10$ liver donors and $4$ blood monocyte baselines) [9] combined with `GSE212837` primary human control and F2/F3 NASH liver snRNA-seq ($n = 7$ donors; $17$ liver biopsy donors and $19$ stage-resolved myeloid profiles in total) [16].
3. **Pulmonary fibrosis**: `GSE135893` primary human control, intermediate interstitial lung disease, and F3/F4 IPF lung scRNA-seq ($n = 30$ donors) [17].
4. **Renal fibrosis**: `GSE271188` primary human control, minimal-change baseline, and fibrotic CKD/DKD/IgAN/MN kidney snRNA-seq ($n = 17$ donors) [18, 67].
5. **Solid oncology single-cell, pre-treatment bulk biopsy, and 10x Visium spatial cohorts**: `GSE123813` paired pre- and post-anti-PD-1 monotherapy human cutaneous carcinoma scRNA-seq ($n = 8$ strictly pre-treatment full-TME BCC patients and $n = 15$ paired longitudinal patients comprising 11 BCC and 4 cutaneous SCC, streamed at exact single-UMI fidelity with separate `CD45+CD3+` FACS-enriched T-cell sorting tubes deconvoluted from non-`CD3+` TME stromal cells; 7 of the 15 longitudinal patients were sequenced from T-cell-sorted fractions lacking paired non-`CD3+` `myCAF` counts, and longitudinal cytotoxic trajectory was defined as `Net CD8+ Cytotoxic Score` $= S_{\mathrm{CD8,eff}} - 0.5\,S_{\mathrm{CD8,exh}}$) [46]; `GSE91061` strictly pre-nivolumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 49$, 10 responders) [48]; `GSE78220` strictly pre-pembrolizumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 26$ unique patients, 14 responders) [49]; `GSE207422` strictly pre-treatment neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 plus platinum-doublet chemo-immunotherapy NSCLC bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 24$, `Pre_biopsy`, 9 responders) and post-neoadjuvant resected NSCLC TME scRNA-seq ($n = 14$, 4 responders) [47]; and 12 primary human carcinoma 10x Visium spatial sections (`GSE171351` bladder carcinoma $n = 4$ [43] and `GSE203612` breast, ovarian, liver, and gastrointestinal carcinoma $n = 8$ [44]; 20,880 quality-filtered spots of 21,796 total profiled spots). Solid-oncology progression drift ($\Delta\tau$) for `DNAcc` was computed strictly from response-label-free early primary BCC tumors (`GSE123813`) versus advanced resected NSCLC tumors (`GSE207422`) without accessing clinical immunotherapy response labels.
6. **Autoimmune mucosal inflammation and intravascular sepsis**: `GSE250487` human ulcerative colitis and control mucosal biopsies ($n = 28$ donor biopsies demultiplexed across 8 GEO sequencing pools) [19, 68] and `GSE167363` longitudinal human sepsis PBMCs ($n = 12$) [20].
7. **External human fibroblast perturbation and non-ICB melanoma specificity cohorts**: `GSE226249` primary human IPF and normal lung fibroblast RNA-seq across basal serum-free, 20% serum-activated, and 1 mM pirfenidone conditions ($n = 27$ across 3 human donor lines) [52]; `GSE154646` human carcinoma niche RNA-seq across paired vehicle DMSO, `TGF-beta1` stimulation, and selective `TGFBR1`/ALK5 inhibitor RepSox triplets ($n = 24$, 8 triplets) [53]; `GSE210020` wild-type primary human diploid fibroblast RNA-seq under 5 µM nintedanib versus DMSO ($n = 22$ wild-type samples from 40 total series samples) [54]; `GSE65904` population-based metastatic melanoma biopsies without anti-PD-1 therapy ($n = 210$ with disease-specific survival follow-up from 214 samples, 102 deaths) [55]; and `GSE22153` Stage IV melanoma biopsies without checkpoint blockade ($n = 54$ with overall survival follow-up from 57 samples, 47 deaths) [56], scored on $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ or quantile-normalized $\log_2$ probe intensities with zero signature weight refitting.

### Dynamic specificity drift, empirical ECM barrier density, `DNAcc` ablation, and human genetics benchmark

For any target gene $g$ across $K$ cellular compartments with normalized mean log-expression $x_{g,k} \ge 0$, Yanai's specificity index [8] was computed in healthy baseline ($\tau_0$), early disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and advanced disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$) as:

$$\tau(g) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(1 - \frac{x_{g,k}}{\max_{j} x_{g,j}}\right)}{K - 1}$$

Dynamic specificity drift was defined as $\Delta\tau(g) = \tau_{\mathrm{late}}(g) - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g)$, dynamic retention as $R_{\mathrm{dyn}}(g) = 0.5\,\tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g) + 0.5\,\mathrm{clip}(0.5 + \Delta\tau(g), 0, 1)$, and ectopic compartment switching $I_{\mathrm{switch}}(g) \in \{0, 1\}$ as a shift in dominant expressing lineage ($\arg\max_k x_{g,k}$) between early and advanced pathology (tabulated across all profiled targets as primary metric and among expressed targets with $\max_k x_{g,k} > 0.05$ in Supplementary Table 3). For the six registry disease axes without dedicated stage-resolved single-cell progression datasets (`51.3%` of scientific trials), `Dermatologic_Immune` inherited mucosal epithelial-immune barrier drift features from `Autoimmune_IBD_Rheum`, whereas `Cardiovascular_HF_Athero`, `Metabolic_Endocrine`, `Hematologic_Malignancy`, `Musculoskeletal_Bone`, and `Ophthalmic_Retinal` assigned gene-specific pan-organ mean progression features (`consensus_drift`) averaged across the six directly profiled single-cell axes while preserving exact gene-level healthy $\tau_0$, vital-organ bystander hazard $H_{\mathrm{vital}}(g)$, drug modality, and axis-specific ECM density. Vital-organ bystander hazard $H_{\mathrm{vital}}(g)$ quantified peak healthy expression across cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, renal tubular epithelium, and alveolar pneumocytes relative to target lineage expression. Axis-specific interstitial ECM barrier density $D_{\mathrm{ECM}}(\mathrm{axis}) \in [0.070, 0.350]$ was derived objectively from a 10-gene collagen-myofibroblast signature (`COL1A1`, `COL1A2`, `COL3A1`, `FN1`, `POSTN`, `ACTA2`, `FAP`, `LUM`, `DCN`, `THBS2`) after harmonizing both advanced human single-cell stromal pseudobulk profiles and Human Protein Atlas v23 organ stromal cell types onto a common $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ expression scale prior to min-max normalization (`Solid_Oncology` = 0.3500, `Pulmonary_IPF_ARDS` = 0.3265, `Autoimmune_IBD_Rheum` = 0.2921, down to `Infectious_Sepsis` = 0.0700; `Supplementary Table 3`), yielding modality-by-matrix barrier compatibility $B_{\mathrm{acc}}(m, d)$ across small molecules, peptides, and ~150-kDa IgG monoclonal antibodies.

In the six-step nested logistic ablation (`M1` through `M6`), out-of-fold trial probabilities were evaluated under both stratified 5-fold cross-validation and target-disjoint 5-fold `GroupKFold` grouped by `primary_target_gene` (`M1`: $[\tau_0]$; `M2`: $+ H_{\mathrm{vital}}$; `M3`: $+ \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$; `M4`: $+ \Delta\tau + R_{\mathrm{dyn}}$; `M5`: $+ I_{\mathrm{switch}} + S_{\mathrm{stromal}}$; `M6`: full `DNAcc` adding $B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and the $\tau_{\mathrm{early}} \times B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ delivery-selectivity interaction). For the orthogonal human genetics benchmark [4, 5], Open Targets Platform v24.09 target-disease genetic association ($g_{\mathrm{assoc}}$) and somatic mutation ($g_{\mathrm{som}}$) evidence scores were mapped per trial-indication pair into the four-feature genetic vector $[g_{\mathrm{assoc}}, g_{\mathrm{som}}, g_{\max}, \mathbb{I}(g_{\max} \ge 0.10)]$ (`G1`) and compared head-to-head against static $\tau_0$ (`G2`), `Human Genetics + Static` $\tau_0$ (`G3`), `DNAcc` alone (`G4`), and `Joint Human Genetics + DNAcc` (`G5`) under identical Stratified 5-Fold and Unseen-Target `Group5Fold` splits (`Supplementary Table 3`).

### Closure-free fibrosis coupling, Visium spatial proximity, and stochastic Langevin Waddington solver

Across the 64 human liver, lung, and kidney biopsy donors, compartment states (`Parenchyma_Injury`, `SAMac_SPP1_TREM2`, and `Myofibroblast`) were quantified from cell-level $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ transcriptional module activity rather than cell-count proportions that sum to unity, eliminating compositional closure artifacts; directed intercellular coupling weights were compiled from donor-level module activity Pearson correlations. Drug-compartment weights $w_c \in [0, 1]$ across all 24 SHA-256-locked Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials were derived automatically from organ-specific single-cell pseudobulk expression (assigning active engagement where compartmental $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ reached $\ge 58\%$ of the target's peak compartment), scaled by $\tau_0$-weighted receptor-sink dilution $\min(1, (\min(\sqrt{n_t}, 1.85)/\max(\sum_c w_c, 1))(0.76 + 0.48\bar{\tau}_0))$ and unsigned ridge-GRN causal leverage at reference potency $\mathrm{pIC}_{50} = 7.5$. Extrahepatic metabolic incretin targets lacking intrahepatic receptor expression (`GLP1R` and `GIPR`) and renal proximal-tubule cotransporter `SLC5A2` (which exhibits high healthy tubular specificity $\tau_0 = 0.8959$ in `GSE271188` epithelium rather than `Myofibroblast` or `SAMac` scar compartments and acts via tubuloglomerular hemodynamic unloading) were routed to upstream `Parenchyma_Injury` unloading (reference leverage $\eta = 0.5400$; $0.7637$ for dual `GLP1R;GIPR`). Steric accessibility followed $B_{\mathrm{acc}} = 1 - c_m D_{\mathrm{ECM}}$ ($c_m = 0.72$ for IgG monoclonal antibodies, $0.42$ for large proteins, and $0.07$ for small molecules or compact peptides), evaluated from stage-specific initial states (`F2_F3` $= [0.54, 0.43, 0.39]$, `F3_F4` $= [0.78, 0.82, 0.84]$, `F4_Cirrhosis` $= [0.86, 0.89, 0.91]$) and a stage-blind fixed cohort-centroid baseline (`OmniNiche_StageBlind_FixedS0`, $s_0 = [0.66, 0.61, 0.60]$), contrasting continuous rank AUROC and empirical Youden-optimal thresholding ($p \ge 0.0535$) against the raw physical $0.50$ attractor-basin cutoff.

For each 10x Visium section ($21,796$ total profiled spots across 12 carcinoma sections; $20,880$ quality-filtered spots in Supplementary Table 5), standardized spot-level compartment scores $z_c(u)$ were linked via physical $k = 6$ Euclidean nearest-neighbor graphs, mapping directed $k = 6$ nearest-neighbor bivariate Moran spatial lag $I_{ij}$ (exhibiting minor boundary-induced off-diagonal directional asymmetry $\le 0.002$ on irregular tissue margins in Extended Data Fig. 4b) to proximity $P_{ij} = (1 + \exp(-4.5\,I_{ij}))^{-1}$ and Stromal Exclusion Index $\mathrm{SEI} = P_{\mathrm{myCAF,TAM}} - P_{\mathrm{Tumor,CD8}}$. Physical 500-µm scale bars equaled five times the median 100-µm Visium spot pitch, and continuous radial profiles tracked minimum distance from malignant core spots (top quartile of `Tumor` score) across 0 to 6 spot pitches (0 to 600 µm). In the five-compartment solid-tumor solver (`Tumor`, `CD8_Effector`, `TAM_ImmunoSupp`, `myCAF`, and `Treg_Exhaust`), joint `myCAF` and `TAM` capsule load $C_{\mathrm{cap}} = 0.76\,S_{\mathrm{myCAF}} + 0.24\,S_{\mathrm{TAM}}$ gated anti-PD-1 and $CD8^+$ T-cell access via $C_{\mathrm{cap}}^{4.2} / (0.62^{4.2} + C_{\mathrm{cap}}^{4.2})$ scaled by empirical Visium exclusion $\Delta P_{\mathrm{Visium}} = \max(0, P_{\mathrm{TAM,myCAF}} - P_{\mathrm{Tumor,CD8}})$, coupled with Holling Type-II effector-to-target tumor-burden saturation $(1 + 0.78(S_{\mathrm{Tumor}} / 0.55)^{2.9})^{-1}$. Multi-cohort pre-treatment pooling ($n = 83$ pure monotherapy and $n = 107$ total pre-treatment biopsies) used zero-label leave-one-patient-out (LOPO) robust median/MAD z-scoring mapped via an unclipped monotonic within-cohort logistic projection $(1 + \exp(-0.90\,\tilde{z}_{\mathrm{LOPO}}))^{-1}$ centered at the 50% decision threshold in Fig. 5c while archiving both cohort-aligned `simulated_icb_remission_prob` and raw physical `raw_icb_remission_prob` in Supplementary Table 5.

Multicellular state trajectories $\mathbf{S}(t) \in [0, 1]^N$ over compartments $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ were integrated over $T = 96.0\text{ h}$ with step $\Delta t = 0.5\text{ h}$ using fixed deterministic pseudorandom streams under the state-bounded stochastic Itô-Langevin differential equation [26]:

$$dS_j(t) = \left[ \alpha_j \rho_j(t) + (1 - S_j(t)) \rho_j(t) \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_j} v_{ij} \frac{(\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}}{K_{ij}^{n_{ij}} + (\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}} \right) \prod_{q \in \mathcal{I}_j} \frac{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}}}{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}} + (\rho_q(t) S_q(t))^{n_{qj}}} - \gamma_j S_j(t) \right] dt + \sigma_j \sqrt{S_j(t)(1 - S_j(t))}\, dW_j(t)$$

where $\alpha_j$, $\gamma_j$, and $\sigma_j$ denote baseline production, decay rate, and demographic noise amplitude; $v_{ij}$, $K_{ij}$, and $n_{ij}$ denote directed coupling weight, half-maximal constant, and Hill cooperativity across activating ($\mathcal{A}_j$) and inhibitory ($\mathcal{I}_j$) inputs; and $\rho_j(t) = \prod_{r \in \mathcal{R}_j(t)} (1 - \mathrm{clip}(\eta_r B_{\mathrm{acc},r}, -0.95, 0.98))$ denotes unblocked signaling under regimen $\mathcal{R}_j$ with perturbation efficacy $\eta_r$. Smooth ensemble remission probability was evaluated across 220 to 250 Monte Carlo trajectories as $\mathbb{E}[(1 + \exp((S_{\mathrm{rem}}(T) - \theta_{\mathrm{rem}}) / \tau_{\mathrm{kernel}}))^{-1}]$ ($\theta_{\mathrm{rem}} = 0.36$, $\tau_{\mathrm{kernel}} = 0.10$ for fibrosis `Myofibroblast`; $\theta_{\mathrm{rem}} = 0.45$, $\tau_{\mathrm{kernel}} = 0.11$ for solid-tumor `Tumor`). Two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential surfaces $U(x, y) = -\ln P_{\mathrm{ss}}(x, y)$ were estimated on a Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma = 2.2$ bins) $60 \times 60$ stationary density grid [26], and 25-point bilateral profile-likelihood curves scanned each edge weight $v_{ij} \in [0.50, 1.50] \times v_{ij,\mathrm{MLE}}$ while re-optimizing nuisance rate scale $k_{\mathrm{scale}} \in [0.82, 1.22]$, declaring structural identifiability at $\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$ [27].

### Statistical analysis

Correlated ROC curves on shared cohorts were compared using two-sided paired nonparametric DeLong tests [22], evaluating both cumulative gains over `M1` (static $\tau_0$) and incremental step attributions (`M6` versus `M3` early-disease specificity and sequential `M_{k}` versus `M_{k-1}`). Confidence intervals (95%) for AUROC, AUPRC, and Brier scores were computed via 2,000 stratified bootstrap resamples. For clinical trial and biopsy validation cohorts (24 and Core-14 antifibrotic Phase IIb/III trials and $n = 8$ to $107$ solid-tumor immunotherapy cohorts), exact empirical *P*-values were computed from 10,000 Monte Carlo label permutations using vectorized Mann-Whitney rank statistics. Multivariable confounder control used L2-regularized ($C = 50.0$) fixed-effects logistic regression reporting both analytic inverse-Hessian Wald standard errors and Liang-Zeger target-gene cluster-robust sandwich standard errors [23] ($\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{G}{G - 1}\mathbf{H}^{-1}(\sum_{g=1}^{G}\mathbf{u}_g\mathbf{u}_g^{\top})\mathbf{H}^{-1}$ across $G = 374$ and $G = 561$ unique `primary_target_gene` clusters), adjusting simultaneously for out-of-fold Open Targets human genetic support, static $\tau_0$, disease axis (11 indicator variables), drug modality, calendar start era (`Pre_2010`, `2010_2014`, `2015_2019`, `2020_Plus`), sponsor class (`INDUSTRY` versus `NON_INDUSTRY`), and polypharmacology ($n_{\mathrm{targets}} \ge 2$ versus single-target). Seven sensitivity regimes (`R1`-`R7`) audited robustness across naive keywords, worst-case failure assignment of all 2,618 target-resolved unreported stops, inverse-probability weighting, posted-results-only trials, ChEMBL/Open Targets regulatory approval gold standards, and the strict nine-axis solid-parenchymal boundary. Categorical $2 \times 2$ associations were tested by two-sided Fisher's exact test and paired spatial or trajectory comparisons by Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Overall survival across $n = 74$ pre-treatment anti-PD-1 monotherapy melanoma patients (`GSE78220` $n = 25$ and `GSE91061` $n = 49$, stratified at median pre-treatment stromal `CAF`-exclusion barrier) was evaluated by Kaplan-Meier estimation with Greenwood 95% CIs alongside both unstratified and cohort-stratified Mantel-Cox log-rank tests and 1-d.f. Cox partial-likelihood hazard ratios, and clinical utility by Decision Curve Analysis net benefit $\mathrm{NB}(p_t) = \mathrm{TP}/N - (\mathrm{FP}/N)[p_t / (1 - p_t)]$ [24]. Orthogonal stress tests evaluated Core-6 L1/L2 shrinkage and intercept-free Leave-One-Disease-Axis-Out (`LODO`) zero-shot transfer (fitting feature scalers and L2 weights $C = 0.25$ strictly within each five-axis training split without held-out axis labels), alongside ChEMBL v33 human target potency modulation ($\eta_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha) = \eta_0 \cdot \mathrm{clip}(1 + \alpha(\mathrm{pXC}_{50} - 7.5), 0.35, 1.55)$ for $\alpha \in \{0.08, 0.15, 0.25\}$) paired with Leave-One-Organ-Out (`LOOO`) Platt recalibration. Spatial and clinical stress tests evaluated UMI-depth and non-lymphoid admixture residualization with 2,000 two-dimensional toroidal cyclic coordinate-shift permutations per Visium section ($24,000$ realizations aggregated via spot-weighted Stouffer $Z$), four-cohort Leave-One-Cohort-Out (`LOCO`) pre-ICB transfer, and exact Breslow multivariable Cox regression (`Cox_Model1` cohort-adjusted `EPV = 21.5` and `Cox_Model2` five-covariate `EPV = 8.6`) with VanderWeele's unmeasured-confounding E-value ($\mathrm{HR} + \sqrt{\mathrm{HR}(\mathrm{HR} - 1)}$ on the common-outcome transformation) [69]. Pharmacological perturbation shifts in `GSE226249` ($n = 27$) and `GSE154646` ($n = 24$) were tested via two-sided paired $t$-tests (`GSE210020` $n = 22$ via Welch's $t$-test), immunotherapy survival specificity across $n = 338$ melanoma patients ($n = 74$ anti-PD-1 versus $n = 264$ non-ICB controls in `GSE65904` and `GSE22153`) via a two-regime Breslow Cox model including an `anti-PD-1 x locked stromal barrier` interaction term adjusted for cytotoxic T-cell score, `PD-L1`, and cohort, and 24-trial macro-to-meso consilience via two-sided Spearman and Pearson correlations (Supplementary Table 6). Multi-gene target screens controlled false discovery rates (FDR) via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure, single-cell and spatial reporting followed MINSEQE standards, and zero-label multicellular Langevin simulations adhered to an ASME V&V 40 verification, validation, and uncertainty quantification (VVUQ) Context of Use (itemized checklist in Supplementary Information Section 4). All computations ran in Python 3.14.4 (`NumPy 2.4.4`, `SciPy 1.16.3`, `pandas 2.3.3`, `scikit-learn 1.8.0`, and `Matplotlib 3.10.9`) with fixed deterministic pseudorandom seeds (`42` and `20260418`), verified end-to-end by a 55-test `pytest` suite.
