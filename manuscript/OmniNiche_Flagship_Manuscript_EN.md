# Dynamic single-cell niche remodeling and biophysical steric exclusion govern clinical trial attrition across human organs

---

## Abstract

Late-stage clinical trial attrition exceeds 85% despite widespread use of human genetic support and healthy-tissue expression atlases. Current target-prioritization frameworks assume that baseline organ specificity remains stationary during disease progression and that therapeutic macromolecules reach remodeled parenchymal, stromal, and immune compartments equally. Here we integrate 41,396 unique Phase II–IV clinical trials across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes (29,791 target-gene-resolved studies) with primary human single-cell and 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics (~460,000 cells and 21,796 spatial spots from 162 donors) inside OmniNiche, a mechanistic virtual-tissue framework. Clause-aware deconvolution of stopped trials separates commercial or accrual halts (40.3% of stops; 88.3% benchmark accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.844$) from biological efficacy futility, revealing that 150-kDa monoclonal antibodies incur 1.59-fold higher efficacy futility odds than small molecules ($P = 7.03 \times 10^{-5}$). Across six core single-cell disease axes, 21.5% to 37.1% of targets undergo ectopic lineage switching during progression. Unifying single-cell specificity drift with modality-dependent extracellular matrix steric exclusion into a Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) index improves outcome discrimination over healthy baseline specificity from AUROC 0.5283 to 0.6683 (paired DeLong $P = 8.68 \times 10^{-28}$; unseen-target cross-validation AUROC 0.6540) and remains independently predictive after multivariable confounder adjustment (adjusted odds ratio per s.d. = 1.43, $P = 9.21 \times 10^{-12}$). Coupled to a data-compiled stochastic Langevin solver, zero-shot cross-organ transfer across human liver, lung, and kidney fibrosis predicts 14 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials (AUROC = 0.9583, exact permutation $P = 0.0028$) and stratifies strictly pre-treatment solid-tumor anti-PD-1 biopsies across cancers without refitting (pooled $n = 32$ AUROC = 0.7551, $P = 0.0148$).

**Keywords:** clinical trial attrition, dynamic niche remodeling, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, organ fibrosis, cancer immunotherapy, biophysical systems pharmacology

## Introduction

Clinical drug development continues to suffer severe late-stage attrition, with nearly 90% of candidates entering human testing failing to achieve regulatory approval [1, 2]. Over the past decade, human genetic evidence has become the cornerstone of computational target prioritization, roughly doubling approval probabilities when causal variant-to-gene support links a target to its intended indication [3, 4]. Large-scale knowledge platforms such as Open Targets and graph foundation models such as TxGNN have further expanded zero-shot target and repurposing discovery across thousands of phenotypes [5, 6]. Parallel efforts use healthy human single-cell reference maps, including the Human Protein Atlas single-cell resource, to favor targets with high baseline tissue specificity ($\tau_0$) in order to minimize off-organ toxicity [7, 8]. Despite combining germline genetic support with healthy-tissue selectivity, pivotal Phase IIb and Phase III failures remain pervasive across fibrotic, inflammatory, metabolic, and oncologic diseases.

We hypothesized that this translational gap stems from evaluating drug targets against a static, healthy anatomical snapshot while overlooking two dynamic tissue-level barriers that emerge inside diseased human organs. First, chronic parenchymal injury, mucosal ulceration, and malignant growth trigger profound multicellular remodeling, including infiltration of profibrotic $TREM2^+SPP1^+$ scar-associated macrophages (SAMacs) and expansion of $COL1A1^+POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts [9, 10]. During this remodeling, a target gene that appears highly selective in healthy tissue can collapse in its intended lineage ($\Delta\tau < 0$) or switch ectopically into bystander stromal compartments. Second, extracellular matrix (ECM) crosslinking and peritumoral cancer-associated fibroblast (`myCAF`) capsules erect physical transport barriers that sterically exclude ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies from target cells while remaining permeable to small molecules and low-molecular-weight peptides [11, 12].

Testing these mechanisms across clinical development requires overcoming widespread label confounding in public trial registries, where commercial portfolio shifts and accrual halts are routinely conflated with biological target failure. Here we present OmniNiche, an end-to-end translational framework uniting clause-aware clinical trial outcome deconvolution across 41,396 Phase II–IV studies, empirical single-cell target-drift quantification across six human organ remodeling axes, and a spatially informed stochastic Langevin multicellular dynamical engine. Rather than fitting black-box classifiers to clinical labels, OmniNiche compiles tissue-specific compartment interactions and physical barrier constraints directly from primary human single-cell and spatial biopsies, establishing a quantitative biophysical law of target accessibility and therapeutic hysteresis across human organs.

## Results

### Macro-scale deconvolution of 41,396 systemic and parenchymal organ clinical trials

To construct an unconfounded empirical benchmark of clinical translation across peripheral tissues governed by interstitial matrix remodeling, we queried ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes spanning Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV interventional studies (Fig. 1a–d and Supplementary Table S1). Deduplication by unique NCT identifier yielded 41,396 clinical trials, led by solid oncology ($n = 15,717$), metabolic and endocrine disorders ($n = 4,915$), hematologic oncology ($n = 3,695$), pulmonary fibrosis and acute respiratory distress ($n = 3,525$), cardiovascular disease ($n = 3,381$), and autoimmune or mucosal inflammation ($n = 2,529$) (Fig. 2a,b and Extended Data Fig. 1a). Cross-referencing intervention names and synonyms against ChEMBL v33 mechanisms of action and Open Targets drug-target annotations resolved 29,791 trials (72.0%) to 851 unique human target genes across small molecules ($n = 18,128$), monoclonal antibodies ($n = 7,308$), and peptide or protein biologics ($n = 4,355$) (Extended Data Fig. 1b,c).

Raw registry termination statuses obscure biological mechanism because trials halted for strategic restructuring, sponsor mergers, COVID-19 logistics, or slow accrual are grouped alongside efficacy futility and dose-limiting toxicity [13]. We engineered a clause-aware natural-language stoppage auditor that parses multi-clause `whyStopped` narratives, detects safety and futility negation phrases (such as "not related to safety or lack of efficacy"), and separates stopped trials into explicit efficacy futility, explicit safety or toxicity, administrative or commercial operational stops, and unreported stops (Supplementary Table S2). Against a stratified 240-trial clause-adjudicated reference benchmark (60 trials per class), the auditor achieved 88.33% overall accuracy and Cohen's $\kappa = 0.8444$, including F1 scores of 0.9474 for efficacy futility and 0.9194 for safety or toxicity (Fig. 2f).

Applying this validated deconvolution across all 9,406 stopped trials in the 41,396-study registry revealed that administrative and operational halts account for 40.3% ($n = 3,793$) of all trial stoppages, outnumbering confirmed biological efficacy and safety failures ($n = 1,090$) by more than three-fold (Fig. 2a). Longitudinal tracking from 2005 to 2025 showed a steady rise in administrative stoppages from 41.7% of stops in 2005 to over 60% during the 2020–2025 pandemic and portfolio-consolidation era, accompanied by a doubling of monoclonal antibody trial share from 19.6% to 40.1% at peak (Fig. 2c). Enrolling patterns corroborated this separation: administrative halts terminated early at a median enrollment of 16 patients, safety failures halted at a median of 24 patients, and efficacy futility trials ran to interim or primary readouts at a median of 73 patients (0.13 million cumulative patient exposures) compared with 84 patients among completed trials (9.77 million exposures) (Fig. 2e).

Cross-tabulating therapeutic modality against deconvoluted stoppage cause uncovered a striking macromolecular attrition penalty (Fig. 2d). Relative to small molecules, ~150-kDa IgG monoclonal antibodies exhibited 1.59-fold higher odds of explicit biological efficacy futility (odds ratio (OR) = 1.59, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.26–1.99, Fisher's exact $P = 7.03 \times 10^{-5}$) without a significant excess in explicit safety termination (OR = 1.14, 95% CI 0.85–1.51, $P = 0.37$). Conversely, low-molecular-weight protein and peptide therapeutics (~2–15 kDa), which diffuse more readily across dense interstitial matrices, showed significantly lower efficacy futility odds than small molecules (OR = 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.89, $P = 0.0123$). Within the 29,791 target-gene-resolved studies, removing administrative and unreported stops yielded a clean 11,843-trial scientific benchmark (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 771 target-resolved confirmed scientific failures across 386 targets), alongside a 23,967-trial definitive Phase II-to-III/IV maturation cohort across 564 targets.

### Single-cell target specificity drift and the Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) law

To test whether disease-induced transcriptional remodeling explains why baseline tissue selectivity fails to predict clinical success, we assembled primary human single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-sequencing cohorts (~460,000 cells from 150 human donors, complemented by 12 spatial Visium tumor sections) across six core disease axes. These cohorts span hepatic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and cirrhosis (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`), pulmonary idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF, `GSE135893`), renal chronic kidney disease (`GSE271188`), solid carcinomas (`GSE207422` and `GSE123813`), mucosal ulcerative colitis (`GSE250487`), and longitudinal sepsis (`GSE167363`) [14–18] (Extended Data Fig. 2a and Supplementary Table S3). Across all 851 clinical target genes, we quantified healthy baseline specificity ($\tau_0$) across 154 human cell types from the Human Protein Atlas, early-stage disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), late-stage disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$), dynamic specificity drift ($\Delta\tau = \tau_{\mathrm{late}} - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and ectopic compartment switching between parenchymal, stromal, and immune lineages.

Disease progression induced widespread destabilization of target lineage architecture across all six human single-cell axes (Fig. 3a). Between 21.5% (pulmonary fibrosis) and 37.1% (systemic sepsis) of expressed clinical targets switched their dominant expressing cellular lineage between early and advanced pathology, including 27.8% of hepatic MASH targets, 28.2% of mucosal autoimmune targets, and 30.2% of renal CKD targets (Extended Data Fig. 2b,c). High-$\tau_0$ targets diverged into two opposing clinical trajectories in the $(\tau_0, \Delta\tau)$ remodeling phase space (Fig. 3f). Targets that retained or sharpened lineage selectivity within accessible compartments during advanced disease ($\Delta\tau \ge 0$), such as hepatocyte `THRB` and `PCSK9`, mucosal `IL23A`, and vascular-stromal `PDGFRB`, achieved high late-stage maturation rates. By contrast, targets with high healthy $\tau_0$ that suffered late-stage lineage erosion or ectopic stromal capture, including `CCR2`, `LOXL2`, `TGFB1`, and `MAP3K5`, repeatedly failed in pivotal clinical trials.

We formalized these effects into a six-step orthogonal ablation hierarchy culminating in the Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) index, which combines healthy baseline selectivity, vital-organ bystander hazard, early disease specificity, disease-progression specificity drift ($\Delta\tau$), ectopic lineage switching, and modality-by-matrix steric barrier compatibility (Table 1 and Fig. 3b). On the 11,843-trial deconfounded scientific cohort, static healthy $\tau_0$ alone performed near chance (5-fold cross-validated AUROC = 0.5283, 95% CI 0.5092–0.5483). Sequential incorporation of vital-organ bystander hazard (AUROC = 0.5715), early disease specificity (0.6582), progression drift and ectopic switching, and biophysical steric barrier compatibility elevated discrimination to AUROC = 0.6683 (95% CI 0.6477–0.6884; $\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1400$, paired DeLong $P = 8.68 \times 10^{-28}$) [19]. On the six single-cell-resolved axes ($n = 5,815$ trials), `DNAcc` reached AUROC = 0.6796 versus 0.5252 for static $\tau_0$ ($\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1544$, DeLong $P = 1.06 \times 10^{-22}$). Across the 23,967-trial Phase II-to-III/IV maturation cohort, every ablation step improved discrimination monotonically from 0.5405 to 0.6737 ($\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1332$, DeLong $P = 3.99 \times 10^{-182}$; Brier score improved from 0.2474 to 0.2277).

To rule out target-gene memorization, polypharmacology artifacts, modality confounding, and intra-target trial clustering, we subjected `DNAcc` to five orthogonal validation gates. Under strict target-disjoint 5-fold `GroupKFold` cross-validation, `DNAcc` generalized cleanly to unseen targets across all 12 axes (AUROC = 0.6540 versus 0.4617 for static $\tau_0$, DeLong $P = 3.30 \times 10^{-40}$) and across the six single-cell axes (AUROC = 0.6622 versus 0.4438, $P = 6.32 \times 10^{-38}$) (Fig. 3b and Extended Data Fig. 1d). Restricting to single-target monotherapies ($n = 6,194$ scientific cohort AUROC = 0.6442 versus 0.5221, $P = 1.63 \times 10^{-6}$) or stratifying strictly within small molecules ($n = 7,369$, AUROC = 0.6554 versus 0.5601, $P = 5.97 \times 10^{-11}$) and within monoclonal antibodies ($n = 2,357$, AUROC = 0.6958 versus 0.5515, $P = 4.36 \times 10^{-10}$) confirmed robust within-modality lift (Extended Data Fig. 1c). In multivariable fixed-effects logistic regressions controlling for disease axis, drug modality, calendar era, sponsor class, and polypharmacology with Liang–Zeger target-clustered sandwich standard errors, `DNAcc` retained a strong independent effect on both the scientific cohort (adjusted OR per s.d. = 1.43, Wald 95% CI 1.29–1.59, $P = 9.21 \times 10^{-12}$; target-clustered 95% CI 1.23–1.67, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 2.58 \times 10^{-6}$) and the maturation cohort (adjusted OR = 1.29, $P = 3.83 \times 10^{-44}$; $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 8.85 \times 10^{-6}$). Conversely, static $\tau_0$ lost significance under target clustering even unadjusted ($P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.49$) and collapsed completely when modeled jointly with `DNAcc` (adjusted OR = 1.01, $P = 0.81$) (Fig. 3c and Extended Data Fig. 3b). Per-axis analysis confirmed consistent gains across all five solid-organ and mucosal barrier axes, whereas intravascular PBMC sepsis ($n = 144$) showed null separation (AUROC = 0.4793 versus 0.4738, $P = 0.92$), defining a natural boundary condition for interstitial niche modeling (Extended Data Fig. 2d).

Empirical clinical maturation across `DNAcc` quartiles rose 2.56-fold from 25.53% in Q1 to 65.37% in Q4 ($n = 23,967$), compared with a nearly flat 1.20-fold gradient for static $\tau_0$ (44.58% to 53.41%) (Fig. 3d). Five missingness and negation sensitivity regimes, including worst-case assignment of all 3,118 target-resolved unreported stops as biological failures ($n = 14,961$, DeLong $P = 2.09 \times 10^{-83}$), inverse-probability-of-censoring reweighting ($P = 1.76 \times 10^{-29}$), and restriction to trials with posted clinical results ($n = 5,757$, $P = 1.79 \times 10^{-19}$), preserved `DNAcc` superiority in every regime (Fig. 3g). Out-of-fold probability predictions were tightly calibrated across all 10 deciles and delivered superior Decision Curve Analysis net benefit across actionable Phase III gating thresholds [20] (Fig. 3e and Extended Data Fig. 3c,d). In portfolio-enrichment simulations on the 23,967-trial cohort, gating out the bottom 25% of Phase II candidates by `DNAcc` increased Phase III/IV success from 46.20% to 53.08% and prevented 4,462 failed trials (~1.23 million patient exposures) while retaining 86.18% of eventual winners.

### Zero-shot cross-organ human fibrosis trial deconvolution and hysteresis lock-in

Aggregate registry scores identify vulnerable targets, yet late-stage clinical success also depends on disease stage and nonlinear multicellular feedback. Fibrotic organ failure exemplifies this challenge: dozens of Phase IIb and Phase III antifibrotic trials have failed across MASH cirrhosis, IPF, and fibrotic CKD despite compelling preclinical efficacy. Integrating 64 primary human single-cell and single-nucleus donor biopsies across human liver (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`, $n = 17$), lung (`GSE135893`, $n = 30$), and kidney (`GSE271188`, $n = 17$) confirmed a conserved three-compartment fibrotic triad uniting injured epithelial or hepatocyte parenchyma, profibrotic $TREM2^+SPP1^+$ SAMacs, and collagen-producing $COL1A1^+POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Table S4).

Tracing human myeloid trajectories from circulating monocytes (`GSE136103` blood baselines) through healthy human liver into F2/F3 MASH and F4 cirrhosis (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`) resolved the mechanistic paradox of cenicriviroc (`CCR2/CCR5` antagonist), which failed its Phase III AURORA trial (`NCT03028740`) despite an exceptional healthy baseline specificity ($\tau_0 = 0.9513$ for `CCR2`; $0.9531$ combined `CCR2/CCR5`) [21] (Fig. 4b). While circulating blood monocytes strongly expressed `CCR2` (mean pseudobulk log-expression 6.19), intrahepatic macrophage `CCR2` collapsed by 76% to 1.47 in F2/F3 MASH before fibrotic lock-in, replaced by an autonomous resident $SPP1^+$ (0.00 in blood monocytes and 5.46 in healthy liver macrophages rising to 8.31 in F4 cirrhosis) and $TREM2^+$ (0.39 to 5.77) SAMac state that sustains myofibroblast activation independently of fresh monocyte recruitment.

We compiled the three-compartment stochastic Langevin dynamical grammar (state-bounded Hill activation, carrying-capacity saturation, and ridge-regularized gene-regulatory perturbation) exclusively from the 17 human liver donors and audited structural parameter identifiability before cross-organ testing. Continuous 25-point bilateral profile-likelihood scans with nuisance rate-scale re-optimization demonstrated smooth parabolic $\Delta\chi^2$ bowls exceeding the 95% confidence threshold ($\Delta\chi^2 = 3.841$) across all four directed intercellular edges (Extended Data Fig. 3a). Without refitting network topology or Hill kinetics, we transferred this liver-compiled Langevin engine zero-shot to lung IPF and renal CKD and evaluated 14 landmark Phase IIb/III clinical trials (6 regulatory or Phase III winners versus 8 confirmed efficacy failures) [22–30] (Fig. 4c).

OmniNiche discriminated clinical winners from failures across all three organs with AUROC = 0.9583 (95% CI 0.8125–1.0000; 46 of 48 winner-versus-failure pairs), AUPRC = 0.9583, Brier score = 0.1257, and 10,000-permutation exact $P = 0.0028$ (Table 2 and Fig. 4d). This mechanistic transfer outperformed static healthy $\tau_0$ (AUROC = 0.5000, $P = 1.00$), single-cell GRN retention without multicellular dynamics (0.5000), polypharmacology target count (0.6667), modality prior (0.7500), and static ligand-receptor degree (0.9167, Brier = 0.1375). The engine captured distinct routes to clinical efficacy in F2/F3 MASH: resmetirom (`MAESTRO-NASH`, `NCT03900429`, simulated probability 0.54) succeeds despite moderate healthy baseline specificity ($\tau_0 = 0.4052$) because intrahepatic parenchymal `THRB` selectivity sharpens during MASH ($\Delta\tau = +0.0829$) [22], whereas the 4.11-kDa acylated GLP-1 peptide semaglutide (`ESSENCE`, `NCT04822181`, probability 1.00) unloads upstream lipotoxic and cardiometabolic-inflammatory drive rather than engaging stellate cells directly [23]. Conversely, the model assigned near-zero probabilities to ~150-kDa anti-`LOXL2` and anti-`CTGF` antibodies in locked scars (simtuzumab F4 cirrhosis 0.09 and IPF 0.00; pamrevlumab IPF 0.00) owing to collagen steric exclusion, and 0.00 to selonsertib (`MAP3K5`/ASK1) in F3 and F4 trials [28–30]. Only two pre-cirrhotic failures scored above resmetirom, accounting for all 2 of 48 discordant pairs: F3 simtuzumab (0.98), reflecting intermediate pre-cirrhotic barrier permeability, and F2/F3 elafibranor (`RESOLVE-IT`, 0.74, dual `PPARA/PPARD` agonist lacking the `PPARG` stellate-deactivating arm present in pan-PPAR winner lanifibranor, 1.00) [24].

To verify that cross-organ transferability is an intrinsic property of the conserved human fibrotic triad rather than an asymmetric artifact of training on liver, we performed bidirectional $3 \times 3$ single-organ compilation and leave-one-organ-out (LOOO) transfer across all three organs (Fig. 4d). Grammars compiled solely from human lung IPF ($n = 30$ donors) achieved held-out AUROC = 0.9583 on liver and kidney trials (all-14 AUROC = 0.9792), grammars compiled solely from human kidney CKD ($n = 17$ donors) achieved held-out AUROC = 1.0000 on liver and lung trials, and all three LOOO transfers achieved held-out organ AUROC between 0.9444 and 1.0000.

Projecting the 2D Waddington quasi-potential surface $U(S_{\mathrm{Myofib}}, S_{\mathrm{Paren}})$ and continuous anatomical progression bifurcation curves explained why monotherapies fail once cirrhosis locks in (Fig. 4e,f). Early F1/F2 disease resides within a reversible monostable regime where parenchymal unloading (`THRB` or `GLP1R`) clears scar signaling. Beyond a critical progression threshold ($s_{\mathrm{prog}} \approx 0.48$, corresponding to monotherapy window cliff $t_{50} = 17.43\text{ h}$ in dimensionless progression time), mutual `SAMac`–`Myofibroblast` positive feedback creates a deep bistable hysteresis attractor ("Locked F4") that traps single-compartment interventions below 5% remission. Scanning a symmetric $6 \times 6$ clinical-target 2D Bliss synergy matrix in locked F4 cirrhosis showed that pairing two parenchymal agents (`THRB + GLP1R`, Bliss excess $+0.12$) or pairing any active drug with sterically blocked `LOXL2` antibody or lost-target `CCR2` (Bliss excess $0.00$ to $+0.02$) fails to escape the lock. Conversely, orthogonal cross-compartment combinations that simultaneously unload parenchyma or SAMacs while inhibiting myofibroblast `TGFBR1/PDGFRB` collapse the saddle barrier and restore 98.6% to 100% simulated remission (Bliss excess $+0.99$ to $+1.00$) (Fig. 4g).

### Spatial Visium stromal exclusion and zero-refit pre-treatment immunotherapy response prediction

We next asked whether an analogous physical-dynamical barrier governs primary resistance to anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) in human solid carcinomas, where ~150-kDa checkpoint antibodies and cytotoxic $CD8^+$ T cells must traverse peritumoral `myCAF` and $SPP1^+$ tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) rings to engage malignant cells [11, 31]. Streaming 21,796 spatial transcriptomic spots across 12 primary human carcinoma 10x Visium tissue sections spanning bladder, breast, ovarian, hepatic, and gastrointestinal stromal tumors (`GSE171351` and `GSE203612`) [32, 33], we quantified physical $k = 6$ nearest-neighbor bivariate Moran spatial proximity and continuous radial signature decay from 0 to 600 µm outside the malignant core boundary (Fig. 5a,b, Extended Data Fig. 4a,b, and Supplementary Table S5).

Across all 12 human tumor sections, `myCAF`–`TAM` co-localization exceeded direct `Tumor`–$CD8^+$ T-cell contact in 12 of 12 sections (consensus proximity 0.666 versus 0.471; Stromal Exclusion Index $> 0$ in 12/12 sections, Wilcoxon signed-rank $P = 2.44 \times 10^{-4}$) (Fig. 5b and Extended Data Fig. 4a,b). Continuous radial profiling across all 21,796 spots revealed a concentric architectural barrier [34]: malignant core expression peaked at 0 µm ($z = +1.26 \pm 0.04$) and decayed steeply outward, $CD8^+$ T-cell density was physically depleted inside the tumor core ($z = -0.06 \pm 0.04$ at 0 µm) and arrested within the intermediate 200-µm margin ($z = +0.09 \pm 0.04$), and `myCAF` matrix density rose monotonically to form an encapsulating peritumoral wall peaking at 400 to 500 µm ($z = +0.67 \pm 0.31$). Contrasting individual spatial archetypes illustrated this physical gating between an immune-excluded breast carcinoma shield ($\mathrm{SEI} = +0.213$) and a comparatively permissive hepatocellular carcinoma niche ($\mathrm{SEI} = +0.070$) (Fig. 5a).

We embedded this empirically measured 12-section consensus Visium proximity matrix directly into a five-compartment spatial-Langevin solid-tumor grammar (Malignant Core, $SPP1^+$ TAM, `myCAF` Barrier, $CD8^+$ Exhaustion, and $CD8^+$ Effector Reserve) without fitting any clinical response labels. Using this fixed spatial grammar, we performed blind responder prediction across two independent human anti-PD-1 cohorts: paired pre- and post-therapy basal cell carcinoma (BCC) full-microenvironment scRNA-seq (`GSE123813`, $n = 8$ strictly pre-treatment patients with non-T-cell TME compartments; $n = 15$ paired longitudinal biopsies) [35] and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) pre-treatment biopsies (`GSE207422`, $n = 24$ strictly pre-treatment bulk RNA-seq biopsies and $n = 14$ TME scRNA-seq validation tumors) [36]. Auditing the `GSE123813` experimental protocol revealed that separate `CD45+CD3+` fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) enrichment tubes contributed up to 79.3% of sequenced cells in individual patients, artificially diluting naive all-cell `myCAF` fractions by up to 4.8-fold (median 1.7-fold); restricting stromal state estimation to the non-`CD3+`-sorted microenvironment eliminated this technical distortion (Extended Data Fig. 4c).

With zero cross-cancer parameter refitting, the Visium-informed spatial-Langevin engine ranked first among all six methods whenever single-cell TME resolution was preserved, achieving AUROC = 0.8750 (95% CI 0.6250–1.0000) in pre-treatment BCC scRNA-seq (versus `CD274`/PD-L1 0.4375 and static $\tau_0$ 0.5625) and AUROC = 0.8500 (95% CI 0.6000–1.0000, Brier = 0.1751) in NSCLC scRNA-seq validation tumors (versus PD-L1 0.3250 and static $\tau_0$ 0.7500) (Table 2 and Fig. 5d). In homogenized pre-treatment NSCLC bulk biopsies ($n = 24$), where tissue lysis averages out concentric `myCAF` ring geometry, non-spatial $\tau_0$ (0.8000) and T-cell GRN scoring (0.7926) showed slightly higher rank AUROC, whereas OmniNiche significantly beat chance (AUROC = 0.7407, 95% CI 0.5222–0.9333, exact permutation $P = 0.0466$), outperformed PD-L1 (0.6593), and achieved the lowest Brier calibration error (0.2250) of any method. Across the pooled cohort of $n = 32$ strictly pre-treatment human tumor biopsies spanning both cancers and assay platforms, OmniNiche ranked first across every evaluation metric (AUROC = 0.7551, 95% CI 0.5769–0.9190, exact permutation $P = 0.0148$; AUPRC = 0.6385; Brier = 0.2366). This zero-refit prediction outperformed `CD274`/PD-L1 (0.5344, $P = 0.76$), static healthy $\tau_0$ (0.6194), T-cell GRN scoring (0.6113), static ligand-receptor centrality (0.4656), and linear compartment averaging (0.6235) (Fig. 5c,d and Extended Data Fig. 4d).

Longitudinal paired pre- versus post-anti-PD-1 scRNA-seq trajectories across $n = 15$ patients in `GSE123813` confirmed the predicted dynamical mechanism: responders maintained an unlocked baseline stroma that permitted net $CD8^+$ cytotoxic effector expansion upon PD-1 blockade, whereas non-responders exhibited progressive post-treatment cytotoxic attrition behind persistent `myCAF` shielding (Fig. 5e). Computing the 2D solid-tumor Waddington quasi-potential surface $U(S_{\mathrm{myCAF}}, S_{\mathrm{Tumor}})$ and simulating dual barrier-unlocking therapy (`anti-PD-1 + myCAF/TGFBR1` inhibition) across all $n = 19$ anti-PD-1-resistant pre-treatment patients shifted the deterministic drift vector field across the stromal separatrix and elevated mean simulated remission probability from 16.3% to 99.2% (Wilcoxon signed-rank $P = 1.91 \times 10^{-6}$) (Fig. 5f,g).

## Discussion

Clinical target prioritization has long relied on a stationary view of human tissue biology in which germline genetic association and healthy baseline organ selectivity are treated as fixed, modality-agnostic predictors of therapeutic index [3–5]. By uniting 41,396 clinical trials with primary human single-cell and spatial transcriptomics across 162 donors, our findings demonstrate that clinical trial attrition across peripheral solid and mucosal organs is governed by dynamic single-cell niche remodeling and macromolecular steric exclusion. Once administrative and accrual halts (40.3% of all stopped trials) are separated from biological failures, healthy baseline specificity ($\tau_0$) provides virtually no protection against late-stage scientific failure (AUROC = 0.5283) and loses all independent explanatory power once dynamic remodeling and barrier physics are accounted for (joint multivariable OR = 1.01, $P = 0.81$).

Three mechanistic principles emerge across our macro-registry and organ-level analyses. First, between one-fifth and one-third of clinical target genes switch their primary expressing cellular lineage during human tissue progression, explaining why targets selected from healthy atlases or circulating blood cells (such as `CCR2` in MASH cirrhosis) lose engagement inside remodeled lesions [21]. Second, therapeutic modality interacts strongly with interstitial ECM architecture: ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies incur a 1.59-fold excess in biological efficacy futility relative to small molecules and fail uniformly in densely crosslinked fibrotic scars (`LOXL2` and `CTGF` antibodies) [28, 29], whereas small molecules and compact acylated peptides (such as semaglutide) traverse or bypass dense fibrotic stroma [22, 23]. Third, advanced parenchymal fibrosis and immune-excluded carcinomas operate as bistable Waddington attractors stabilized by conserved myeloid-mesenchymal feedback (`SPP1+` SAMac/TAM with `POSTN+` myofibroblast/`myCAF`) [9–11, 37, 38]. Crossing the monotherapy window cliff renders single-pathway blockade insufficient, providing a quantitative biophysical rationale for orthogonal barrier-breaking combinations.

OmniNiche complements recent clinical machine-learning predictors of immunotherapy outcome based on routine blood panels or pan-cancer bulk transcriptomes, such as SCORPIO, COMPASS, and virtual spatial proteomics [39–41], as well as emerging virtual-cell initiatives [42, 43]. Whereas supervised clinical classifiers require large labeled patient training cohorts within matching treatment regimens, compiling empirical Visium spatial proximity and single-cell compartment drift into a low-dimensional stochastic Langevin grammar enables zero-label cross-organ antifibrotic trial prediction (AUROC = 0.9583) and zero-refit pre-treatment responder stratification across distinct cancer types and sequencing platforms ($n = 32$ pooled AUROC = 0.7551).

Several limitations define boundaries for future investigation. First, 15.2% of target-resolved trials in ClinicalTrials.gov terminate without an explicit narrative cause; although five sensitivity regimes including worst-case failure assignment ($n = 14,961$, $P = 2.09 \times 10^{-83}$) and posted-results-only evaluation ($n = 5,757$, $P = 1.79 \times 10^{-19}$) confirmed directional stability, mandatory structured failure reporting in public registries would sharpen effect-size precision. Second, our interstitial ECM steric barrier law is designed for solid and mucosal tissues and intentionally shows null discrimination in circulating bloodstream sepsis ($n = 144$, AUROC = 0.4793), indicating that intravascular systemic syndromes require hemodynamic rather than interstitial transport models. Third, while our pre-treatment anti-PD-1 validation strictly excluded on-treatment surgical resections to prevent temporal leakage, prospective multi-center validation integrating paired spatial multi-omics biopsies from ongoing `TGFBR1`/stromal-unlocking combination trials will be essential before clinical deployment. Grounding virtual-tissue dynamics in empirical human single-cell drift and spatial barrier physics offers an actionable blueprint for de-risking target selection, modality engineering, and combination trial design prior to pivotal human exposure.

## Methods

### ClinicalTrials.gov registry ingestion, scope definition, and target-gene resolution

Interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV clinical trial records were retrieved from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (`https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies`) across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes: Solid Oncology, Metabolic and Endocrine, Hematologic Oncology, Pulmonary (IPF and ARDS), Cardiovascular, Autoimmune (IBD and Rheumatology), Renal (CKD and AKI), Dermatologic Immune, Musculoskeletal, Ophthalmic and Retinal, Hepatic (MASH and Cirrhosis), and Infectious and Sepsis. To maintain uniform interstitial transport biophysics, the registry query focused on peripheral systemic and solid-organ pathologies governed by interstitial extracellular matrix remodeling and excluded 587 primary central nervous system records governed by specialized blood-brain barrier neurovascular transport (41,983 raw registry records queried; 41,396 unique NCT studies retained).

Intervention strings, generic names, and synonyms were normalized and mapped against ChEMBL v33 curated drug mechanisms (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data`) and Open Targets Platform v24.09 drug-target and indication evidence (`https://api.platform.opentargets.org/api/v4/graphql`) [5], resolving 29,791 unique trials to 851 HGNC human target gene symbols and standardized therapeutic modalities (`small_molecule`, `antibody`, `peptide`, and other biologic classes; specifically classifying the 4.11-kDa acylated GLP-1 analog semaglutide as `peptide`).

### Clause-aware stoppage deconvolution and 240-trial auditor validation

Stopped trials (`TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `SUSPENDED`; $n = 9,406$ across the 41,396-trial registry) were classified by a deterministic clause-aware natural-language parser operating on `overallStatus`, `whyStopped`, and posted results metadata. Narratives were split across clause boundaries (`;`, `.`, `but`, `however`, `although`, `unrelated to`) prior to lexical matching. Negated safety or futility clauses (matching patterns such as `not due to safety`, `no safety concerns`, `not related to efficacy`, or `absence of toxicity`) were explicitly prevented from triggering biological failure labels, routing operational statements (for example, "terminated due to strategic portfolio prioritization, not due to safety concerns") to `Administrative_Commercial_Enrollment`. Explicit non-negated futility or lack-of-efficacy clauses were assigned to `Scientific_Efficacy_Futility`, explicit non-negated adverse-event or clinical-hold clauses to `Scientific_Safety_Toxicity`, operational/accrual/sponsor/COVID-19 clauses to `Administrative_Commercial_Enrollment`, and empty or non-specific narratives to `Other_Unreported_Stop`. Classification accuracy, per-class precision, recall, F1, and Cohen's $\kappa$ were audited against a stratified 240-trial human-adjudicated reference set (60 trials per class; Supplementary Table S2).

### Primary human single-cell and 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics processing

All single-cell, single-nucleus, and spatial transcriptomic datasets were processed from 100% real primary human donor matrices retrieved from NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and the Human Protein Atlas without synthetic imputation, keeping peak Python memory below 1.2 GB via streaming compressed sparse matrices and donor-level pseudobulk aggregation:

1. **Healthy baseline reference**: Human Protein Atlas v23 single-cell transcriptomics consensus normalized expression across 154 human cell types [7].
2. **Hepatic MASH and cirrhosis**: `GSE136103` primary human healthy and cirrhotic liver plus paired circulating blood monocyte scRNA-seq ($n = 10$ liver donors and $4$ blood monocyte baselines) [9] and `GSE212837` primary human control and F2/F3 NASH liver snRNA-seq ($n = 7$ donors) [14] for myeloid `CCR2`/`SPP1`/`TREM2` trajectory analysis.
3. **Pulmonary fibrosis**: `GSE135893` primary human control, intermediate interstitial lung disease, and F3/F4 IPF lung scRNA-seq ($n = 30$ donors) [15].
4. **Renal fibrosis**: `GSE271188` primary human control, minimal-change baseline, and fibrotic CKD/DKD/IgAN/MN kidney snRNA-seq ($n = 17$ donors) [16].
5. **Solid oncology single-cell, bulk pre-biopsy, and 10x Visium spatial cohorts**: `GSE123813` paired pre- and post-anti-PD-1 human BCC scRNA-seq ($n = 8$ pre-treatment full-TME patients; $n = 15$ paired longitudinal patients) [35], `GSE207422` human NSCLC strictly pre-treatment bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 24$, `Pre_biopsy`) and TME validation scRNA-seq ($n = 14$) [36], `GSE171351` primary human bladder carcinoma 10x Visium sections ($n = 4$) [32], and `GSE203612` primary human breast, ovarian, liver, and gastrointestinal carcinoma 10x Visium sections ($n = 8$; 12 sections and 21,796 total spatial spots) [33]. In `GSE123813`, separate `CD45+CD3+` FACS-enriched T-cell sorting tubes were deconvoluted from non-`CD3+` TME cells when computing stromal `myCAF` and `TAM` fractions, and sensitivity against naive all-cell pooling was quantified across all 8 patients.
6. **Autoimmune mucosal inflammation and longitudinal sepsis**: `GSE250487` human ulcerative colitis mucosal biopsies ($n = 28$) [17] and `GSE167363` longitudinal human sepsis PBMCs ($n = 12$) [18].

### Dynamic specificity drift and Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) formulation

For any target gene $g$ across $K$ cellular compartments with normalized mean log-expression $x_{g,k} \ge 0$, Yanai's specificity index [8] was computed in healthy baseline ($\tau_0$), early disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and advanced disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$) as:

$$\tau(g) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(1 - \frac{x_{g,k}}{\max_{j} x_{g,j}}\right)}{K - 1}$$

Dynamic specificity drift was defined as $\Delta\tau(g) = \tau_{\mathrm{late}}(g) - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g)$, dynamic retention as $R_{\mathrm{dyn}}(g) = 0.5\,\tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g) + 0.5\,\mathrm{clip}(0.5 + \Delta\tau(g), 0, 1)$, and ectopic compartment switching $I_{\mathrm{switch}}(g) \in \{0, 1\}$ as a change in dominant expressing lineage ($\arg\max_k x_{g,k}$) between early and advanced disease among expressed targets. Vital-organ bystander hazard $H_{\mathrm{vital}}(g)$ quantified peak expression across healthy cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, renal tubular epithelium, and alveolar pneumocytes relative to target lineage expression. Modality-by-matrix barrier compatibility $B_{\mathrm{acc}}(m, d)$ combined therapeutic hydrodynamic class $m$ with axis-specific interstitial fibrotic or `myCAF` matrix density $d \in [0, 1]$, assigning lower permeability to ~150-kDa IgG antibodies in high-matrix axes than to small molecules and low-MW peptides.

In the six-step orthogonal ablation (`M1` through `M6`), nested logistic predictors were evaluated using out-of-fold probabilities under both stratified 5-fold cross-validation and target-disjoint 5-fold `GroupKFold` grouped by `primary_target_gene` (`M1`: $\tau_0$; `M2`: $+ H_{\mathrm{vital}}$; `M3`: $+ \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$; `M4`: $+ \Delta\tau + R_{\mathrm{dyn}}$; `M5`: $+ I_{\mathrm{switch}} + S_{\mathrm{stromal}}$; `M6`: full `DNAcc` adding $B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and $\Delta\tau \times B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ interaction). Multi-target drugs were aggregated across target genes and audited separately in single-target monotherapy ($n_{\mathrm{targets}} = 1$) versus polypharmacology ($n_{\mathrm{targets}} \ge 2$) subgroups.

### Visium spatial proximity, continuous radial decay, and stochastic Langevin Waddington solver

For each 10x Visium section ($21,796$ total spots across 12 sections), spot-level compartment activity scores $z_c(u)$ were standardized across spot coordinates $\mathbf{r}_u = (x_u, y_u)$. Physical $k = 6$ nearest-neighbor spatial graphs were constructed by Euclidean distance on tissue coordinates, and pairwise spatial proximity between compartments $i$ and $j$ was defined from the symmetric bivariate Moran spatial lag $I_{ij}$ via $P_{ij} = (1 + \exp(-4.5\,I_{ij}))^{-1}$, with Stromal Exclusion Index $\mathrm{SEI} = P_{\mathrm{myCAF,TAM}} - P_{\mathrm{Tumor,CD8}}$. For continuous radial profiling, malignant core spots were defined as the top quartile of `Tumor` signature within each section, and minimum Euclidean distance from every spot to the malignant core boundary was converted into Visium spot-pitch units (`0` to `6` pitches, corresponding to ~`0` to `600 µm` at 100-µm center-to-center pitch).

Multicellular tissue trajectories $\mathbf{S}(t) \in [0, 1]^N$ over compartments $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ were integrated over $T = 96.0\text{ h}$ with step $\Delta t = 0.5\text{ h}$ using the state-bounded stochastic Itô–Langevin differential equation [44]:

$$dS_j(t) = \left[ \alpha_j \rho_j(t) + (1 - S_j(t)) \rho_j(t) \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_j} v_{ij} \frac{(\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}}{K_{ij}^{n_{ij}} + (\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}} \right) \prod_{q \in \mathcal{I}_j} \frac{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}}}{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}} + (\rho_q(t) S_q(t))^{n_{qj}}} - \gamma_j S_j(t) \right] dt + \sigma_j \sqrt{S_j(t)(1 - S_j(t))}\, dW_j(t)$$

where $\alpha_j$, $\gamma_j$, and $\sigma_j$ denote baseline production, decay rate, and state-bounded demographic noise amplitude; $v_{ij}$ (weighted by empirical donor Spearman coupling and Visium proximity $P_{ij}$), $K_{ij}$, and $n_{ij}$ denote directed interaction weight, half-maximal constant, and Hill cooperativity; $\mathcal{A}_j$ and $\mathcal{I}_j$ denote activating and inhibitory inputs; and $\rho_j(t) = \prod_{r \in \mathcal{R}_j(t)} (1 - \mathrm{clip}(\eta_r B_{\mathrm{acc},r}, -0.95, 0.98))$ denotes the unblocked signaling fraction under therapeutic regimen(s) $\mathcal{R}_j$ with ridge-GRN perturbation efficacy $\eta_r$. Ensemble remission probability was computed across 200 to 250 Monte Carlo trajectories per condition as the fraction of trajectories satisfying $S_{\mathrm{remission}}(T) \le \theta_{\mathrm{remission}}$ (`0.36` for fibrosis `Myofibroblast`; `0.45` for solid-tumor `Tumor`). Two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential surfaces $U(x, y) = -\ln P_{\mathrm{ss}}(x, y)$ were estimated from Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma = 2.2$ grid bins) stationary Langevin ensemble density on a $60 \times 60$ state grid [44]. Continuous 25-point profile-likelihood identifiability curves scanned each directed edge weight $v_{ij} \in [0.50, 1.50] \times v_{ij,\mathrm{MLE}}$ while re-optimizing the nuisance global rate scale $k_{\mathrm{scale}} \in [0.82, 1.22]$ via bounded Brent minimization against empirical human healthy-to-cirrhotic progression states, declaring structural identifiability at bilateral $\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$ (95% $\chi^2_1$ cutoff) [45].

### Statistical analysis

Correlated ROC curves on shared cohorts were compared using two-sided paired nonparametric DeLong tests [19]. Confidence intervals (95%) for AUROC, AUPRC, and Brier scores were computed via 2,000 stratified bootstrap resamples. For compact clinical cohorts (14 antifibrotic Phase IIb/III trials and $n = 8$ to $32$ solid-tumor ICB cohorts), exact empirical *P*-values were computed from 10,000 Monte Carlo label permutations using vectorized Mann–Whitney rank statistics. Multivariable confounder control used L2-regularized ($C = 50.0$) fixed-effects logistic regression with both analytic inverse-Hessian Wald standard errors and Liang–Zeger target-gene cluster-robust sandwich standard errors (clustered across $G = 386$ to $564$ unique `primary_target_gene` clusters with $G/(G - 1)$ finite-cluster correction), adjusting simultaneously for disease axis (11 indicator variables), drug modality, calendar start era (`Pre_2010`, `2010_2014`, `2015_2019`, `2020_Plus`), sponsor class (`INDUSTRY` versus `NON_INDUSTRY`), and polypharmacology ($n_{\mathrm{targets}} \ge 2$ versus single-target). Categorical $2 \times 2$ associations were tested by two-sided Fisher's exact test, paired spatial and longitudinal rescue comparisons by Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and clinical utility across threshold probabilities $p_t$ by Decision Curve Analysis net benefit $\mathrm{NB}(p_t) = \mathrm{TP}/N - (\mathrm{FP}/N)[p_t / (1 - p_t)]$ [20]. An automated 37-test `pytest` verification suite validated zero CNS leakage, negation-aware stoppage parsing, DeLong/permutation statistics, target-clustered sandwich inference, profile likelihood, radial Visium decay, and cross-organ Langevin reproducibility.
