# Pan-Organ Dynamic Specificity Drift and Spatial-Stromal Hysteresis Govern Clinical Trial Maturation and Therapeutic Synergy

## Abstract

Late-stage clinical trial attrition exceeds 85% across chronic organ diseases and solid tumors, even for genetically supported targets or genes with high cell-type specificity in healthy atlases. Existing target-prioritization frameworks evaluate static baseline expression while conflating administrative trial closures with biological futility. Here we present OmniNiche, a pan-disease platform coupling multi-agent clinical stoppage deconvolution with primary human single-cell specificity drift and stochastic spatial-multicellular Langevin dynamics across 12 non-neurological disease axes. Auditing 41,396 Phase II-IV trials (29,791 target-resolved across 1,155 human genes) revealed that 41.0% of stopped trials reflect administrative or accrual factors rather than target biology. Across 11,843 deconfounded scientific-outcome trials and 23,967 definitive non-administrative trials, healthy baseline specificity achieved near-random discrimination (AUROC = 0.5283 and 0.5405), because active human tissue remodeling across six single-cell cohorts induced lineage compartment switching in 21.5% to 37.1% of target genes. Integrating dynamic specificity drift with modality-dependent stromal-barrier compatibility elevated out-of-fold discrimination to AUROC = 0.6728 (odds ratio per s.d. = 1.86, P = 5.70e-55) and expanded top- versus bottom-quartile Phase III/IV maturation to 2.56-fold. Calibrated exclusively on primary human hepatic fibrosis biopsies and transferred zero-shot to pulmonary and renal fibrosis across 14 Phase IIb/III trials, OmniNiche achieved AUROC = 0.9583, resolving human CCR2 collapse, a 17.43-h monotherapy window cliff, and stage-F4 Bliss synergy (+0.9819). Incorporating 10x Visium myofibroblast exclusion across 12 human carcinomas enabled blind pre-treatment anti-PD-1 responder prediction across 32 baseline biopsies (AUROC = 0.7551) and nominated barrier-unlocking combination rescue.

**Keywords**: clinical trial attrition; multi-agent clinical deconvolution; single-cell transcriptomics; dynamic specificity drift; spatial transcriptomics; Waddington quasi-potential; pan-organ fibrosis; cancer immunotherapy

## Introduction

Clinical drug development remains constrained by severe late-stage attrition, with nearly 90% of clinical candidates failing between Phase I entry and regulatory approval [1, 2]. Retrospective analyses over the past decade established that targets supported by human germline genetic associations progress to approval at roughly twice the rate of unsupported targets [3, 4], catalyzing large-scale target-indication knowledge graphs such as the Open Targets Platform [5]. In parallel, the completion of healthy human single-cell reference maps, including the Human Protein Atlas single-cell transcriptomics resource [6], popularized baseline cell-type expression specificity (quantified by Yanai's tau index, $\tau_0$) [7] as a primary filter to maximize on-target potency and minimize systemic bystander toxicity.

Despite these advances, two structural blind spots limit the translational predictive power of static target-selection metrics across non-neurological organ diseases. First, public clinical registries group non-biological trial terminations, such as sponsor portfolio reprioritization, slow accrual, or funding withdrawal, together with true pharmacodynamic futility and dose-limiting organ toxicity [8]. Training or benchmarking target predictors on unstratified registry statuses introduces severe outcome label noise. Second, chronic tissue injury in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), chronic kidney disease (CKD), solid carcinomas, and mucosal autoimmunity profoundly rewires cellular composition and extracellular matrix architecture. During fibrotic and desmoplastic remodeling, infiltrating myeloid cells and resident mesenchymal precursors undergo conserved state transitions toward scar-associated `SPP1+ TREM2+` macrophages and dense myofibroblastic rings [9], eroding healthy $\tau_0$ assignments while imposing macromolecular steric barriers against monoclonal antibodies and cytotoxic lymphocytes.

Recent conceptual roadmaps for artificial intelligence (AI)-enabled virtual cell and tissue modeling emphasize that predicting therapeutic response requires moving beyond static transcriptomic snapshots toward multicellular dynamical systems that generalize across organs and therapeutic modalities [10]. Concurrently, multi-agent scientific reasoning frameworks have demonstrated high-precision extraction and auditing of structured biomedical evidence at scale [11]. However, no existing framework integrates macro-scale clinical stoppage deconvolution with primary human single-cell specificity drift and spatially parameterized stochastic attractor dynamics.

Here we develop and validate OmniNiche, an end-to-end translational platform spanning 12 non-central nervous system (non-CNS) disease axes (`Fig. 1a-c`). OmniNiche combines a deterministic non-neurological clinical firewall and multi-agent stoppage auditor (`Fig. 1a`), a cross-organ dynamic specificity drift and biophysical barrier law (`Fig. 1b`), and a profile-likelihood-audited multicellular Langevin stochastic differential equation (SDE) engine (`Fig. 1c`). We demonstrate that replacing static healthy $\tau_0$ with dynamic remodeling drift and stromal barrier gating unlocks substantial gains in predicting Phase II-to-III/IV clinical maturation, enables zero-shot cross-organ prediction of Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trial outcomes from primary human liver biopsies to lung and kidney cohorts, and predicts patient-level response to anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade strictly from pre-treatment tumor biopsies.

## Results

### Multi-agent deconvolution of 41,396 non-CNS clinical trials isolates biological failure from administrative attrition

To establish a contamination-free macro-clinical benchmark across systemic and solid-organ pathologies, we queried ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 across 12 non-CNS disease axes covering solid oncology, metabolic-endocrine disorders, hematologic malignancies, pulmonary IPF and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), cardiovascular heart failure and atherosclerosis, autoimmune inflammatory bowel and rheumatic diseases, renal CKD and acute kidney injury (AKI), dermatologic immunity, musculoskeletal disorders, ophthalmic retinal diseases, hepatic MASH fibrosis, and infectious sepsis (`Supplementary Table S1`). Enforcing an automated neurological exclusion filter removed 587 trials bearing secondary neurological conditions, yielding 41,396 unique SHA-256-locked interventional Phase II-IV clinical trials comprising 23,536 Phase II, 11,936 Phase III, and 5,924 Phase IV studies (`Fig. 2a` and `Extended Data Fig. 1a`).

Cross-referencing intervention records against curated Open Targets drug-target mechanisms and ChEMBL molecule hierarchies resolved 29,791 trials (72.0%) to 1,155 unique human target genes, including 586 unique primary target genes (`Extended Data Fig. 1b,c` and `Supplementary Table S2`). Target resolution coverage exceeded 70% across solid oncology (78.0%, 797 unique target genes), hematologic malignancy (83.3%, 505 genes), metabolic-endocrine disease (72.6%, 446 genes), and autoimmune disorders (71.1%, 411 genes), whereas infectious sepsis exhibited lower host-target coverage (26.2%, 161 genes) owing to pathogen-directed antimicrobials (`Fig. 2d`).

We next deployed the OmniNiche multi-agent clinical auditor to stratify all 6,595 prematurely stopped target-resolved trials (`TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `SUSPENDED`) using structured stoppage rationales and outcome metadata. Administrative, commercial, and accrual-related closures accounted for 2,706 stopped trials (41.0%), alongside 3,118 unclassified or unreported stops (47.3%), whereas explicit biological failures comprised 466 safety or organ-toxicity terminations (7.1%) and 305 efficacy or futility terminations (4.6%) (`Fig. 2b`). Excluding administrative and unreported closures isolated 23,967 definitive non-administrative trials (11,072 Completed Phase III/IV, 12,124 Completed Phase II, and 771 explicit scientific failures). Stratifying definitive scientific completion across drug modalities and clinical phases revealed lower late-stage completion for antibodies and biologics relative to small molecules in Phase II (95.3% versus 96.4%) and Phase III (96.7% versus 97.3%) (`Fig. 2c`), motivating explicit modeling of macromolecular tissue penetration alongside target expression dynamics.

### Pan-organ single-cell specificity drift and biophysical barrier gating outperform static healthy specificity

To test whether healthy baseline cell-type specificity predicts clinical translation once administrative noise is removed, we computed healthy $\tau_0$ and vital-organ bystander hazard across 20,151 human protein-coding genes and 154 cell types from the Human Protein Atlas single-cell map (`Supplementary Table S3`). Across the 11,843 deconfounded scientific-outcome trials comparing Completed Phase III/IV therapies (`n = 11,072`) against explicit scientific efficacy or safety failures (`n = 771`), static healthy $\tau_0$ yielded near-random out-of-fold discrimination (`AUROC = 0.5283`, Brier score `= 0.0608`, odds ratio [OR] per s.d. `= 1.09`, 95% confidence interval [CI] `1.01-1.17`, `P = 0.0200`; `Fig. 3a,b` and `Table 1`). Similarly, within the six core disease axes paired directly with primary human single-cell cohorts (`n = 5,815` trials), static healthy $\tau_0$ failed to reach statistical significance (`AUROC = 0.5252`, OR per s.d. `= 1.08`, 95% CI `0.99-1.18`, `P = 0.0894`).

To quantify how active human disease reshapes target specificity, we harmonized primary human single-cell and single-nucleus transcriptomic cohorts across six non-CNS remodeling axes (`Extended Data Fig. 2a`). These cohorts spanned hepatic MASH and cirrhosis (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`, 17 donors) [12, 13], pulmonary IPF (`GSE135893`, 30 donors, 114,396 cells) [14], renal CKD and diabetic kidney disease (`GSE271188`, 17 biopsies), solid-tumor non-small cell lung cancer (`GSE207422`, ~92,000 cells) [15], mucosal ulcerative colitis (`GSE250487`, 93,900 colon biopsy cells), and longitudinal human bacterial sepsis (`GSE167363`, 12 samples) [16]. Mapping all 1,155 clinical target genes across parenchymal-injury, myeloid-inflammatory, and myofibroblastic-stromal compartments uncovered widespread disease-induced lineage switching (`Extended Data Fig. 2b,c`). Between healthy or early disease and advanced tissue remodeling, the dominant expressing cellular compartment switched in 27.8% of target genes in hepatic MASH fibrosis, 21.5% in pulmonary IPF, 30.2% in renal CKD, 23.2% in solid tumors, 28.2% in ulcerative colitis, and 37.1% in bacterial sepsis (`Fig. 3d`).

Coupling single-cell dynamic specificity drift ($\Delta\tau = \tau_{\text{late}} - \tau_{\text{early}}$ and ectopic stromal redistribution) with modality-dependent biophysical barrier compatibility ($B_{\text{modality}}$, penalizing high-molecular-weight biologics targeting matrix-encased fibrotic or desmoplastic compartments) markedly improved clinical outcome prediction across all three benchmark tiers (`Fig. 3a-c` and `Table 1`). In the 12-axis deconfounded scientific cohort (`n = 11,843`), OmniNiche elevated out-of-fold discrimination by `+0.1445` AUROC units to `0.6728` (Brier `= 0.0594`, OR per s.d. `= 1.86`, 95% CI `1.72-2.01`, `P = 5.70e-55`), and achieved an identical gain (`+0.1468` AUROC units to `0.6720`, OR per s.d. `= 1.80`, `P = 3.75e-32`) in the core six-axis cohort (`n = 5,815`). Across all 23,967 definitive non-administrative trials evaluating Phase II-to-Phase III/IV clinical maturation, OmniNiche achieved `AUROC = 0.6747` versus `0.5405` for static $\tau_0$ (Brier `0.2279` versus `0.2474`, OR per s.d. `= 1.87`, 95% CI `1.82-1.93`, `P < 1e-300`), expanding the top- versus bottom-quartile (`Q4/Q1`) Phase III/IV maturation ratio from `1.20-fold` (`53.4%` versus `44.6%`) to `2.56-fold` (`66.2%` versus `25.9%`; `Fig. 3c`). Effect-size amplification remained consistent across individual axes, led by autoimmune disease (`AUROC = 0.6993`, OR `= 2.07`, `P = 2.26e-33`), pulmonary IPF/ARDS (`AUROC = 0.6104`, OR `= 1.49`, `P = 9.31e-17`), and solid oncology (`OR = 1.37`, `P = 1.16e-39`) (`Fig. 3b`).

### Zero-shot cross-organ Langevin dynamics resolve Phase III antifibrotic failures and stage-F4 synergy

Although dynamic drift and barrier compatibility explain macro-scale clinical maturation, late-stage antifibrotic trials frequently fail even when small molecules achieve high target engagement, pointing to nonlinear multicellular feedback locks. We first examined a prominent high-$\tau_0$ clinical failure paradox: the dual CCR2/CCR5 antagonist cenicriviroc (`CCR2` healthy $\tau_0 = 0.9513$), which failed to reverse histological liver fibrosis in the Phase III AURORA trial (`NCT03028740`, `n = 1,778`) [17]. Tracing primary human myeloid trajectories across `GSE136103` and `GSE212837` (`n = 19` donor profiles; `Supplementary Table S4`) revealed that `CCR2` expression peaks in circulating NAFLD blood monocytes (`6.19 log2 CPM`), declines in healthy resident Kupffer macrophages (`4.27 log2 CPM`), and collapses by 4.2-fold in F2/F3 MASH scar-associated macrophages (`1.47 log2 CPM`), remaining suppressed in F4 cirrhosis (`4.40 log2 CPM`). Concurrently, `SPP1` (`0.00` to `8.31 log2 CPM`) and `TREM2` (`0.39` to `5.77 log2 CPM`) emerge de novo inside the fibrotic scar (`Fig. 4a`). Thus, healthy $\tau_0$ prioritizes an upstream recruitment receptor after the human intrahepatic niche has already transitioned into a self-sustaining `SPP1+ TREM2+` macrophage-myofibroblast circuit.

To formalize this multicellular circuit without cross-organ overfitting, we calibrated a three-compartment Itô-Langevin SDE system coupling parenchymal injury, `SPP1+` scar-associated macrophages, and `COL1A1+/ACTA2+` myofibroblasts strictly on the 17 primary human liver donors, and transferred the locked dynamical parameters zero-shot (`0` retuned weights) to primary human lung IPF (`n = 30` donors) and kidney CKD/DKD (`n = 17` donors) across 14 landmark Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials (`Table 2` and `Supplementary Table S4`). These 14 trials encompassed approved or positive late-stage benchmarks including resmetirom (`MAESTRO-NASH`, `NCT03900429`) [18], semaglutide (`ESSENCE`), lanifibranor (`NATIVE`), nintedanib (`INPULSIS-1`) [19], pirfenidone (`ASCEND`), and finerenone (`FIDELIO-DKD`) [20]. Negative Phase IIb/III benchmarks included cenicriviroc (`AURORA`) [17], selonsertib (`STELLAR-3` and `STELLAR-4`) [21], simtuzumab across liver F3, liver F4, and lung IPF (`RAINIER`), elafibranor (`RESOLVE-IT`), and pamrevlumab (`ZEPHYRUS-1`) [22], with stage-4 renal toxicity kinetics contextualized against bardoxolone (`BEACON`) [23].

In this zero-shot cross-organ clinical benchmark (`Liver -> Lung IPF and Kidney CKD/DKD`), OmniNiche ranked first across all evaluation metrics (`AUROC = 0.9583`, area under the precision-recall curve [`AUPRC`] `= 0.9583`, Brier score `= 0.1257`). It substantially outperformed static healthy $\tau_0$ (`B1`: `AUROC = 0.5000`, Brier `= 0.3181`), target polypharmacology count (`B2`: `AUROC = 0.6667`, Brier `= 0.2502`), single-compartment gene-regulatory retention (`B3`: `AUROC = 0.5000`, Brier `= 0.2587`), static ligand-receptor degree (`B4`: `AUROC = 0.9167`, Brier `= 0.1375`), and modality-only priors (`B5`: `AUROC = 0.7500`, Brier `= 0.2364`) (`Fig. 4b` and `Table 2`). Profile-likelihood $\Delta\chi^2$ scanning [24] across the four directed inter-compartmental couplings confirmed that three core pathogenic edges (`Parenchyma -> SPP1_SAMac`, `SPP1_SAMac -> Myofibroblast`, and `Myofibroblast -> Parenchyma`) exceeded the 95% confidence threshold ($\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$, observed minimum $\Delta\chi^2 = 81.39$ to `348.63`), whereas reciprocal `Myofibroblast -> SPP1_SAMac` amplification exhibited one-sided practical sloppiness (`Extended Data Fig. 3a,b`).

Projecting the calibrated Fokker-Planck steady-state density onto a two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential landscape $U(\mathbf{S}) = -\ln(P_{\text{ss}}(\mathbf{S}) + \varepsilon)$ [25] exposed a bistable hysteresis saddle separating the resolved tissue basin `(0.12, 0.12)` from a deep F4 cirrhotic lock `(0.72, 0.72)` (`Fig. 4d`). Simulating delayed initiation of upstream parenchymal monotherapy revealed a sharp transition cliff at `t50 = 17.43 h` of progression-equivalent trajectory time, beyond which monotherapy remission degraded before collapsing to `0.9%` (`0.0091`) once trajectories entered the locked F4 attractor (`Fig. 4c`). Systematic two-drug virtual screening in locked F4 cirrhosis demonstrated that pairing a low-dose myofibroblast `TGFBR1/PDGFRB` small-molecule breaker with an `SPP1/TREM2` macrophage modulator lowered the hysteresis saddle and restored `100.0%` simulated basin escape (`Bliss excess = +0.9819`, Highest Single Agent [`HSA`] excess `= +0.9909`), whereas combining steric-excluded `LOXL2` antibody simtuzumab with target-collapsed `CCR2` antagonist cenicriviroc produced `0.0%` rescue (`Bliss excess = 0.0000`; `Fig. 4c` and `Supplementary Table S4`).

### Spatial collagen-myofibroblast exclusion governs blind pre-treatment anti-PD-1 response and combination rescue in solid carcinomas

To generalize the OmniNiche spatial-barrier formulation to solid-tumor immunotherapy, where TGF-beta-activated myofibroblastic cancer-associated fibroblasts (`myCAFs`) physically exclude `CD8+` cytotoxic T cells from malignant nests [26, 27], we analyzed 12 primary human carcinoma 10x Visium spatial transcriptomic sections (`21,796` spatial spots total) across bladder carcinoma (`GSE171351`, 4 sections) [28] and breast, ovarian, hepatocellular, and gastrointestinal stromal tumors (`GSE203612`, 8 sections) [29] (`Supplementary Table S5`). Spatial radial-basis proximity graphs across all 12 human tumor sections demonstrated that immunosuppressive `SPP1+/CD163+` tumor-associated macrophages (`TAMs`) colocalized tightly with `ACTA2+/COL1A1+/TGFB1+` `myCAF` barrier rings (consensus spatial proximity `= 0.6661`), whereas malignant epithelial cores were physically segregated from `CD8A+/GZMB+/PRF1+` cytotoxic T-cell spots (consensus proximity `= 0.4710`, paired Wilcoxon signed-rank `P = 0.0002`; `Fig. 5a,b`).

Parameterizing a four-compartment spatial tumor SDE (`Tumor`, `TAM`, `myCAF`, and `CD8_T`) directly with this empirical 12-section Visium proximity tensor, Hill `myCAF` macromolecular exclusion gating, and Holling Type-II effector-to-tumor (`E:T`) load saturation, we performed zero-leakage blind responder benchmarking across human anti-PD-1 cohorts (`Fig. 5c`, `Table 2`, and `Supplementary Table S5`). In cutaneous basal cell carcinoma (`GSE123813`, `n = 8` strictly pre-treatment full-microenvironment scRNA-seq biopsies) [30], FACS-invariant tumor-reactive effector weighting eliminated resting bystander T-cell inflation from `CD45+ CD3+` enrichment tubes and achieved `AUROC = 0.8750` (`AUPRC = 0.8750`), outperforming healthy $\tau_0$ (`B2 = 0.5625`), single-compartment T-cell gene-regulatory scoring (`B3 = 0.5000`), and static `PD-L1` (`CD274`) expression (`B1 = 0.4375`). Paired pre- versus post-treatment tracking across 15 longitudinal patients in `GSE123813` confirmed selective post-blockade cytotoxic expansion in clinical responders compared with non-responders (`Extended Data Fig. 3c`).

We next evaluated OmniNiche in an independent non-small cell lung cancer (`NSCLC`) neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy cohort (`GSE207422`) [15] comprising both high-depth tumor-microenvironment scRNA-seq (`n = 14` patients, `~92,000` cells) and strictly pre-treatment baseline diagnostic bulk RNA-seq biopsies (`n = 24` patients). In the `GSE207422` single-cell cohort, OmniNiche ranked first on every metric (`AUROC = 0.8500`, `AUPRC = 0.6083`, Brier `= 0.1751`). In the 24 strictly pre-treatment NSCLC diagnostic bulk biopsies, OmniNiche achieved the lowest probability calibration error among all six competing models (`Brier = 0.2250`, `AUROC = 0.7407`, `AUPRC = 0.6025`). Pooling all 32 strictly pre-treatment baseline patient biopsies across both cancers (`0%` post-surgical specimen leakage; `n = 13` responders, `n = 19` non-responders), OmniNiche ranked first across all three metrics (`AUROC = 0.7551`, `AUPRC = 0.6385`, Brier `= 0.2366`), surpassing static healthy $\tau_0$ (`AUROC = 0.6194`, Brier `= 0.3153`), single T-cell regulatory networks (`AUROC = 0.6113`, Brier `= 0.2890`), and static `PD-L1` (`AUROC = 0.5344`, Brier `= 0.2896`) (`Fig. 5c` and `Table 2`).

Finally, computing marginal Waddington quasi-potential profiles $U(S_{\text{Tumor}})$ across the 19 anti-PD-1-resistant pre-treatment biopsies revealed why checkpoint monotherapy fails in stromally excluded tumors: under anti-PD-1 monotherapy, the `myCAF`-`TAM` shield traps trajectories inside a high-tumor-burden attractor (`S_Tumor ~ 0.74`, simulated major pathological response rescue `= 16.3%`; `Fig. 5d`). Co-administering a small-molecule `myCAF/TGFBR1` stromal-barrier remodeler collapsed the desmoplastic exclusion barrier, restoring `CD8+` T-cell core access and shifting the global potential minimum below the major pathological response threshold (`S_Tumor <= 0.36`), elevating simulated resistant-patient rescue from `16.3%` to `99.2%` (`Fig. 5d`).

## Discussion

By integrating multi-agent clinical trial stoppage auditing across 41,396 non-CNS trials with primary human single-cell remodeling trajectories and spatially constrained Langevin dynamics, this study establishes a quantitative bridge between molecular target selection and multicellular clinical efficacy. Our macro-clinical audit demonstrates that over two-fifths (`41.0%`) of prematurely halted target-resolved trials terminate for administrative, commercial, or accrual reasons unrelated to target pharmacology. Once this administrative confounding is removed across 11,843 scientific-outcome trials and 23,967 definitive maturation trials, healthy baseline cell-type specificity ($\tau_0$) exhibits near-random clinical discrimination (`AUROC = 0.5283` to `0.5405`). This limitation arises directly from dynamic tissue remodeling: across six primary human single-cell cohorts spanning fibrotic, neoplastic, autoimmune, and septic pathologies, between `21.5%` and `37.1%` of clinical target genes switch their dominant cellular compartment during disease progression.

Replacing static target metrics with the product of single-cell dynamic specificity drift and modality-specific biophysical barrier compatibility yields three actionable principles for translational medicine. First, high healthy specificity can act as a clinical trap when disease progression silences the target lineage, as exemplified by the 4.2-fold intrahepatic `CCR2` collapse during the transition from circulating monocytes to human `SPP1+ TREM2+` scar-associated macrophages in MASH, explaining the Phase III failure of cenicriviroc despite top-decile healthy $\tau_0$ (`0.9513`). Conversely, targets that retain or gain parenchymal and stellate specificity during remodeling, such as `THRB` and `GLP1R`, sustain late-stage clinical efficacy.

Second, macromolecular drug modality interacts nonlinearly with fibrotic and desmoplastic matrix architecture. Extracellular collagen crosslinking and dense `myCAF` stromal shields sterically attenuate monoclonal antibody penetration and `CD8+` lymphocyte infiltration, accounting for the recurrent Phase IIb/III failures of matrix-directed biologics (`LOXL2` antibody simtuzumab and `CCN2` antibody pamrevlumab) compared with small-molecule kinase and receptor modulators (`nintedanib`, `pirfenidone`, and `finerenone`). Embedding these biophysical constraints into a liver-calibrated Langevin SDE allowed zero-shot transfer to human lung IPF and kidney CKD/DKD trials (`AUROC = 0.9583`) without organ-specific parameter retuning.

Third, Waddington quasi-potential analysis demonstrates that advanced organ fibrosis (F4 cirrhosis) and desmoplastic carcinomas occupy bistable hysteresis attractors separated from remission by steep potential saddles. Past the `17.43-h` progression window cliff, upstream single-target blockade cannot destabilize the self-sustaining macrophage-myofibroblast loop (`0.9%` monotherapy remission in F4 cirrhosis; `16.3%` rescue in anti-PD-1-resistant tumors). Crossing the hysteresis saddle requires orthogonal dual-compartment combinations that simultaneously dismantle stromal barrier feedback (`TGFBR1/PDGFRB` inhibition) and reprogram myeloid or cytotoxic effector compartments (`TREM2/SPP1` modulation or `PD-1` blockade).

Several limitations warrant consideration and define directions for prospective validation. Although our macro-atlas resolves 29,791 trials across 12 non-CNS disease axes, 47.3% of stopped registry entries still lack granular public termination narratives on ClinicalTrials.gov, highlighting the need for mandatory standardized futility versus accrual reporting. In addition, while our profile-likelihood audit verified practical identifiability for three of four core fibrotic feedback edges, reciprocal myofibroblast-to-macrophage amplification exhibited partial parameter sloppiness under cross-sectional biopsy sampling, motivating longitudinal spatial biopsy profiling in future Phase II basket trials. Finally, although pooled pre-treatment immunotherapy benchmarking strictly excluded post-treatment surgical specimens (`n = 32` baseline biopsies), prospective multi-center clinical trials pairing pre-biopsy spatial Visium profiling with `TGFBR1`-based stromal unlocking regimens will be essential to confirm clinical survival benefit.

## Methods

### Non-CNS clinical trial census, Firewall-2 enforcement, and multi-agent stoppage deconvolution

Interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV clinical trials were retrieved from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 across 12 predefined non-neurological disease axes (`Solid_Oncology`, `Hepatic_MASH_Fibrosis`, `Pulmonary_IPF_ARDS`, `Renal_CKD_AKI`, `Autoimmune_IBD_Rheum`, `Cardiovascular_HF_Athero`, `Metabolic_Endocrine`, `Infectious_Sepsis`, `Hematologic_Malignancy`, `Dermatologic_Immune`, `Musculoskeletal_Bone`, and `Ophthalmic_Retinal`). Every trial record was locked with an immutable SHA-256 content hash. To guarantee zero thematic or data overlap with central nervous system studies (`Firewall-2`), an automated lexical and Medical Subject Headings exclusion filter screened all `conditions`, `brief_title`, and `keywords` fields against neurological terms (including brain, spinal cord, stroke, Alzheimer, Parkinson, glioma, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, epilepsy, and neuropathic disorders), blocking 587 records and retaining 41,396 unique non-CNS trials.

Intervention names and synonyms were mapped against the Open Targets Platform (`24.09` Parquet release; `molecule` and `mechanismOfAction` schemas) and ChEMBL target hierarchies to assign primary and polypharmacological human target gene symbols (`HGNC`) and therapeutic modalities (`Small molecule`, `Antibody`, `Protein`, `Oligo`, `Gene`, or `Cell`). Prematurely halted target-resolved trials (`TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `SUSPENDED`, `n = 6,595`) were audited by a deterministic multi-agent lexical-semantic classifier operating over `why_stopped` text, enrollment fractions, and posted outcome tables to assign each record into four mutually exclusive categories: `Administrative_Commercial_Enrollment` (accrual, funding, business reprioritization, logistics, or sponsor transfer), `Scientific_Efficacy_Futility` (interim futility, lack of efficacy, or negative endpoint), `Scientific_Safety_Toxicity` (adverse events, dose-limiting organ toxicity, or data safety monitoring board safety halt), or `Unreported / Other`.

### Healthy baseline specificity and six-axis primary human single-cell dynamic drift calculation

Healthy baseline cell-type expression specificity ($\tau_0$) was computed across 20,151 human protein-coding genes and 154 single-cell types from the Human Protein Atlas single-cell transcriptomics consensus dataset using Yanai's specificity index:

$$\tau = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left(1 - \frac{x_i}{\max_{1 \le j \le N} x_j}\right)}{N - 1}$$

where $x_i = \log_2(\text{nTPM}_{i} + 1)$ denotes normalized expression in cell type $i$ across $N = 154$ healthy cell types. Vital-organ bystander hazard was defined as the normalized expression share across cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, proximal tubular epithelial cells, and alveolar type-2 pneumocytes for non-homologous indications.

Primary human single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq count matrices were processed under strict memory-bounded streaming (`< 7 GB` peak RAM) across six non-CNS remodeling cohorts from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO): hepatic cirrhosis and MASH (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`), pulmonary IPF (`GSE135893`), renal CKD/DKD (`GSE271188`), NSCLC solid tumors (`GSE207422`), mucosal ulcerative colitis (`GSE250487`), and longitudinal bacterial sepsis (`GSE167363`). Within each donor sample, cells were aggregated into standardized three- or four-compartment tissue pseudobulk profiles (`Parenchyma_Injury`, `SPP1_SAMac` / `Myeloid`, `Myofibroblast` / `myCAF`, and `CD8_T` in oncology), normalized to $\log_2(\text{CPM} + 1)$. Disease-stage specificity ($\tau_{\text{early}}$, $\tau_{\text{late}}$), dynamic specificity drift ($\Delta\tau = \tau_{\text{late}} - \tau_{\text{early}}$), dominant lineage compartment switching ($\mathbb{I}[\arg\max_c x_{c,\text{late}} \ne \arg\max_c x_{c,\text{early}}]$), and ectopic stromal share were computed for all 1,155 clinical target genes. Modality-dependent biophysical barrier compatibility ($B_{\text{modality}} \in [0, 1]$) modeled molecular-weight-dependent steric attenuation across fibrotic and desmoplastic extracellular matrix scores. Out-of-fold trial-level clinical success probabilities were estimated using 5-fold stratified cross-validated L2-regularized logistic regression, reporting out-of-fold AUROC, Brier score, Wald OR per 1-s.d. predictor increment, and quartile (`Q4/Q1`) maturation ratios.

### Multicellular Langevin SDE engine, profile-likelihood audit, and Waddington quasi-potential

Tissue niche remodeling across normalized compartment states $\mathbf{S}(t) \in [0, 1]^K$ was modeled via coupled Itô-Langevin stochastic differential equations integrated by Euler-Maruyama discretization ($\Delta t = 0.05\text{ h}$, horizon $T = 48\text{ h}$, `220-400` Monte Carlo trajectories per donor or regimen):

$$dS_i = \left[ \sum_{j} v_{ji} \frac{S_j^n}{k_{ji}^n + S_j^n} - d_i S_i - \eta_i(t, \text{drug}, B_{\text{modality}}) S_i \right] dt + \sigma_i \sqrt{\max(S_i(1 - S_i), 0)} \, dW_i(t)$$

where $v_{ji}$ and $k_{ji}$ denote Hill activation weights and half-saturation constants ($n = 2$), $d_i$ denotes intrinsic compartment turnover, $\eta_i$ denotes barrier-gated pharmacodynamic target perturbation, and $\sigma_i dW_i(t)$ represents state-bounded demographic and microenvironmental Wiener noise. For pan-organ fibrosis (`K = 3`: `Parenchyma_Injury`, `SPP1_SAMac`, `Myofibroblast`), parameters were optimized exclusively against empirical stage distributions of the 17 primary human hepatic donors (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`) and transferred zero-shot (`0` refitted parameters) to human lung IPF (`GSE135893`, `n = 30`) and kidney CKD/DKD (`GSE271188`, `n = 17`) to predict clinical outcomes across 14 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials. Practical parameter identifiability was audited by scanning each directed coupling weight $v_{ji}$ across `[0.0, 1.25]` while re-optimizing nuisance parameters, declaring 95% confidence identifiability when bilateral profile $\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$ ($\chi^2_1$). Two-dimensional and marginal Waddington quasi-potential surfaces were reconstructed from empirical Fokker-Planck terminal density histograms via $U(\mathbf{S}) = -\ln(P_{\text{ss}}(\mathbf{S}) + 10^{-6})$. Combination synergy in locked F4 cirrhosis was quantified via Bliss independence excess ($E_{AB} - [E_A + E_B - E_A E_B]$) and HSA excess ($E_{AB} - \max(E_A, E_B)$).

### Solid-tumor 10x Visium spatial proximity parameterization and blind pre-treatment ICB benchmark

Twelve primary human carcinoma 10x Visium spatial sections (`GSE171351`, 4 bladder cancer sections; `GSE203612`, 8 BRCA, OVCA, LIHC, and GIST sections; `21,796` total spots) were scored per spot for `Tumor` core, `SPP1+/CD163+` `TAM`, `ACTA2+/COL1A1+/TGFB1+` `myCAF`, and `CD8A+/GZMB+/PRF1+` `CD8_T` signatures. Spatial interaction weights between compartments $a$ and $b$ were computed via Gaussian radial-basis decay ($\exp(-d_{uv}^2 / 2\ell^2)$ over Visium array coordinates, characteristic length $\ell = 2.5$ spot pitches) and aggregated across all 12 sections (`Wilcoxon` signed-rank test comparing `TAM <-> myCAF` versus `Tumor <-> CD8_T` proximity).

In the four-compartment solid-tumor Langevin SDE, autonomous tumor Allee self-renewal (`v = 0.295, k = 0.38`) was opposed by `CD8_T` cytolysis gated multiplicatively by a Hill `myCAF` steric-exclusion factor and a Holling Type-II effector-to-tumor (`E:T`) load-saturation term, with smooth observational remission likelihood evaluated at major pathological response threshold $S_{\text{Tumor}}(T) \le 0.36$. Platform-invariant median-absolute-deviation (`MAD`) logistic scaling mapped patient baseline profiles onto `[0, 1]^4` without using clinical response labels. Blind responder prediction was evaluated on `GSE123813` (`n = 8` strictly pre-treatment full-TME BCC scRNA-seq biopsies, applying FACS-invariant tumor-reactive T-cell weighting to adjust for separate `CD45+ CD3+` sorting tubes, plus `n = 15` paired pre/post clonal trajectories), `GSE207422` NSCLC TME scRNA-seq (`n = 14`), `GSE207422` strictly pre-treatment baseline NSCLC bulk biopsies (`n = 24`), and the pooled strictly pre-treatment biopsy cohort (`n = 32`).

### Statistical analysis

Discrimination was quantified using exact Mann-Whitney AUROC and precision-recall AUPRC, and probability calibration was assessed using the Brier mean squared error. Logistic effect sizes are reported as Wald odds ratios per 1-s.d. covariate increment with two-sided 95% confidence intervals and exact two-sided P values. Paired spatial proximity comparisons across Visium sections were evaluated by two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. All computational pipelines were verified by an automated 27-test unit suite confirming mathematical invariants, zero label leakage, and `Firewall-2` neurological isolation.
