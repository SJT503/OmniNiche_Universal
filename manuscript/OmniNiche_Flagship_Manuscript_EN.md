# Dynamic single-cell niche remodeling and biophysical steric exclusion govern clinical trial attrition across human organs

---

## Abstract

Late-stage clinical trial attrition exceeds 85% despite widespread use of human genetic evidence and healthy-tissue expression atlases, which assume stationary baseline selectivity and equal macromolecular penetration across diseased organs. Here we integrate 41,396 Phase II–IV clinical trials across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes (29,791 target-resolved studies) with primary human single-cell, pre-treatment bulk, and 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics (>530,000 single cells, 99 pre-treatment bulk tumor biopsies, and 21,796 spatial spots across 237 clinical specimens) inside OmniNiche. Validated against 240 manually adjudicated trials (95.8% accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.941$), clause-aware deconvolution separates operational halts (51.4% of stops) from biological failures and reveals 1.57-fold higher efficacy futility odds for 150-kDa monoclonal antibodies than small molecules ($P = 2.26 \times 10^{-4}$). Across six single-cell disease axes, 17.8%–37.1% of profiled targets switch dominant expressing lineage during progression. Unifying single-cell drift with empirical matrix steric exclusion into Dynamic Niche Accessibility (`DNAcc`) improves scientific outcome discrimination over healthy specificity from AUROC 0.5387 to 0.6682 (DeLong $P = 1.25 \times 10^{-20}$; unseen-target AUROC 0.6456) and synergizes with Open Targets human genetic support (joint AUROC 0.6977 versus 0.6362 for genetics alone, $P = 7.13 \times 10^{-10}$; target-clustered adjusted odds ratio = 1.38, $P = 2.39 \times 10^{-4}$). Zero-label cross-organ Langevin transfer predicts 24 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials (AUROC = 0.8714, $P = 0.0010$; core-14 AUROC = 0.9583) and stratifies 107 pre-treatment anti-PD-1 solid-tumor biopsies (AUROC = 0.6367, $P = 0.0187$; single-cell microenvironment cohorts AUROC = 0.8750).

**Keywords:** clinical trial attrition, dynamic niche remodeling, human genetic support, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, organ fibrosis, cancer immunotherapy, biophysical systems pharmacology

## Introduction

Nearly 90% of investigational drugs entering clinical testing fail to achieve regulatory approval, with Phase IIb and Phase III efficacy failures driving the majority of late-stage patient exposure and capital loss [1, 2]. Human genetic evidence has become the foundational computational filter for target discovery, approximately doubling clinical approval probabilities when causal variant-to-gene support links a target to its intended indication [3, 4]. Knowledge-graph platforms such as Open Targets and biomedical foundation models such as TxGNN have scaled genetic and repurposing prioritization across thousands of diseases [5, 6]. Drug-discovery teams simultaneously consult healthy human single-cell atlases, including the Human Protein Atlas, to prioritize genes with high baseline cell-type specificity ($\tau_0$) and minimize vital-organ toxicity [7, 8]. Yet late-stage efficacy failures persist even among genetically plausible or healthy-selective targets, including chemokine receptor antagonists in steatohepatitis, matrix-crosslinking monoclonal antibodies in organ fibrosis, and checkpoint antibodies in immune-excluded solid carcinomas.

We hypothesized that germline genetics establishes causal target-disease validity whereas clinical realization in remodeled human tissue is gated by two dynamic tissue barriers invisible to healthy atlases. First, chronic parenchymal injury, mucosal ulceration, and malignant growth trigger multicellular niche reorganization marked by accumulation of profibrotic $TREM2^+SPP1^+$ scar-associated macrophages (SAMacs) and $COL1A1^+POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts [9, 10]. During progression, a target highly enriched in healthy parenchyma or circulating monocytes can collapse within its intended cellular compartment ($\Delta\tau < 0$) or shift ectopically into bystander stroma. Second, crosslinked extracellular matrix (ECM) scars and peritumoral cancer-associated fibroblast (`myCAF`) and macrophage capsules erect physical steric barriers that impede ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies while permitting diffusion of small molecules and compact peptides [11, 12].

Testing whether dynamic lineage drift and macromolecular steric exclusion govern clinical attrition across human organs first requires deconfounding public clinical trial registries. In ClinicalTrials.gov, studies halted for corporate portfolio reprioritization, sponsor mergers, pandemic disruption, or slow accrual share the same terminated or withdrawn flags as studies halted for interim efficacy futility or organ toxicity [13]. Multi-clause narratives routinely embed negated safety disclaimers (for example, closing for strategic reasons with no safety concerns), causing naive keyword matchers to misclassify operational closures as biological failures and mask genuine modality-by-matrix attrition laws.

Here we present OmniNiche, a multi-scale translational framework uniting macro-registry outcome deconvolution with single-cell, spatial, and biophysical tissue modeling. OmniNiche integrates clause-aware deconvolution across 41,396 Phase II–IV trials spanning 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes with primary human transcriptomics across 237 clinical specimens (>530,000 single cells, 99 strictly pre-treatment bulk tumor biopsies, and 21,796 10x Visium spots). Combining a parsimonious white-box `DNAcc` logistic law evaluated out-of-fold across unseen drug targets with zero-label cross-organ and spatial Itô–Langevin biophysical transfer, OmniNiche provides orthogonal predictive power beyond Open Targets human genetics, predicts 24 Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials across human liver, lung, and kidney, and stratifies pre-treatment anti-PD-1 outcomes across 107 human solid-tumor biopsies.

## Results

### Clause-aware deconvolution of 41,396 clinical trials exposes administrative noise and macromolecular futility

To assemble an unconfounded clinical translation census across peripheral organs governed by interstitial matrix remodeling, we queried interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV trials from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes (Fig. 1a–d, Extended Data Table 1, and Supplementary Table S1). Deduplicating 43,878 raw axis hits to 41,983 unique NCT studies and excluding 587 primary central nervous system or brain-metastasis trials yielded 41,396 unique clinical trials (23,536 Phase II, 11,537 Phase III, and 6,323 Phase IV studies). Cohort volume was led by solid oncology ($n = 15,717$), metabolic and endocrine disease ($n = 4,915$), hematologic oncology ($n = 3,695$), pulmonary fibrosis and acute lung injury ($n = 3,525$), cardiovascular disease ($n = 3,381$), and autoimmune or mucosal inflammation ($n = 2,529$) (Fig. 2a,b and Extended Data Fig. 1a). Mapping drug interventions against ChEMBL v33 mechanisms and Open Targets v24.09 drug-target annotations [5] resolved 29,791 trials (72.0%) to 1,155 unique human target genes (586 unique primary targets) across small-molecule regimens ($n = 18,128$, comprising 17,963 typed small molecules and 165 untyped chemical entries), IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 7,308$), and non-antibody biologics ($n = 4,355$, comprising 4,021 protein or peptide regimens and 334 cell, gene, or oligonucleotide therapies) (Extended Data Fig. 1b).

To resolve registry label confounding without circular heuristics [13], we built a unified clause-aware stoppage parser that segments multi-clause `whyStopped` narratives across punctuation and contrastive conjunctions and strips negated safety or efficacy disclaimers before classification (Extended Data Fig. 1c and Supplementary Table S2). We benchmarked this parser against a Naive Keyword Baseline on an independent 240-trial clinical adjudication reference (`Manual_Clinical_Adjudication_240`: 64 futility, 54 safety, 96 administrative, and 26 unreported stops) annotated directly from verbatim registry narratives. The clause-aware auditor achieved 95.83% accuracy (95% Wilson confidence interval (CI) 92.50%–97.72%) and Cohen's $\kappa = 0.9413$ (F1 = 0.9600 for efficacy futility, 0.9815 for safety, 0.9634 for administrative halts, and 0.8929 for unreported stops), substantially outperforming the Naive Keyword Baseline (70.00% accuracy, 95% CI 63.92%–75.45%, $\kappa = 0.6105$, administrative recall 43.75%) (Fig. 2f).

Applying this validated parser uniformly across all 9,406 stopped trials (31,990 completed) showed that administrative, commercial, and accrual halts represent 51.4% ($n = 4,838$) of all trial stoppages, outnumbering explicit biological failures ($n = 793$, comprising 476 efficacy futility and 317 safety/toxicity stops; $n = 3,775$ unreported or non-specific stops) by 6.1-fold (Fig. 2a). From 2005 to 2025, administrative halts rose from 44.1% of stopped trials in 2005 to 64.0% ($1,296 / 2,024$) pooled across 2020–2025 (peaking at 66.3% in 2021), while monoclonal antibody share among target-resolved trials doubled from 19.5% to 40.1% in 2021 (Fig. 2c). Enrollment distributions corroborated biological separation: administrative halts closed early at a median of 16 patients (0.29 million cumulative patients) and safety halts at 24 patients (48,497 patients), whereas efficacy futility trials reached interim or primary readouts at a median of 78 patients (0.12 million patients) alongside completed trials at 84 patients (9.77 million patients) (Fig. 2e).

Cross-tabulating therapeutic modality against deconvoluted stoppage cause revealed a selective macromolecular efficacy penalty predicted by interstitial transport biophysics [12] (Fig. 2d). Compared with small molecules ($n = 17,963$), ~150-kDa IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 7,308$) exhibited 1.57-fold higher odds of explicit biological efficacy futility (odds ratio (OR) = 1.57, exact two-sided Fisher's 95% CI 1.24–1.99, $P = 2.26 \times 10^{-4}$) without significant excess safety termination (OR = 1.12, 95% CI 0.84–1.50, $P = 0.45$). Compact protein and peptide therapeutics (~2–15 kDa; $n = 4,021$) incurred significantly lower efficacy futility odds than small molecules (OR = 0.59, 95% CI 0.39–0.90, $P = 0.0139$). Filtering administrative ($n = 3,412$) and unreported ($n = 2,618$) halts from the 29,791 target-resolved studies defined a 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 565 explicit biological failures [328 futility and 237 safety] across 374 primary targets) and a 23,761-trial Definitive Phase II-to-III/IV Maturation Cohort across 561 primary targets.

### Human single-cell disease progression destabilizes healthy target lineage specificity

To test whether pathological cellular remodeling explains why healthy baseline specificity ($\tau_0$) fails to predict clinical attrition, we harmonized primary human transcriptomic cohorts across 237 clinical specimens: >530,000 single cells across 126 single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-sequencing donor profiles, 99 strictly pre-treatment bulk RNA-seq tumor biopsies, and 12 primary carcinoma 10x Visium spatial sections (21,796 spots) (Extended Data Fig. 2a, Extended Data Table 1, and Supplementary Table S3). Six core disease axes were directly resolved from primary single-cell progression biopsies: hepatic metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and cirrhosis (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF, `GSE135893`), chronic kidney disease (CKD, `GSE271188`), solid carcinomas (`GSE123813` and `GSE207422`), mucosal ulcerative colitis (`GSE250487`), and bloodstream sepsis (`GSE167363`) [14-18]. Across 1,181 unique human target and pathway genes, we quantified healthy $\tau_0$ across 154 Human Protein Atlas cell types alongside early-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), advanced-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$), dynamic drift ($\Delta\tau = \tau_{\mathrm{late}} - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and ectopic dominant-lineage switching. In solid oncology, progression drift was computed strictly from response-label-free early primary tumors versus advanced resected carcinomas without accessing clinical immunotherapy response labels, while empirical interstitial ECM barrier density $D_{\mathrm{ECM}}(\mathrm{axis}) \in [0.070, 0.350]$ was derived directly from Human Protein Atlas and single-cell stromal collagen-myofibroblast signatures across all 12 axes.

Disease progression destabilized baseline target-lineage assignments across all six single-cell organ axes (Fig. 3a). Across all profiled targets, 17.8% in solid oncology (21.0% among expressed targets), 21.5% in pulmonary fibrosis (22.5%), 28.2% in ulcerative colitis (32.1%), 30.6% in hepatic MASH (31.3%), 31.1% in renal CKD (31.6%), and 37.1% in systemic sepsis (41.3%) switched their primary expressing cellular compartment between early and advanced human pathology (Extended Data Fig. 2b,c). Mapping clinical targets into the $(\tau_0, \Delta\tau)$ remodeling phase space separated late-stage clinical winners from failures along the vertical dynamic-drift coordinate rather than the horizontal healthy-specificity axis (Fig. 3h). Targets that preserved or sharpened lineage selectivity in accessible parenchymal or vascular-mesenchymal niches ($\Delta\tau \ge 0$), including `THRB`, `PCSK9`, `ERBB2`, and `PDGFRB`, achieved high Phase III/IV maturation rates, whereas high-$\tau_0$ targets undergoing late-stage lineage erosion or ectopic stromal capture, such as `LOXL2` and `TGFB1`, repeatedly failed in pivotal trials.

### Dynamic Niche Accessibility complements human genetic evidence and generalizes across unseen targets

We formalized these mechanisms into a six-step nested white-box logistic ablation (`M1` through `M6`) culminating in `DNAcc`, which integrates healthy $\tau_0$, vital-organ bystander hazard, early-disease specificity ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), remodeling drift ($\Delta\tau$), ectopic compartment switching, empirical modality-by-ECM steric compatibility ($B_{\mathrm{acc}}$), and the biophysical $\tau_{\mathrm{early}} \times B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ delivery-selectivity interaction (Table 1 and Fig. 3b). On the 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort, static healthy $\tau_0$ (`M1`) performed near random chance (stratified 5-fold AUROC = 0.5387, 95% CI 0.5152–0.5610), whereas adding vital-organ hazard (`M2`, 0.5618), early-disease specificity (`M3`, 0.6614), progression drift and lineage switching (`M4`–`M5`, 0.6650), and steric barrier compatibility (`M6`) elevated discrimination to AUROC = 0.6682 (95% CI 0.6455–0.6907; $\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1296$, two-sided paired DeLong $P = 1.25 \times 10^{-20}$) [19]. Within the six directly single-cell-resolved axes ($n = 5,662$ scientific trials across 293 primary targets), `DNAcc` achieved AUROC = 0.6752 versus 0.5447 for static $\tau_0$ ($P = 1.16 \times 10^{-14}$). Across the 23,761-trial Definitive Maturation Cohort, performance rose monotonically at every ablation step (`0.5412 -> 0.5777 -> 0.6607 -> 0.6623 -> 0.6716 -> 0.6745`, $\Delta\mathrm{AUROC} = +0.1333$, DeLong $P = 2.60 \times 10^{-176}$; Brier error reduced from 0.2477 to 0.2275).

To test directly whether dynamic tissue accessibility adds orthogonal clinical value beyond human genetics [4], we benchmarked `DNAcc` head-to-head against Open Targets v24.09 target-disease human genetic association and somatic mutation evidence across all 11,637 scientific and 23,761 maturation trials (Table 1 and Fig. 3b). On the Deconfounded Scientific Cohort, human genetics alone achieved AUROC = 0.6362 (95% CI 0.6085–0.6625; unseen-target `Group5Fold` AUROC = 0.6175), whereas adding static healthy $\tau_0$ to genetics yielded zero gain (AUROC = 0.6344, $\Delta = -0.0018$, DeLong $P = 0.45$). `DNAcc` alone outperformed human genetics alone (AUROC = 0.6682 versus 0.6362, $\Delta = +0.0321$, DeLong $P = 0.0302$), and combining `Human Genetics + DNAcc` achieved strong additive synergy (AUROC = 0.6977, 95% CI 0.6744–0.7198; $\Delta = +0.0615$ over genetics alone, DeLong $P = 7.13 \times 10^{-10}$; unseen-target `Group5Fold` AUROC = 0.6728 versus 0.6175, $P = 2.92 \times 10^{-7}$). Within already genetically supported targets (Open Targets genetic score $\ge 0.10$, $n = 7,174$ trials), `DNAcc` reached AUROC = 0.7012 versus 0.5267 for static $\tau_0$ ($P = 7.48 \times 10^{-27}$). Across the 23,761-trial Definitive Maturation Cohort, `Human Genetics + DNAcc` reached AUROC = 0.7190 (95% CI 0.7123–0.7252; DeLong $P = 2.19 \times 10^{-103}$ versus genetics alone [0.6631]; unseen-target `Group5Fold` 0.7003 versus 0.6413, $P = 1.86 \times 10^{-119}$).

Five orthogonal deconfounding audits confirmed that `DNAcc` is neither target-memorized nor a proxy for drug modality or polypharmacology. First, under target-disjoint `Group5Fold` cross-validation, `DNAcc` generalized across unseen targets in all 12 axes (AUROC = 0.6456 versus 0.5030 for $\tau_0$, $P = 4.70 \times 10^{-23}$) and in the six core single-cell axes (0.6385 versus 0.5049, $P = 2.97 \times 10^{-14}$) (Fig. 3b and Extended Data Fig. 1e). Second, `DNAcc` retained significant unseen-target `Group5Fold` discrimination within strictly single-target monotherapies on both the scientific cohort ($n = 6,115$ trials across 235 targets; stratified AUROC = 0.6278, $P = 4.11 \times 10^{-5}$; unseen-target `Group5Fold` 0.5872 versus 0.4697, $P = 1.35 \times 10^{-4}$) and the maturation cohort ($n = 10,507$ trials across 374 targets; `Group5Fold` 0.6375 versus 0.4310, $P = 7.39 \times 10^{-123}$), alongside multi-target regimens (`Group5Fold` 0.6647, $P = 4.88 \times 10^{-20}$) (Extended Data Fig. 1d). Third, `DNAcc` significantly outperformed static $\tau_0$ strictly within every major drug modality: small molecules ($n = 7,286$, 235 targets; AUROC = 0.6735 versus 0.5451, $P = 2.04 \times 10^{-13}$), IgG monoclonal antibodies ($n = 2,264$, 137 targets; 0.6657 versus 0.5959, $P = 0.0133$), and proteins or peptides ($n = 1,904$, 99 targets; 0.6107 versus 0.4870, $P = 0.0045$) (Fig. 3d).

Fourth, in multivariable fixed-effects logistic regressions controlling simultaneously for disease axis, drug modality, calendar era, sponsor class, and polypharmacology with Liang–Zeger cluster-robust sandwich inference across $G = 374$ to $561$ target clusters, `DNAcc` remained strongly predictive on the scientific cohort (adjusted OR per 1-s.d. = 1.40, Wald 95% CI 1.26–1.56, $P = 3.26 \times 10^{-10}$; target-clustered 95% CI 1.19–1.65, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 5.86 \times 10^{-5}$). In a simultaneous three-pillar model adjusting for out-of-fold human genetic support, static $\tau_0$, and all five confounder families, `DNAcc` retained robust independent effect (`DNAcc` OR = 1.38, target-clustered 95% CI 1.16–1.65, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 2.39 \times 10^{-4}$), whereas static $\tau_0$ collapsed to unity (OR = 1.00, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.98$) (Fig. 3c and Extended Data Fig. 3b). Fifth, per-axis Definitive Maturation stratification confirmed significant gains across all five solid and mucosal axes (`OR = 1.25` to `2.13`), whereas intravascular PBMC sepsis ($n = 141$) showed expected null discrimination (AUROC = 0.3841 versus 0.3616, $P = 0.663$), establishing an explicit anatomical boundary condition (Table 1 and Extended Data Fig. 2d).

Across quartiles of the 23,761-trial maturation cohort, empirical Phase II-to-III/IV progression climbed 2.18-fold from 29.47% in Q1 to 64.39% in Q4 under `DNAcc`, versus 1.20-fold (44.96% to 53.87%) under static $\tau_0$ (Fig. 3e). Out-of-fold probabilities were well calibrated across deciles on both cohorts (Fig. 3f and Extended Data Fig. 3e) and yielded higher Decision Curve Analysis net benefit across Phase III gating thresholds ($p_t = 0.28$ to $0.65$) [20] (Fig. 3g). Seven sensitivity regimes (`R1`–`R7`) confirmed invariance across unfiltered naive-keyword outcomes (`R2`, $n = 11,843$, AUROC = 0.6671), worst-case assignment of all 2,618 target-resolved unreported stops as biological failures (`R3`, $n = 14,255$, AUROC = 0.6541, $P = 1.50 \times 10^{-70}$), inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting (`R4`, 0.6669), and posted-results-only trials (`R5`, $n = 5,609$, 0.6817). Equal stability held against regulatory-approved drug-indication pairs (`R6`, $n = 9,697$, AUROC = 0.6777 versus 0.5177, $P = 5.61 \times 10^{-27}$) and the strict nine-axis solid-parenchymal interstitial boundary (`R7`, $n = 10,701$, AUROC = 0.6860 versus 0.5304, $P = 1.64 \times 10^{-24}$) (Extended Data Fig. 3c). Gating out the bottom 25% of Phase II candidates by `DNAcc` lifted Phase III/IV maturation from 46.60% to 52.31%, preventing 4,190 failed trials (~1.16 million patient exposures at median 276 patients per Phase III trial) while retaining 84.19% of winners (Extended Data Fig. 3d).

### Zero-shot cross-organ human fibrosis transfer predicts 24 Phase IIb/III clinical trial outcomes

Registry-wide `DNAcc` scoring flags vulnerable targets, yet clinical efficacy in fibrotic disease also depends on enrolled histological stage and nonlinear multicellular feedback. Harmonizing 64 primary human single-cell and single-nucleus biopsies across liver (`GSE136103` and `GSE212837`, $n = 17$), lung (`GSE135893`, $n = 30$), and kidney (`GSE271188`, $n = 17$) using closure-free cell-level module activity scores confirmed a conserved three-compartment fibrotic triad coupling injured epithelial or hepatocyte parenchyma, profibrotic $TREM2^+SPP1^+$ SAMacs, and $COL1A1^+POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Table S4). Across 19 human myeloid donor profiles spanning blood monocytes, healthy liver macrophages, F2/F3 MASH, and F4 cirrhosis, circulating monocyte `CCR2` expression (mean log-expression 6.19) plunged by 76% to 1.47 in F2/F3 intrahepatic macrophages. Concurrently, tissue-resident $SPP1^+$ (0.00 in blood and 5.46 in healthy liver rising to 8.31 in F4 cirrhosis) and $TREM2^+$ (0.39 to 5.77) SAMacs expanded, explaining why the high-$\tau_0$ `CCR2/CCR5` antagonist cenicriviroc ($\tau_0 = 0.9531$) failed in Phase III `AURORA` (`NCT03028740`) [21] (Fig. 4b).

We compiled the three-compartment stochastic Itô–Langevin grammar exclusively from the 17 human liver donors without accessing any clinical trial outcome labels. Bilateral 25-point profile-likelihood scans with nuisance rate-scale re-optimization verified structural identifiability ($\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$) across all four directed intercellular edges [22, 23] (Extended Data Fig. 3a). To eliminate hand-coded drug-compartment heuristics, target compartment weights were derived objectively from organ-specific single-cell pseudobulk expression ($\ge 58\%$ of peak compartmental expression) coupled with $\tau_0$-weighted receptor-sink dilution and unsigned ridge-GRN causal leverage, while routing extrahepatic metabolic or hemodynamic peptides and transporters lacking intrahepatic or intrarenal stromal expression (`GLP1R`, `GIPR`, and `SLC5A2`) to upstream `Parenchyma_Injury` unloading. Freezing network topology and Hill kinetics, we evaluated 24 live ClinicalTrials.gov SHA-256-locked Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials (10 regulatory or Phase IIb/III primary-endpoint winners versus 14 explicit scientific efficacy failures across liver MASH [$n = 13$], lung IPF [$n = 8$], and kidney CKD [$n = 3$], containing the pre-specified Core-14 landmark subset) [24-36] (Fig. 4c).

Across all 24 Phase IIb/III trials, zero-label Langevin transfer separated clinical winners from failures with AUROC = 0.8714 (95% CI 0.7071–0.9929), AUPRC = 0.7769, Brier error = 0.2072, and 10,000-permutation exact $P = 0.0010$ ($P = 0.0103$ versus static $\tau_0$ [AUROC = 0.4643]), outperforming single-cell GRN retention (0.8357), enrolled-stage prior alone (`B0`, 0.7714), modality prior (0.7500), target count (0.6750), and static ligand-receptor degree (0.4714) (Table 2 and Fig. 4d). On the Core-14 landmark subset, OmniNiche achieved AUROC = 0.9583 (95% CI 0.8333–1.0000, AUPRC = 0.9484, Brier = 0.1191, exact $P = 0.0031$). Even when initial histological stage was held identical across all trials (`OmniNiche_StageBlind_FixedS0`), target-by-barrier dynamics retained significant discrimination across all 24 trials (AUROC = 0.7643, exact $P = 0.0311$) and reached AUROC = 0.9375 ($P = 0.0058$) on Core-14, surpassing enrolled-stage prior alone (0.8125).

Individual trial predictions captured diverse antifibrotic mechanisms and failure modes (Fig. 4c). Small-molecule `THRB` agonist resmetirom (`MAESTRO-NASH`, simulated probability 0.9648), pan-PPAR agonist lanifibranor (`NATIVE`, 0.9649), multi-kinase inhibitor nintedanib (`INPULSIS-1`, 0.9704), and metabolic peptides semaglutide (`ESSENCE`, 0.7531) and tirzepatide (`SYNERGY-NASH`, 0.8545) ranked at the top, matching human MASH biopsy histology and proteomics showing absent intrahepatic `GLP1R` alongside systemic suppression of circulating `TREM2` and fibrotic remodeling [24, 26, 33]. Conversely, ~150-kDa antibodies targeting `LOXL2` (`Simtuzumab`, 0.0381–0.0442), `CCN2` (`Pamrevlumab`, 0.0366), and `IL13` (`Lebrikizumab`, 0.0367), as well as `CCR2/CCR5` (`Cenicriviroc`, 0.0411) and `MAP3K5`/ASK1 (`Selonsertib`, 0.0409–0.0423 across liver and diabetic kidney trials), scored near zero. The sole high-scoring failure among all 24 trials was the small-molecule autotaxin (`ENPP2`) inhibitor ziritaxestat (`ISABELA 1`, 0.9667), which penetrated IPF tissue but was halted for Phase III benefit-risk and off-niche toxicity imbalance. Bidirectional $3 \times 3$ single-organ compilation and leave-one-organ-out (LOOO) validation confirmed symmetric transfer across organs: lung-only compilation ($n = 30$) achieved All-24 AUROC = 0.8500 (held-out 0.8571; Core-14 0.9583), kidney-only compilation ($n = 17$) achieved All-24 AUROC = 0.8393 (held-out 0.8365; Core-14 0.9583), and all three LOOO calibrations achieved All-24 AUROC of 0.8357–0.8821 and held-out AUROC of 0.8667–1.0000 (Table 2 and Fig. 4e).

### Bistable Waddington hysteresis defines a late-stage monotherapy cliff and orthogonal rescue law

Reconstructing the two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential surface $U(S_{\mathrm{Myofib}}, S_{\mathrm{Paren}})$ and scanning 25 anatomical progression states from early F1/F2 steatohepatitis to F4 cirrhosis explained why single-compartment drugs lose activity in advanced scars [9, 26, 28] (Fig. 4f,g). Early F1/F2 disease resides in a monostable reversible valley where unloading parenchymal injury (`THRB` or `GLP1R`) permits spontaneous relaxation of downstream SAMac and myofibroblast activity. Once progression crosses a saddle-node bifurcation ($s_{\mathrm{prog}} \approx 0.42\text{–}0.54$, corresponding to monotherapy window cliff $t_{50} = 8.3\text{ h}$), cooperative Hill feedback ($n = 3.6$) between $SPP1^+TREM2^+$ SAMacs and $POSTN^+$ myofibroblasts forms a deep second attractor (`Locked F4`, $s_0 = [0.854, 0.880, 0.901]$) that traps every single-agent monotherapy at 0.0% simulated remission (Fig. 4f,g).

Evaluating a symmetric $6 \times 6$ clinical-target two-dimensional Bliss synergy matrix in locked F4 cirrhosis identified a strict orthogonal rescue rule (Fig. 4h). Pairing two parenchymal agents (`THRB + GLP1R`, 0.0% remission, Bliss excess $0.00$) or combining an active parenchymal agent with sterically blocked anti-`LOXL2` IgG or target-lost `CCR2` blockade (Bliss excess $0.00$) failed to breach the stromal saddle. Conversely, all four orthogonal combinations pairing upstream parenchymal unloading (`THRB` or `GLP1R`) with direct small-molecule myofibroblast `TGFBR1/PDGFRB` inhibition or `TREM2/SPP1` SAMac modulation collapsed the hysteresis barrier and restored 99.1% to 100.0% simulated remission (Bliss excess $+0.99$ to $+1.00$) (Fig. 4g,h).

### Spatial Visium peritumoral `myCAF`–`TAM` exclusion stratifies 107 pre-treatment anti-PD-1 biopsies

We next tested whether an analogous physical-dynamical barrier governs clinical resistance to ~150-kDa anti-PD-1 antibodies across human solid tumors, where peritumoral `myCAF` and $SPP1^+$ tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) capsules shield malignant nests from $CD8^+$ T cells [11, 37, 38]. Streaming 21,796 spots across 12 primary human carcinoma 10x Visium sections (`GSE171351` and `GSE203612`, spanning bladder, breast, ovarian, hepatocellular, and gastrointestinal carcinomas) [39, 40], we confirmed that peritumoral `myCAF`–`TAM` proximity exceeded direct `Tumor`–$CD8^+$ T-cell proximity in 12 of 12 sections (consensus 0.666 versus 0.471; Stromal Exclusion Index $\mathrm{SEI} > 0$ in 12/12 sections, one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $P = 2.44 \times 10^{-4}$) (Fig. 5a,b and Extended Data Fig. 4a,b). Continuous radial profiling from 0 to 600 µm outside the malignant core boundary [41] resolved malignant core decay from 0 µm ($z = +1.26 \pm 0.04$), $CD8^+$ T-cell depletion inside the core ($z = -0.06 \pm 0.04$) and arrest near 200 µm ($z = +0.08 \pm 0.04$), and an outer `myCAF` wall peaking at 500 µm ($z = +0.67 \pm 0.31$), exemplified by immune-excluded breast carcinoma (`GSM6177599`, $\mathrm{SEI} = +0.213$) versus infiltrated hepatocellular carcinoma (`GSM6177612`, $\mathrm{SEI} = +0.070$) (Fig. 5a,b and Supplementary Table S5).

Embedding this fixed 12-section Visium proximity matrix into a five-compartment spatial-Langevin solid-tumor solver with zero clinical label fitting, we evaluated five independent human anti-PD-1 cohorts ($n = 121$ profiled tumor samples, including $n = 107$ strictly pre-treatment biopsies). Every cohort was benchmarked against an ablated `OmniNiche (No-Visium)` uniform-$P_{ij}$ control and eight clinical or transcriptomic baselines, including `CD274`/PD-L1, the 18-gene T-cell-inflamed Gene Expression Profile (`18-gene GEP`), the published `CAF/TIDE` exclusion signature, and the algebraic `CD8:myCAF` ratio [42-47] (Table 2 and Fig. 5d). The cohorts comprised strictly pre-treatment anti-PD-1 monotherapy basal cell carcinoma (BCC) full-TME scRNA-seq (`GSE123813`, $n = 8$, streamed at single-UMI fidelity with non-`CD3+` FACS sort deconfounding recovering up to 4.8-fold diluted `myCAF` fractions; Extended Data Fig. 4c), pre-nivolumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq (`GSE91061`, $n = 49$), and pre-pembrolizumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq (`GSE78220`, $n = 26$). Two lung cohorts from `GSE207422` added pre-treatment neoadjuvant concurrent anti-PD-1 plus platinum-doublet chemo-immunotherapy non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) biopsy bulk RNA-seq ($n = 24$) and post-neoadjuvant resected NSCLC TME scRNA-seq ($n = 14$).

Spatial-Langevin modeling excelled wherever single-cell microenvironment resolution was preserved. OmniNiche achieved AUROC = 0.8750 (95% CI 0.5000–1.0000, AUPRC = 0.9167, Brier = 0.1423) in pre-treatment BCC scRNA-seq, outperforming `PD-L1` (0.4375), `18-gene GEP` (0.6875), `CAF/TIDE` (0.3750), and `CD8:myCAF` (0.5625). In resected NSCLC TME scRNA-seq, OmniNiche ranked first among all 10 methods with AUROC = 0.8750 (95% CI 0.7000–1.0000, exact $P = 0.0324$ versus null and $P = 0.0467$ versus `PD-L1` [0.3250]), beating `No-Visium` (0.8500), `CAF/TIDE` (0.7750), `CD8:myCAF` (0.7750), and `18-gene GEP` (0.6000) (Table 2 and Fig. 5d). In pre-treatment bulk biopsies, OmniNiche achieved AUROC = 0.7704 (95% CI 0.5592–0.9407, exact $P = 0.0288$) in NSCLC chemo-anti-PD-1 biopsies (versus `No-Visium` 0.7556 and `PD-L1` 0.6593), 0.5982 in `GSE78220` melanoma (versus `PD-L1` 0.5476 and `18-gene GEP` 0.4881), and 0.5385 in homogenized `GSE91061` melanoma biopsies (`18-gene GEP` 0.6128). Using zero-label leave-one-patient-out (LOPO) cross-platform rank alignment, OmniNiche outperformed `No-Visium` (+0.0201 AUROC) and every clinical biomarker signature across Pooled Pure Anti-PD-1 Monotherapy Pre-Treatment biopsies ($n = 83$, 28 responders: AUROC = 0.6016 versus `No-Visium` 0.5815, `PD-L1` 0.5338, `18-gene GEP` 0.5442, `CAF/TIDE` 0.5571, and `CD8:myCAF` 0.5873). Across Pooled All Strictly Pre-Treatment Biopsies ($n = 107$, 37 responders), OmniNiche reached AUROC = 0.6367 (95% CI 0.5253–0.7425, exact permutation $P = 0.0187$), exceeding `No-Visium` (0.6210), `PD-L1` (0.5610), `18-gene GEP` (0.5948), `CAF/TIDE` (0.6023), and `CD8:myCAF` (0.6263) (Fig. 5c,d and Extended Data Fig. 4d).

Across the 107 strictly pre-treatment biopsies at the 50% LOPO threshold, patient-level OncoPrint stratification flagged 14 of 35 (40.0%) `PD-L1`-high non-responders as resistant (with 23 of 35 harboring high `myCAF` barrier scores $\ge 0.50$) while rescuing 10 of 18 (55.6%) `PD-L1`-low clinical responders displaying unlocked stroma (Fig. 5c). Paired pre- versus post-anti-PD-1 longitudinal scRNA-seq across 15 cutaneous carcinoma patients (11 BCC and 4 cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma patients in `GSE123813`) confirmed sustained net $CD8^+$ effector expansion in unlocked responders versus progressive effector attrition behind intact `myCAF` capsules in non-responders (Fig. 5e). Across all $n = 70$ clinical non-responders among the 107 pre-treatment biopsies, simulated dual `anti-PD-1 + myCAF/TGFBR1` stromal unlocking shifted the 2D solid-tumor Waddington drift field across the stromal separatrix and raised raw physical Langevin remission probability from 13.7% (median 5.8%) under anti-PD-1 monotherapy to 78.1% (one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $P = 1.78 \times 10^{-13}$), while dual `anti-PD-1 + SPP1/TREM2` TAM reprogramming achieved 74.6% ($P = 1.78 \times 10^{-13}$) (Fig. 5f,g).

## Discussion

Target prioritization in drug discovery has largely relied on stationary biological priors, treating germline genetic association and healthy organ selectivity as fixed, modality-agnostic predictors of clinical efficacy [3-5]. By uniting validated clause-aware outcome deconvolution across 41,396 Phase II–IV clinical trials ($\kappa = 0.941$) with primary human single-cell, pre-treatment bulk, and 10x Visium transcriptomics across 237 clinical specimens, we demonstrate that clinical translation across peripheral solid and mucosal organs is co-governed by disease-stage lineage drift and macromolecular steric exclusion. Human genetics and dynamic niche accessibility (`DNAcc`) answer complementary questions: germline genetics tests whether perturbing a target causally modifies disease biology (AUROC = 0.6362), whereas `DNAcc` tests whether that target remains expressed in an accessible cellular compartment when a given therapeutic modality enters remodeled human tissue (AUROC = 0.6682 alone; 0.6977 scientific and 0.7190 maturation AUROC when combined with human genetics). Conversely, once operational halts (51.4% of all trial stoppages) are stripped from registry labels, static healthy specificity ($\tau_0$) adds zero predictive value to human genetics ($P = 0.45$) and collapses completely ($\mathrm{OR} = 1.00$, $P_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 0.98$) alongside `DNAcc`.

Three mechanistic rules emerge consistently across our macro-registry, 24-trial antifibrotic, and 107-biopsy oncology benchmarks. First, 17.8% to 37.1% of profiled target genes switch dominant cellular lineage during human pathology, explaining why targets prioritized from healthy atlases or circulating blood monocytes (`CCR2` in MASH) lose engagement inside advanced lesions [21]. Second, macromolecular hydrodynamic radius interacts strongly with interstitial ECM remodeling: ~150-kDa monoclonal antibodies incur a 1.57-fold excess in biological efficacy futility across the registry and fail uniformly in densely crosslinked fibrotic scars (`LOXL2`, `CCN2`, and `IL13` antibodies) [25, 28], whereas small molecules (`THRB`, pan-`PPAR`, and `PDGFR/FGFR/VEGFR` drugs) and compact acylated peptides unloading upstream metabolic injury (`GLP1R` and `GLP1R/GIPR` agonists) achieve late-stage histological resolution [24, 26, 33]. Third, advanced organ fibrosis and immune-excluded solid tumors behave as bistable Waddington attractors locked by reciprocal myeloid-mesenchymal feedback (`SPP1+TREM2+` SAMac/TAM coupled to `POSTN+` myofibroblast/`myCAF`) [9, 10, 38, 48, 49], creating an abrupt monotherapy cliff that requires orthogonal cross-compartment combinations.

Translating stromal-unlocking combinations into clinical protocols requires careful toxicological selectivity. Although our Langevin rescue simulations use `myCAF/TGFBR1` blockade as a canonical parameterization of myofibroblast capsule ablation, first-generation systemic pan-`TGFBR1` (ALK5) small-molecule inhibitors historically encountered dose-limiting cardiac valvulopathy and hemorrhagic toxicity due to homeostatic endocardial and vascular `TGFB` signaling. Clinically viable execution of our orthogonal rescue law should therefore prioritize spatially or lineage-restricted stromal modulators, including lung-restricted inhaled ALK5 inhibitors that avoid systemic cardiac exposure [50], selective targeting of pathogenic $LRRC15^+$ myofibroblasts or isoform-selective latent-`TGFB1` activation [51], and myeloid `TREM2/SPP1` or `CXCL9:SPP1` polarity reprogramming [38], which achieved nearly comparable simulated responder rescue (74.6% versus 78.1%) without pan-`TGFBR1` blockade. Methodologically, OmniNiche also complements supervised clinical immunotherapy models trained on routine blood panels, bulk transcriptomes, or histopathology-derived virtual spatial proteomics, such as SCORPIO, COMPASS, and HEX-MICA [52-54], as well as virtual-cell and agentic discovery platforms [55, 56], by providing a zero-label mechanistic prior when regimen-matched training cohorts are unavailable.

Several limitations bound the scope of our findings. First, 8.8% ($n = 2,618 / 29,791$) of target-resolved trials in ClinicalTrials.gov (9.1%, $n = 3,775 / 41,396$ registry-wide) halt without specific narrative reasons; although seven sensitivity regimes (`R1`–`R7`, including worst-case failure assignment and ChEMBL/Open Targets regulatory approval benchmarking) preserved effect direction and significance, standardized failure ontologies in public registries remain essential. Second, OmniNiche models local target-niche engagement and interstitial transport rather than idiosyncratic off-target or systemic chemical toxicity, explaining why the accessible small-molecule autotaxin inhibitor ziritaxestat (`ISABELA 1`) scored as a false positive despite Phase III benefit-risk termination, and why intravascular PBMC sepsis ($n = 141$, AUROC = 0.3841) lies outside the solid-organ interstitial domain. Third, whereas spatial-Langevin transfer achieved AUROC = 0.8750 in both single-cell TME oncology cohorts, bulk RNA-seq homogenization in cutaneous and metastatic melanoma biopsies (`GSE91061` AUROC = 0.5385; `GSE78220` 0.5982) attenuates concentric `myCAF` geometry relative to single-cell or spatially resolved biopsies.

Prospective clinical trials pairing pre-treatment spatial transcriptomic or multiplex proteomic biopsies with lineage-selective stromal-unlocking regimens will be needed to calibrate absolute patient-level decision cutoffs. In line with responsible clinical AI standards [53], OmniNiche outputs are intended as mechanistic hypothesis-generating instruments for target prioritization, modality matching, and biomarker-enriched trial design and must never be used in isolation to withhold standard-of-care immunotherapy or antifibrotic therapy. Anchoring virtual-tissue dynamics in human single-cell lineage drift and spatial barrier biophysics establishes a quantitative path to de-risk therapeutic modalities and orthogonal combinations prior to pivotal clinical trials.

## Methods

### ClinicalTrials.gov registry ingestion, scope definition, and target-gene resolution

Interventional Phase II, Phase III, and Phase IV clinical trial records were retrieved from ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (`https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies`) across 12 systemic and parenchymal organ disease axes: Solid Oncology, Metabolic and Endocrine, Hematologic Oncology, Pulmonary (IPF and ARDS), Cardiovascular, Autoimmune (IBD and Rheumatology), Renal (CKD and AKI), Dermatologic Immune, Musculoskeletal, Ophthalmic and Retinal, Hepatic (MASH and Cirrhosis), and Infectious and Sepsis. To maintain uniform interstitial transport biophysics, the cohort focuses on peripheral systemic and solid-organ pathologies governed by interstitial extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling rather than specialized blood-brain barrier neurovascular transport (43,878 raw axis-level registry hits deduplicated to 41,983 unique NCT studies prior to excluding 587 primary central nervous system or brain-metastasis records; 41,396 unique NCT studies retained).

Intervention strings, generic names, and synonyms were normalized and mapped against ChEMBL v33 curated drug mechanisms (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data`) and Open Targets Platform v24.09 drug-target and indication evidence (`https://api.platform.opentargets.org/api/v4/graphql`) [5], resolving 29,791 unique trials (72.0%) to 1,155 HGNC human target gene symbols (586 unique primary target genes; 1,181 total target and pathway genes profiled in the six-axis single-cell drift atlas) and standardized therapeutic modalities: 18,128 small-molecule regimens (17,963 typed small molecules and 165 untyped chemical entries), 7,308 IgG monoclonal antibody regimens, and 4,355 non-antibody biologic regimens (4,021 protein or peptide regimens, classifying compact acylated peptides such as 4.11-kDa semaglutide and 4.81-kDa tirzepatide as `peptide`, and 334 cell, gene, or oligonucleotide therapies).

### Unified clause-aware stoppage deconvolution and 240-trial clinical adjudication

Stopped trials (`TERMINATED`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `SUSPENDED`; $n = 9,406$ across the 41,396-trial registry, alongside $n = 31,990$ `COMPLETED` studies) were classified by a unified deterministic clause-aware natural-language parser operating identically across registry ingestion and validation on verbatim `whyStopped` narratives [13]. Narratives were segmented across punctuation and contrastive clause boundaries (`;`, `.`, `but`, `however`, `although`, `whereas`, `while`, `unrelated to`, `independent of`, `with no`, `without any`, and `not due to`) prior to lexical matching. Clauses containing negated safety or efficacy disclaimers (such as `no safety concerns`, `not due to safety or efficacy`, `absence of toxicity`, or `no unexpected adverse events`) were stripped of false-positive biological triggers before classification. Remaining non-negated futility or lack-of-efficacy clauses were assigned to `Scientific_Efficacy_Futility` ($n = 476$), explicit non-negated toxicity, adverse-event, or clinical-hold clauses to `Scientific_Safety_Toxicity` ($n = 317$), operational, commercial, accrual, sponsor-reprioritization, or pandemic clauses to `Administrative_Commercial_Enrollment` ($n = 4,838$; 51.4% of stopped trials), and empty or non-specific narratives to `Other_Unreported_Stop` ($n = 3,775$).

Parser accuracy, per-class precision, recall, F1, and Cohen's $\kappa$ were benchmarked head-to-head against a Naive Keyword Baseline (which matches raw safety, futility, and operational substrings without clause splitting or negation stripping) on an independent 240-trial clinical adjudication reference (`Manual_Clinical_Adjudication_240`: 64 efficacy futility, 54 safety/toxicity, 96 administrative/operational, and 26 unreported/other stops; Supplementary Table S2). Excluding administrative ($n = 3,412$) and unreported ($n = 2,618$) halts among the 29,791 target-resolved studies defined the 11,637-trial Deconfounded Scientific Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 565 explicit biological failures [328 futility and 237 safety] across 374 primary targets) and the 23,761-trial Definitive Phase II-to-III/IV Maturation Cohort (11,072 completed Phase III/IV successes versus 12,689 Phase II non-maturing or biologically failed trials across 561 primary targets).

### Primary human single-cell, pre-treatment bulk, and 10x Visium spatial transcriptomics processing

All single-cell, single-nucleus, pre-treatment bulk, and spatial transcriptomic datasets were processed from primary human clinical matrices retrieved from NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and the Human Protein Atlas without synthetic imputation, maintaining peak Python memory below 2.3 GB via exact single-UMI streaming (`umi_stride = 1`), compressed sparse matrices, and donor-level pseudobulk aggregation across 237 human clinical specimens:

1. **Healthy baseline reference**: Human Protein Atlas v23 single-cell transcriptomics consensus normalized expression across 154 human cell types [7].
2. **Hepatic MASH and cirrhosis**: `GSE136103` primary human healthy and cirrhotic liver plus paired circulating blood monocyte scRNA-seq ($n = 10$ liver donors and $4$ blood monocyte baselines) [9] combined with `GSE212837` primary human control and F2/F3 NASH liver snRNA-seq ($n = 7$ donors; $17$ liver biopsy donors and $19$ stage-resolved myeloid profiles in total) [14].
3. **Pulmonary fibrosis**: `GSE135893` primary human control, intermediate interstitial lung disease, and F3/F4 IPF lung scRNA-seq ($n = 30$ donors) [15].
4. **Renal fibrosis**: `GSE271188` primary human control, minimal-change baseline, and fibrotic CKD/DKD/IgAN/MN kidney snRNA-seq ($n = 17$ donors) [16].
5. **Solid oncology single-cell, pre-treatment bulk biopsy, and 10x Visium spatial cohorts**: `GSE123813` paired pre- and post-anti-PD-1 monotherapy human cutaneous carcinoma scRNA-seq ($n = 8$ strictly pre-treatment full-TME BCC patients and $n = 15$ paired longitudinal patients comprising 11 BCC and 4 cutaneous SCC, streamed at exact single-UMI fidelity with separate `CD45+CD3+` FACS-enriched T-cell sorting tubes deconvoluted from non-`CD3+` TME stromal cells) [42]; `GSE91061` strictly pre-nivolumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 49$, 10 responders) [44]; `GSE78220` strictly pre-pembrolizumab monotherapy melanoma bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 26$ unique patients, 14 responders) [45]; `GSE207422` strictly pre-treatment neoadjuvant anti-PD-1 plus platinum-doublet chemo-immunotherapy NSCLC bulk RNA-seq biopsies ($n = 24$, `Pre_biopsy`, 9 responders) and post-neoadjuvant resected NSCLC TME scRNA-seq ($n = 14$, 4 responders) [43]; and 12 primary human carcinoma 10x Visium spatial sections (`GSE171351` bladder carcinoma $n = 4$ [39] and `GSE203612` breast, ovarian, liver, and gastrointestinal carcinoma $n = 8$ [40]; 21,796 total spatial spots). Solid-oncology progression drift ($\Delta\tau$) for `DNAcc` was computed strictly from response-label-free early primary BCC tumors (`GSE123813`) versus advanced resected NSCLC tumors (`GSE207422`) without accessing clinical immunotherapy response labels.
6. **Autoimmune mucosal inflammation and intravascular sepsis**: `GSE250487` human ulcerative colitis mucosal biopsies ($n = 28$) [17] and `GSE167363` longitudinal human sepsis PBMCs ($n = 12$) [18].

### Dynamic specificity drift, empirical ECM barrier density, `DNAcc` ablation, and human genetics benchmark

For any target gene $g$ across $K$ cellular compartments with normalized mean log-expression $x_{g,k} \ge 0$, Yanai's specificity index [8] was computed in healthy baseline ($\tau_0$), early disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{early}}$), and advanced disease ($\tau_{\mathrm{late}}$) as:

$$\tau(g) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} \left(1 - \frac{x_{g,k}}{\max_{j} x_{g,j}}\right)}{K - 1}$$

Dynamic specificity drift was defined as $\Delta\tau(g) = \tau_{\mathrm{late}}(g) - \tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g)$, dynamic retention as $R_{\mathrm{dyn}}(g) = 0.5\,\tau_{\mathrm{early}}(g) + 0.5\,\mathrm{clip}(0.5 + \Delta\tau(g), 0, 1)$, and ectopic compartment switching $I_{\mathrm{switch}}(g) \in \{0, 1\}$ as a shift in dominant expressing lineage ($\arg\max_k x_{g,k}$) between early and advanced pathology (tabulated across all profiled targets as primary metric and among expressed targets with $\max_k x_{g,k} > 0.05$ in Supplementary Table S3). Vital-organ bystander hazard $H_{\mathrm{vital}}(g)$ quantified peak healthy expression across cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, renal tubular epithelium, and alveolar pneumocytes relative to target lineage expression. Axis-specific interstitial ECM barrier density $D_{\mathrm{ECM}}(\mathrm{axis}) \in [0.070, 0.350]$ was derived objectively from a 10-gene collagen-myofibroblast signature (`COL1A1`, `COL1A2`, `COL3A1`, `FN1`, `POSTN`, `ACTA2`, `FAP`, `LUM`, `DCN`, `THBS2`) measured across advanced human single-cell stromal compartments and Human Protein Atlas v23 organ cell types (`Supplementary Table S3m`), yielding modality-by-matrix barrier compatibility $B_{\mathrm{acc}}(m, d)$ across small molecules, peptides, and ~150-kDa IgG monoclonal antibodies.

In the six-step nested logistic ablation (`M1` through `M6`), out-of-fold trial probabilities were evaluated under both stratified 5-fold cross-validation and target-disjoint 5-fold `GroupKFold` grouped by `primary_target_gene` (`M1`: $[\tau_0]$; `M2`: $+ H_{\mathrm{vital}}$; `M3`: $+ \tau_{\mathrm{early}}$; `M4`: $+ \Delta\tau + R_{\mathrm{dyn}}$; `M5`: $+ I_{\mathrm{switch}} + S_{\mathrm{stromal}}$; `M6`: full `DNAcc` adding $B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ and the $\tau_{\mathrm{early}} \times B_{\mathrm{acc}}$ delivery-selectivity interaction). For the orthogonal human genetics benchmark [4, 5], Open Targets Platform v24.09 target-disease genetic association ($g_{\mathrm{assoc}}$) and somatic mutation ($g_{\mathrm{som}}$) evidence scores were mapped per trial-indication pair into the four-feature genetic vector $[g_{\mathrm{assoc}}, g_{\mathrm{som}}, g_{\max}, \mathbb{I}(g_{\max} \ge 0.10)]$ (`G1`) and compared head-to-head against static $\tau_0$ (`G2`), `Human Genetics + Static` $\tau_0$ (`G3`), `DNAcc` alone (`G4`), and `Joint Human Genetics + DNAcc` (`G5`) under identical Stratified 5-Fold and Unseen-Target `Group5Fold` splits (`Supplementary Table S3l`).

### Closure-free fibrosis coupling, Visium spatial proximity, and stochastic Langevin Waddington solver

Across the 64 human liver, lung, and kidney biopsy donors, compartment states (`Parenchyma_Injury`, `SAMac_SPP1_TREM2`, and `Myofibroblast`) were quantified from cell-level $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ transcriptional module activity rather than cell-count proportions that sum to unity, eliminating compositional closure artifacts; directed intercellular coupling weights were compiled from donor-level module activity Pearson correlations. Drug-compartment weights $w_c \in [0, 1]$ across all 24 SHA-256-locked Phase IIb/III antifibrotic trials were derived automatically from organ-specific single-cell pseudobulk expression (assigning active engagement where compartmental $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM}+1)$ reached $\ge 58\%$ of the target's peak compartment), scaled by $\tau_0$-weighted receptor-sink dilution $\min(1, (\min(\sqrt{n_t}, 1.85)/\max(\sum_c w_c, 1))(0.76 + 0.48\bar{\tau}_0))$ and unsigned ridge-GRN causal leverage at reference potency $\mathrm{pIC}_{50} = 7.5$. Extrahepatic or extrarenal metabolic and hemodynamic targets lacking local fibrotic-organ receptor expression (`GLP1R`, `GIPR`, and `SLC5A2`) were routed to upstream `Parenchyma_Injury` unloading. Steric accessibility followed $B_{\mathrm{acc}} = 1 - c_m D_{\mathrm{ECM}}$ ($c_m = 0.72$ for IgG monoclonal antibodies, $0.42$ for large proteins, and $0.07$ for small molecules or compact peptides), evaluated from stage-specific initial states (`F2_F3` $= [0.54, 0.43, 0.39]$, `F3_F4` $= [0.78, 0.82, 0.84]$, `F4_Cirrhosis` $= [0.86, 0.89, 0.91]$) as well as a stage-blind fixed cohort-centroid baseline (`OmniNiche_StageBlind_FixedS0`, $s_0 = [0.66, 0.61, 0.60]$).

For each 10x Visium section ($21,796$ spots across 12 carcinoma sections), standardized spot-level compartment scores $z_c(u)$ were linked via physical $k = 6$ Euclidean nearest-neighbor graphs, mapping symmetric bivariate Moran spatial lag $I_{ij}$ to proximity $P_{ij} = (1 + \exp(-4.5\,I_{ij}))^{-1}$ and Stromal Exclusion Index $\mathrm{SEI} = P_{\mathrm{myCAF,TAM}} - P_{\mathrm{Tumor,CD8}}$. Physical 500-µm scale bars equaled five times the median 100-µm Visium spot pitch, and continuous radial profiles tracked minimum distance from malignant core spots (top quartile of `Tumor` score) across 0 to 6 spot pitches (0 to 600 µm). In the five-compartment solid-tumor solver (`Tumor`, `CD8_Effector`, `TAM_ImmunoSupp`, `myCAF`, and `Treg_Exhaust`), joint `myCAF` and `TAM` capsule load $C_{\mathrm{cap}} = 0.76\,S_{\mathrm{myCAF}} + 0.24\,S_{\mathrm{TAM}}$ gated anti-PD-1 and $CD8^+$ T-cell access via $C_{\mathrm{cap}}^{4.2} / (0.62^{4.2} + C_{\mathrm{cap}}^{4.2})$ scaled by empirical Visium exclusion $\Delta P_{\mathrm{Visium}} = \max(0, P_{\mathrm{TAM,myCAF}} - P_{\mathrm{Tumor,CD8}})$, coupled with Holling Type-II effector-to-target tumor-burden saturation $(1 + 0.78(S_{\mathrm{Tumor}} / 0.55)^{2.9})^{-1}$. Multi-cohort pre-treatment pooling ($n = 83$ pure monotherapy and $n = 107$ total pre-treatment biopsies) used zero-label leave-one-patient-out (LOPO) robust quantile-rank scaling so scRNA-seq and bulk RNA-seq distributions were aligned without accessing any clinical response labels.

Multicellular state trajectories $\mathbf{S}(t) \in [0, 1]^N$ over compartments $j \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ were integrated over $T = 96.0\text{ h}$ with step $\Delta t = 0.5\text{ h}$ using fixed deterministic pseudorandom streams under the state-bounded stochastic Itô-Langevin differential equation [22]:

$$dS_j(t) = \left[ \alpha_j \rho_j(t) + (1 - S_j(t)) \rho_j(t) \left( \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_j} v_{ij} \frac{(\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}}{K_{ij}^{n_{ij}} + (\rho_i(t) S_i(t))^{n_{ij}}} \right) \prod_{q \in \mathcal{I}_j} \frac{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}}}{K_{qj}^{n_{qj}} + (\rho_q(t) S_q(t))^{n_{qj}}} - \gamma_j S_j(t) \right] dt + \sigma_j \sqrt{S_j(t)(1 - S_j(t))}\, dW_j(t)$$

where $\alpha_j$, $\gamma_j$, and $\sigma_j$ denote baseline production, decay rate, and demographic noise amplitude; $v_{ij}$, $K_{ij}$, and $n_{ij}$ denote directed coupling weight, half-maximal constant, and Hill cooperativity across activating ($\mathcal{A}_j$) and inhibitory ($\mathcal{I}_j$) inputs; and $\rho_j(t) = \prod_{r \in \mathcal{R}_j(t)} (1 - \mathrm{clip}(\eta_r B_{\mathrm{acc},r}, -0.95, 0.98))$ denotes unblocked signaling under regimen $\mathcal{R}_j$ with perturbation efficacy $\eta_r$. Smooth ensemble remission probability was evaluated across 220 to 250 Monte Carlo trajectories as $\mathbb{E}[(1 + \exp((S_{\mathrm{rem}}(T) - \theta_{\mathrm{rem}}) / \tau_{\mathrm{kernel}}))^{-1}]$ ($\theta_{\mathrm{rem}} = 0.36$, $\tau_{\mathrm{kernel}} = 0.10$ for fibrosis `Myofibroblast`; $\theta_{\mathrm{rem}} = 0.45$, $\tau_{\mathrm{kernel}} = 0.11$ for solid-tumor `Tumor`). Two-dimensional Waddington quasi-potential surfaces $U(x, y) = -\ln P_{\mathrm{ss}}(x, y)$ were estimated on a Gaussian-smoothed ($\sigma = 2.2$ bins) $60 \times 60$ stationary density grid [22], and 25-point bilateral profile-likelihood curves scanned each edge weight $v_{ij} \in [0.50, 1.50] \times v_{ij,\mathrm{MLE}}$ while re-optimizing nuisance rate scale $k_{\mathrm{scale}} \in [0.82, 1.22]$, declaring structural identifiability at $\Delta\chi^2 > 3.841$ [23].

### Statistical analysis

Correlated ROC curves on shared cohorts were compared using two-sided paired nonparametric DeLong tests [19]. Confidence intervals (95%) for AUROC, AUPRC, and Brier scores were computed via 2,000 stratified bootstrap resamples. For clinical trial and biopsy validation cohorts (24 and Core-14 antifibrotic Phase IIb/III trials and $n = 8$ to $107$ solid-tumor immunotherapy cohorts), exact empirical *P*-values were computed from 10,000 Monte Carlo label permutations using vectorized Mann-Whitney rank statistics. Multivariable confounder control used L2-regularized ($C = 50.0$) fixed-effects logistic regression reporting both analytic inverse-Hessian Wald standard errors and Liang-Zeger target-gene cluster-robust sandwich standard errors ($\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{G}{G - 1}\mathbf{H}^{-1}(\sum_{g=1}^{G}\mathbf{u}_g\mathbf{u}_g^{\top})\mathbf{H}^{-1}$ across $G = 374$ and $G = 561$ unique `primary_target_gene` clusters), adjusting simultaneously for out-of-fold Open Targets human genetic support, static $\tau_0$, disease axis (11 indicator variables), drug modality, calendar start era (`Pre_2010`, `2010_2014`, `2015_2019`, `2020_Plus`), sponsor class (`INDUSTRY` versus `NON_INDUSTRY`), and polypharmacology ($n_{\mathrm{targets}} \ge 2$ versus single-target). Seven sensitivity regimes (`R1`-`R7`) audited robustness across naive keywords, worst-case failure assignment of all 2,618 target-resolved unreported stops, inverse-probability weighting, posted-results-only trials, ChEMBL/Open Targets regulatory approval gold standards, and the strict nine-axis solid-parenchymal boundary. Categorical $2 \times 2$ associations were tested by two-sided Fisher's exact test, paired spatial and trajectory rescue comparisons by Wilcoxon signed-rank test, and clinical utility across threshold probabilities $p_t$ by Decision Curve Analysis net benefit $\mathrm{NB}(p_t) = \mathrm{TP}/N - (\mathrm{FP}/N)[p_t / (1 - p_t)]$ [20]. An automated 42-test `pytest` verification suite validated registry isolation, clause-aware stoppage deconvolution, human-genetics orthogonality, closure-free fibrosis coupling, DeLong/permutation/sandwich inference, profile likelihood, radial Visium decay, and multi-cohort reproducibility.
